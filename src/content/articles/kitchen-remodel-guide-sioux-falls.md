A kitchen remodel is one of the most disruptive projects you can do to a home, and also one of the most rewarding when it goes well. In Sioux Falls, there are local details that change how you plan: city permit rules, water hardness from the Big Sioux Aquifer, and clay soils that can shift older foundations and plumbing lines. This guide walks you through every stage, from figuring out your scope to handing a key to your contractor, with links to the deeper articles where you need them most.

## Decide What You Are Actually Remodeling

Before you call anyone, get specific about what "remodel" means for your kitchen.

There is a big difference between a cosmetic refresh (new cabinet fronts, hardware, paint, countertops) and a full gut that moves walls, plumbing, or electrical panels. The first might be done in a week or two. The second can run months.

Ask yourself:

- Do you want to change the layout, or does the current one work?
- Are the cabinet boxes solid, or are they soft, warped, or falling apart?
- Is the plumbing and electrical already where you need it?
- Are you fixing problems (bad ventilation, inadequate storage, failing surfaces), or upgrading for enjoyment or resale?

Being specific here saves you money. Sioux Falls homeowners have found that a designer who plans every inch of the kitchen from the start catches conflicts early, before cabinets are ordered or walls are opened.

## Understand Where Your Budget Goes

A kitchen remodel budget is not one number. It is ten numbers, some of which you will not know until the walls are open.

The largest shares typically go to cabinets and countertops, followed by labor. After that come appliances, flooring, lighting, and plumbing fixtures. The costs that surprise people most are the ones hiding behind walls: outdated wiring, corroded drain lines, water damage under old flooring.

A realistic contingency is not optional. Most remodeling professionals recommend setting aside a reserve for unknowns. What that percentage should be depends on the age of your home and how much of the work involves opening walls.

Sioux Falls homeowners consistently say the most valuable thing a contractor gave them was a detailed, itemized estimate that stayed on target, not a rough ballpark. Push for line items, not a single project number.

## Rethink the Layout Before Anything Is Ordered

If you are moving walls or changing where appliances land, layout decisions have to come before cabinet measurements, before countertop quotes, before anything.

A layout that works day to day is not just about aesthetics. It is about work zones: where you prep, where you cook, where you clean, and how those three areas connect without people colliding.

Common mistakes include:

- Islands that block the path to the refrigerator or stove
- Not enough counter space on either side of the range
- Drawer and door swing conflicts that you will never notice on a blueprint but will hit every single day
- Insufficient storage near the actual point of use (cutting boards stored across the room from the cutting area)

A peninsula or island can add prep space and seating, but it also narrows aisles. Think about how many people are typically in your kitchen at once.

## Cabinets: Refinish, Reface, or Replace?

Cabinets are usually the biggest line on a kitchen remodel budget, and they do not always need to be replaced completely.

If your cabinet boxes are structurally sound, replacing just the doors, drawer fronts, and hardware can transform the look of a kitchen for significantly less than a full replacement. This is called refacing, and Sioux Falls homeowners have used it successfully when the layout is staying the same and the boxes are in good shape.

Refinishing (painting or staining the existing surfaces) goes even further in cost savings, but works best on solid wood and when the finish is the only issue.

Full replacement makes the most sense when:

- The layout is changing
- The boxes are soft, warped, or water-damaged
- You want custom sizing to fit an awkward wall or ceiling height
- You are replacing everything anyway and want a cohesive result

Cabinet lead times in Sioux Falls are a real planning issue. Semi-custom and custom cabinets can take many weeks to arrive. If they show up damaged (which happens), reorder time can push your whole project. ## Countertops, Flooring, and Finishes

Countertops come in materials with very different trade-offs: some handle heat, some do not; some need sealing, some do not; some show seams more than others. The right choice depends on how you actually use your kitchen, not just what looks good in a showroom.

One Sioux Falls-specific consideration: according to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report, total hardness is about 15 grains per gallon for city-treated water and about 10 grains per gallon for Lewis and Clark water. That is very hard water, and it affects faucets, dishwashers, and certain stone finishes over time. Read [hard water and your new kitchen](/guides/hard-water-kitchen) before you finalize a countertop or faucet.

Flooring is a separate decision with a sequencing question you need to settle early: do you install flooring under the cabinets or around them? The answer affects cost, future flexibility, and how the finished room looks.

## Lighting and Ventilation

Lighting is one of the most underplanned parts of a kitchen remodel and one of the cheapest to do right if you plan it before the walls close.

Good kitchen lighting has three layers:

- Ambient (general overhead light for the whole room)
- Task lighting (direct light on the counter where you prep and the stove where you cook)
- Under-cabinet lighting (eliminates the shadow your own body throws when you are standing at the counter)

Recessed lights and new fixture circuits require an electrical permit in Sioux Falls, and that work must be done by a licensed electrician unless you are a homeowner doing your own work in a home you own and occupy (more on permits below). Get the lighting plan locked in before drywall goes up.

Ventilation is just as important and often worse planned. A range hood that vents outside removes grease, moisture, and cooking odors. One that recirculates does not. The duct route matters, and in some kitchens it requires a permit. For the details, read [range hoods and kitchen ventilation](/guides/kitchen-ventilation), and for the full lighting picture, see [kitchen lighting that actually helps](/guides/kitchen-lighting).

## Permits: What Sioux Falls Actually Requires

This is the part homeowners most often get wrong, and it can cause real problems at resale or during an insurance claim.

According to the City of Sioux Falls (April 2025 home improvement reminders), cabinets, countertops, flooring, and painting do not require a permit. Projects involving electrical, mechanical, or plumbing systems do.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs:

- Building permits cover additions and remodels that touch structure
- Electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work requires separate permits
- That work must be done by licensed contractors, unless you own and live in a single-family home and pull a homeowner permit to do it yourself

What this means for a kitchen remodel: if you are adding a circuit for a new microwave, moving the sink drain, or adding a gas line for a range, permits are required. If you are only replacing cabinets and countertops, they are not.

Skipping a required permit is not just a fine risk. It can create problems when you sell. For everything you need to know, read [kitchen remodel permits in Sioux Falls](/guides/kitchen-permits-sioux-falls).

## Timeline and Living Without a Kitchen

Sioux Falls homeowners consistently mention one surprise: living without a functioning kitchen for longer than they expected.

A cosmetic refresh might mean two weeks of inconvenience. A full remodel with moved plumbing or electrical can mean four to eight weeks or more without a stove, sink, or dishwasher. Cabinet lead times, permit review schedules, and the sequencing of trades all affect how long the in-between period lasts.

Planning a temporary kitchen setup before demo day makes a real difference. Think about:

- Where you will set up a microwave and coffee maker
- How you will handle dishes without a kitchen sink
- Where to store pantry items so they are accessible but out of the way
- How to protect the rest of the house from drywall dust

## Choosing and Comparing Remodelers

The most common thing Sioux Falls homeowners say they wish they had done: asked more questions before signing, and seen work similar to theirs before committing.

When you are comparing bids, the number is not the only thing to evaluate. A lower bid that uses allowances instead of real line items can end up far more expensive. A higher bid that is fully itemized tells you much more about what you are actually getting.

Things to ask every contractor:

- Are you licensed in South Dakota for the trades your quote includes?
- Will you pull the required permits, or is that my responsibility?
- Who are your subcontractors, and have you worked with them before?
- What does your payment schedule look like, and what triggers each payment?
- How do you handle change orders, and what is your process for communicating with me during the project?
- Can I see a completed project similar in scope to mine?
