If you're heading south for the winter, your Sioux Falls home faces real risks in your absence that a good home watch service is specifically designed to catch early.

## The core winter risks for a vacant home

- **Frozen and burst pipes**, especially if heating fails or is set too low, which can cause extensive water damage before anyone notices.
- **Snow load on the roof**, which can cause structural stress or ice dam formation if not monitored.
- **Heating system failure.** A furnace that stops working in a vacant home in January can lead to frozen pipes within days, not weeks.

## What a real winter home watch visit should cover

- Confirming the heating system is actually running and holding the set temperature, not just assuming it's fine.
- Checking for any visible signs of water intrusion, ice dam formation, or roof stress.
- Confirming mail and packages aren't accumulating in a way that signals an empty home to passersby.
- Verifying exterior conditions — walkways, driveway, any exterior faucets — are properly winterized.

## Thermostat settings while away

Many home watch professionals recommend keeping your thermostat no lower than 55-60°F during winter absences — low enough to control heating costs over a long absence, high enough to keep interior temperatures well above freezing throughout the home, including less-insulated areas like basements or additions.

## Why visit frequency should increase in winter

A problem that might wait safely for a monthly check in July can cause serious damage within days in January. This is the main reason many home watch companies recommend weekly visits during the coldest months specifically for seasonal-absence homeowners, even if less frequent checks are sufficient the rest of the year.
