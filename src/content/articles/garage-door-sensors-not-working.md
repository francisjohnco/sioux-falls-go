If your garage door won't close and the opener light is blinking, the safety sensors are almost certainly the reason. The good news is that most sensor problems come down to a handful of simple, fixable causes that any homeowner can check in about ten minutes. Before you call a technician, work through this list. For a broader look at the full system, see [Garage Door Repair in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/garage-door-repair-sioux-falls).

## Why the Auto Reverse Safety System Matters

The two small sensors mounted near the floor on each side of your garage door are not optional equipment. They are a federal safety requirement, and they have been since 1993.

One sensor sends an invisible infrared beam across the door opening. The other receives it. When something breaks that beam, the door stops and reverses. That thing could be a child, a dog, or a bicycle wheel.

If the sensors are misaligned, dirty, or damaged and your door still closes anyway, that is a serious problem. The auto reverse system exists to prevent injury and death. A door that closes regardless of what is in its path is not a convenience, it is a hazard.

This is also why the blinking light on your opener is a feature, not a glitch. It is telling you the safety circuit is open and the door will not close until the problem is resolved.

## Check Alignment First

This is the most common cause of a garage door sensor not working. The sensors are mounted on small brackets that bend easily, especially if someone kicked one, bumped it with a trash can, or if a lawn tool leaned against it.

Look at both sensors. Each one will have a small indicator light.

- The sending sensor (usually amber or yellow) should glow steadily.
- The receiving sensor (usually green) should also glow steadily when the beam is aligned.
- A blinking or unlit green sensor almost always means misalignment.

To fix it, loosen the wing nut or bracket screw on the receiving sensor just enough so it can move. Slowly angle it until the green light goes solid, then retighten. Do not overtighten, or you will knock it out of alignment again.

Sioux Falls freeze-thaw cycles and strong prairie wind can vibrate brackets loose over a winter. It is worth checking sensor alignment every spring as part of a basic once-over.

## Clean the Lenses

Even perfectly aligned sensors will fail if the lenses are coated with grime. In a Sioux Falls garage that sees snow, mud, and road salt all winter, sensor lenses get dirty faster than most homeowners expect.

Wipe each lens with a soft, dry cloth or a cotton swab. Do not use glass cleaner with ammonia directly on the plastic, it can cloud the lens over time. A slightly damp cloth followed by a dry one is enough.

After cleaning, check whether the indicator lights go solid. If they do, you found your problem. If they still blink, move to the next check.

## Rule Out Sunlight Glare

This one surprises people. Direct sunlight hitting a sensor's receiver can blind it, exactly the way shining a flashlight into someone's eyes would. The sensor reads the solar glare as a blocked beam and refuses to let the door close.

It tends to happen at a specific time of day, usually late afternoon in spring and fall when the sun is low and shining straight into the garage. If your door closes fine in the morning but not in the afternoon, sunlight is the likely cause.

The fix does not require new hardware. A small cardboard tube or a piece of black electrical conduit slipped over the front of the receiving sensor acts as a sun shade. You can also buy inexpensive sensor visors made for this purpose. Some homeowners simply add a short extension hood cut from a cardboard tube and secured with tape as a temporary test.

## Inspect the Wiring

If alignment and cleaning did not solve it, look at the wires running from each sensor up along the door track to the opener motor head. This step is visual only, no electrical knowledge required.

Look for:

- Wires that have been pinched by the door track bracket
- Bare copper showing through cracked insulation
- A wire that has pulled loose from the sensor terminal
- Corrosion at the terminal connection point (white or greenish buildup)

Sioux Falls winters bring long stretches of extreme cold. According to SiouxFalls.Business, a National Weather Service meteorologist described the February 2021 cold snap as the longest of its kind since about 1996. Cold makes wire insulation brittle and can cause small cracks that only show up under good light.

If a wire has simply pulled loose from the terminal, you can gently reinsert and tighten the small screw. If the insulation is cracked or the wire is pinched, stop there and call a technician. Working inside the opener motor head or handling wiring beyond the sensor terminals is not a DIY task.

## When to Call a Pro, and What to Ask

If you have checked alignment, cleaned the lenses, ruled out sunlight, and confirmed the wiring looks intact, and the sensors still will not work, the sensor units themselves may need replacement. Sensors are not expensive parts nationally, and a technician can replace them in a short visit, though you should ask for a written quote before anyone starts work.

A few questions worth asking before you book:

- Do you carry sensor parts on the truck, or will this be a second trip?
- Is there an after-hours or weekend fee if I book outside standard weekday hours? Several Sioux Falls garage door companies offer evening and weekend service, but rates vary.
- Which city license does your company hold?

On that last question: the City of Sioux Falls licenses residential contractors, and since 2024 has also offered a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license for smaller jobs including door replacements on homes that need no structural changes, according to the City of Sioux Falls (February 2024 and April 2026). The city's announcement does not specifically name garage doors, so ask the company which license it holds. You can verify any contractor at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors or by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672.

Sensor replacement alone does not require a building permit. Widening a garage door opening or modifying the header is structural and would require one. If you are unsure about your situation, the City of Sioux Falls Planning and Development Services can answer that at 605-367-8670.
