Winter in Sioux Falls is not gentle on outdoor structures. Freeze-thaw cycles, heavy snow loads, and months of moisture sitting on wood or composite surfaces can quietly do real damage to a deck before you notice anything obvious in spring.

This guide focuses entirely on winter care, one season at a time. For the full picture of planning, building, and permitting, start with [Building a Deck in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/deck-guide-sioux-falls).

The good news: a few simple habits through the cold months can add years to a deck's life and keep your spring inspection short.

## Deck Snow Removal Done Safely

Letting snow pile up on a deck is tempting, especially when it's cold enough that nothing seems to be melting anyway. But weight accumulates fast, and wet, dense snow is far heavier than the light fluffy stuff.

A plastic shovel is the right tool. Metal shovels scrape and gouge wood surfaces, and they can chip composite boards too. Push snow off the sides rather than lifting when you can, and work with the boards, not across them, to avoid catching edges.

You do not need to clear every flake. Getting it down to an inch or so is usually enough to relieve the load and let the deck breathe. Scraping down to bare wood in freezing temperatures often does more harm than good.

A few things to keep in mind:

- Never use an ice chipper or sharp tool on a deck surface
- Avoid prying at ice that has bonded to the boards, you can lift the finish or crack composite caps
- Clear around posts and stair stringers too, not just the main deck field
- If snow is drifting against the house wall where the ledger is, clear that area first

## Ice Melt on a Deck: What Actually Works

Ice melt is where a lot of homeowners accidentally damage their decks. The wrong product can bleach composite boards, corrode fasteners, or strip a wood stain faster than a full season of sun.

Calcium chloride and sodium chloride (rock salt) are the most common products on store shelves. Rock salt is hard on wood finishes and is corrosive to the metal hardware underneath your decking. Calcium chloride works at lower temperatures but can discolor some composite colors over time.

The safest choice for most decks, wood or composite, is a product labeled "deck-safe" or one based on magnesium chloride or potassium chloride. Read the label before you buy, and check your composite manufacturer's care guide if you still have it.

Some practical rules for ice melt on a deck:

- Use the minimum amount that actually prevents slipping, more product does not mean faster melting
- Rinse the surface as soon as temperatures allow, do not let residue sit through multiple freeze-thaw cycles
- Never use sand as a substitute for traction on composite, it acts like sandpaper
- Keep ice melt away from planters and any area where it will drain toward plants or lawn

If your deck boards already have a stain or sealer on them, check [Staining and Sealing a Wood Deck](/guides/deck-stain-seal) in the spring, because heavy ice melt use through the winter is often what tells you it is time to refinish.

## Storing Deck Furniture and Accessories

Leaving furniture on the deck all winter is one of those things that seems fine until you see the damage in April. Furniture legs trap moisture against deck boards, block drainage, and can leave staining or surface impressions on wood after months of freeze-thaw pressure.

Move furniture to a garage, basement, or covered storage area before the first hard freeze. If that is not possible, at minimum:

- Stack chairs and stack them on a pallet or small risers to allow airflow underneath
- Flip table tops or store them vertically
- Remove cushions entirely, they hold moisture and mold through winter even under covers
- Take down string lights and any fabric elements, UV and moisture together cause fast deterioration

Furniture covers can help if storage is not an option, but covers trap condensation and do not fully protect the deck surface underneath. They are better than nothing, not a substitute for proper storage.

Gas grills and fire pits should also be moved or at least covered and disconnected. A propane tank sitting on a deck through a Sioux Falls winter is not a safety hazard by itself, but the drip pan and grease tray will cause staining that is very hard to remove from wood.

## The Spring Check You Want to Do First

Winter care earns its value in spring. When the last significant snow is gone and temperatures are staying above freezing most days, do a walk-around before you put furniture back or start using the deck.

This does not need to be an expert inspection, just a careful look at a few specific spots:

- **Boards**: look for cupping, cracking, or any board that has shifted or lifted
- **Stairs**: check for movement or wobble at the stringers and where they meet the ground
- **Posts**: look at the base of each post where it meets the footing or bracket, that is where rot starts
- **Ledger area**: look for staining, soft spots, or gaps between the deck frame and the house wall
- **Railings**: grab and push each section, it should feel solid with no flex

If anything looks soft, discolored in a way that suggests rot, or structurally loose, [Is Your Old Deck Safe? A Spring Checklist](/guides/old-deck-safety-check) walks through each component in detail and helps you decide what needs a professional eye.

Decks built before current practices were common, especially those 15 to 20 years old, often show their worst damage after a hard winter. Homeowners who have mentioned wanting a deck "checked over" before another season are asking exactly the right question at exactly the right time of year.

If a post has shifted or the deck surface is visibly uneven, that may be a footing issue. Sioux Falls soil and frost conditions can move footings that were not set to the depth required by City Building Services. [Deck Footings and Frost Heave](/guides/deck-footings-frost) explains what to look for and when it becomes a repair job.

Any structural repair that goes beyond surface work may require a building permit, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs. If you hire a contractor for that work, the City requires a Residential Building Contractor's License for work on one and two family homes within city limits, and you can verify any contractor's license at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors before anyone starts work.
