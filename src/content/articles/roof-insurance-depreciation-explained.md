Getting a check from your insurance company after a Sioux Falls hailstorm or wind event feels like relief, until you look at the number. Many homeowners open that envelope and immediately think something went wrong. Usually, nothing did. Insurance payments for roof replacement almost always come in stages, and the first check is rarely the full amount. For the full picture of how repairs and replacement work here, start with [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls).

Understanding why the number is lower than your contractor's bid can save you weeks of frustration and help you get every dollar your policy actually allows.

## Why the First Check Is Not the Full Amount

Insurance companies almost never pay the total replacement cost in one shot. There are two things that reduce that first check: your deductible and something called depreciation.

Your deductible comes off the top, every time. If your policy carries a $2,000 deductible and the adjuster's estimate is $12,000, your first check reflects that $2,000 reduction before anything else.

Depreciation is the part that surprises most people. The insurer figures out what your roof was worth at the time of the storm, not what it costs to replace it today. A 15-year-old roof has lost some of its value in the insurer's formula. The difference between replacement cost and actual cash value is depreciation, and on an older roof it can be several thousand dollars.

That gap is not necessarily gone. It depends entirely on your policy.

## RCV vs. ACV: The Policy Difference That Changes Everything

There are two basic types of roof coverage. Your policy is one or the other, and it matters enormously.

**Replacement Cost Value (RCV)** policies agree to pay the full cost of replacing the damaged roof with materials of like kind and quality. They release the payment in two stages: actual cash value up front, then the remaining depreciation after work is complete and you submit proof.

**Actual Cash Value (ACV)** policies pay only the depreciated value. You receive one check, and the gap between that and the real replacement cost comes out of your pocket. These policies are less expensive but can leave you seriously short on a full replacement.

Check the declarations page of your policy. Look for the words "replacement cost" or "actual cash value" next to your dwelling coverage. If you are unsure what you have, call your agent and ask directly: "Is my roof covered on a replacement cost basis, and is depreciation recoverable?"

The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to read their policy carefully before filing and to ask the insurer for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer. That is free to request and your right as a policyholder. Their number is 605-773-3563.

## Recoverable Depreciation: How You Collect the Rest

If you have an RCV policy, the held-back depreciation is called recoverable depreciation on your roof claim. It is yours to collect once the work is done. The process usually looks like this:

- Your contractor completes the work.
- You submit the final signed invoice and any completion documentation the insurer requires.
- The insurer releases the withheld depreciation, sometimes called the "recoverable depreciation" or "holdback" amount.
- You pay your contractor in full.

Some insurers release that final payment directly to you. Others may list your mortgage company on the check as well, which can add a step at your bank or lender's office. Ask your insurer upfront: "Who will the depreciation check be made out to, and what do you need from me to release it?"

Timing matters. Most policies set a deadline, often 180 days to two years, for you to complete repairs and file for the recoverable depreciation. Miss that window and you may forfeit it. Check your policy or ask your agent what deadline applies to your claim.

## Why Some Roofers Want to Wait for the Final Check

Sioux Falls homeowners sometimes find that a roofer wants to wait until the insurance process plays out before scheduling the job. That is not always a red flag. On RCV claims, the full payment is not released until after work is complete, which creates a gap.

Some contractors are willing to begin work and wait for the final insurance check to close out the balance. Others require a larger portion upfront. Neither approach is automatically wrong, but the arrangement should be in writing before work starts.

What you should never do is pay a contractor in full before the job is finished. The South Dakota Attorney General's office specifically warns homeowners not to pay in full before work is done, particularly after hailstorms when door-to-door contractors are common. If a contractor demands full payment upfront, that is a serious warning sign.

## What to Document and Keep

Insurance companies release that second check based on proof. That means paperwork matters from day one.

Keep copies of:

- The adjuster's written estimate, every page
- Your policy declarations page
- Every email, letter, or notes from phone calls (the SD Division of Insurance recommends keeping a contact log)
- Your contractor's signed, itemized invoice
- Any supplements your contractor submits for items the adjuster missed
- Permit documentation from the City of Sioux Falls Building Services

The permit piece is worth noting: shingle replacement in Sioux Falls requires a building permit, and the contractor must hold a city Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license, which requires a $20,000 compliance bond and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance. If your contractor skips the permit, your insurance company can use that against you later. You can confirm a contractor's license with Building Services at 605-367-8670.

Photograph the finished roof before the crew leaves. That documentation supports your claim for the final depreciation release and also protects you if a question comes up later.

## What to Ask Your Insurer Directly

If your check feels short and you are not sure why, here are the questions to ask your insurance company before assuming anything went wrong:

- Is my roof covered on a replacement cost or actual cash value basis?
- How much depreciation was withheld, and is it recoverable?
- What documentation do I need to submit to release the withheld depreciation?
- What is the deadline for completing repairs and filing for the depreciation release?
- Was any portion of the damage excluded, and if so, why?
- Can I have an itemized explanation of the settlement amount?

That last question is your right under South Dakota insurance regulations. If the insurer's estimate is lower than your contractor's, the difference often involves items the adjuster missed, called supplements. Your contractor can submit a supplement with documentation, and the insurer must review it.

If you feel you are getting the run-around, the SD Division of Insurance handles consumer complaints and can tell you whether the insurer's handling of your claim meets state requirements. Their line is 605-773-3563.
