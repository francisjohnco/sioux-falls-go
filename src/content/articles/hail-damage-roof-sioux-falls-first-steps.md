A hailstorm rolls through Sioux Falls and by morning your neighbors are already getting door knocks from out-of-state contractors. The pressure starts fast, and so does the clock on your insurance claim.

Knowing what to do in the first 48 hours protects both your home and your money. For the full picture on repairs, costs, and picking a contractor, start with [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls). This article is specifically about those critical first two days.

## Step 1: Stay Off the Roof and Stay Safe

Your first instinct might be to climb up and look. Do not.

Wet shingles are slippery, damaged decking can give way without warning, and you will not see hail bruising accurately from two feet away anyway. The ground is where you do your first assessment.

Walk the perimeter and look for:

- Dented or cracked gutters and downspouts
- Dents in aluminum vent covers, AC units, or metal flashing
- Granules washed into gutters or pooling at downspout exits
- Cracked or broken window screens and siding dents
- Damage to your car (useful as a reference for the size and density of hail)

Hail damage on a car almost always means hail damage on the roof. Photograph the car before you move it, because adjusters use it as a reference point.

## Step 2: Photograph Everything Before You Touch Anything

Take photos and video before any cleanup or temporary repairs. This is the evidence your insurer needs and the record that protects you if there is a dispute later.

Shoot from every corner of the yard, zoom in on gutters, vents, and siding, and get close-up shots of granule buildup at downspouts. If a neighbor's tree came down or debris is sitting on the roof, photograph it in place.

The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to photograph damage as one of the first steps after a storm, and to keep a log of every contact with their insurer. That log matters more than most people expect, so start it now. Date, time, who you spoke with, what was said.

## Step 3: Make Temporary Repairs and Keep Every Receipt

If water is actively getting in, you need to stop it before you can file and wait for an adjuster.

The South Dakota Division of Insurance confirms that you should make temporary repairs to prevent further damage and keep all receipts. Those costs are typically reimbursable as part of your claim. Do not let a leaking flashing or a hole from a fallen branch sit for three days while you wait to hear back.

Acceptable temporary repairs include:

- Tarping a damaged section (get heavy-duty poly tarps, not the thin ones)
- Covering a cracked or missing skylight
- Boarding over any opening where decking is exposed

Do not let anyone make permanent repairs before your insurer has seen the damage. That is a phrase that appears directly in Division of Insurance guidance, and violating it can complicate or reduce your claim.

If you are not sure whether something counts as a "permanent repair," ask your insurer before you do it.

## Step 4: Read Your Policy and File Promptly

Pull out your homeowner's policy or log into your insurer's app and look for two things first: your deductible and your notice requirements.

Policies in South Dakota can set deadlines for reporting a claim. The Division of Insurance specifically tells homeowners to file promptly because waiting too long can create problems even when the damage is real. If you are unsure whether a storm date qualifies, file anyway and let the adjuster determine scope.

Things to find in your policy before you call:

- Your wind and hail deductible (sometimes separate from your general deductible)
- Whether you have actual cash value or replacement cost value coverage
- Any exclusions for roof age or condition

If your insurer does not respond to your claim communications within 30 days, that is a violation of South Dakota rules. The Division of Insurance can be reached at 605-773-3563. Keep that number.

For a detailed walk through the full claims process, including what to do if the payment comes up short, see [How a Roof Insurance Claim Works in South Dakota](/guides/roof-insurance-claim-south-dakota).

## Step 5: Do Not Sign Anything at the Door

After a significant hail event, contractors start canvassing Sioux Falls neighborhoods within hours. Some are legitimate locals. Some are not.

The South Dakota Attorney General's consumer protection office specifically warns homeowners after hailstorms: do not be pressured into a quick agreement, ask for proof of insurance or bond, get a written contract, be cautious of cash-only terms, and do not pay in full before the job is done.

A few things to know about your rights:

- Door-to-door sales contracts in South Dakota carry a three-day right to cancel.
- If you cancel within three days, a refund is due within 10 days.
- You can call the Attorney General's Consumer Protection line at 1-800-300-1986 if you feel pressured or think you have been scammed.

One specific thing to watch for: a contractor who asks you to sign an "assignment of benefits" or an "insurance authorization" at the door before any inspection has happened. That is a red flag. You are not required to hand over control of your claim to get an estimate.

For the full list of storm chaser warning signs and how to check a contractor's credentials on the spot, read [Storm Chasers After Sioux Falls Hail, How to Spot Them](/guides/storm-chasers-sioux-falls-roofing-scams).

## Step 6: Get a Legitimate Inspection From a Licensed Contractor

Once you have filed your claim, you want your own qualified roofer present when the adjuster comes, or at minimum an independent inspection before you accept a settlement.

In Sioux Falls, any contractor doing shingle work on a home must hold a city Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license (or the broader Residential Building Contractor license), according to the City of Sioux Falls. That license requires a $20,000 compliance bond and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance. You can verify a contractor's license by calling Building Services at 605-367-8670.

Ask any contractor who knocks on your door:

- Do you hold a City of Sioux Falls Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license?
- Can I see your certificate of general liability insurance?
- Do you have a current South Dakota contractor's excise tax license?
- Do you have a local address and local references I can call?

A legitimate contractor will answer all of those questions without hesitation and without pressuring you to sign anything first. If they deflect or push you to commit before answering, that is your answer.

The July 25, 2026 storm system brought 72 mph gusts at the Sioux Falls airport and 77 mph near Harrisburg, according to the National Weather Service Sioux Falls. Events like that drive a surge of out-of-area contractors into the metro within 48 hours. The two-day window is exactly when the pressure is highest and when homeowners are most vulnerable to making a rushed decision they will regret.

Take the time. Your claim and your roof will both be better for it.
