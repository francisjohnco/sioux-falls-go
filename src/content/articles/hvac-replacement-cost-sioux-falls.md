Replacing a furnace or central AC is one of the bigger purchases a homeowner makes, and the final number on the invoice can surprise you if you don't know what's inside it. Prices vary widely depending on equipment, your home's existing ductwork and electrical setup, and even the time of year you schedule the work. The [Heating and Cooling in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/hvac-guide-sioux-falls) covers the full picture of how these systems work together. This article focuses on the cost side: what drives the price, what to look for in a bid, and what questions to ask before you sign anything.

## Why Furnace Replacement Cost in Sioux Falls Isn't One Number

There is no single Sioux Falls average for furnace or AC replacement that means much to your situation. Your home's square footage, duct condition, existing venting, gas line size, and electrical panel all affect what a contractor needs to do, and that shapes the final cost as much as the equipment itself does.

Nationally, furnace replacement typically falls somewhere in a wide range, and the same is true for central AC. But those ranges mean little without a written quote that breaks out every line item. Ask for equipment, labor, permits, and any materials as separate line items, not a single lump sum.

What you can control is understanding what moves the needle and asking the right questions when bids come in.

## Equipment Type and Efficiency

The equipment itself is usually the largest cost driver. Here are the main variables:

- **Furnace fuel type.** Most Sioux Falls homes run natural gas furnaces, which are well-suited to the area's cold winters and readily available fuel. Electric furnaces cost less upfront but typically cost more to operate here.
- **AFUE rating.** This is the efficiency percentage. An 80 AFUE furnace converts 80 cents of every dollar of gas into heat. A 96 or 97 AFUE condensing furnace does better but requires PVC venting rather than a metal flue, which adds installation work.
- **AC efficiency (SEER2).** Higher efficiency units carry a higher equipment price. Whether the savings over time justify the upfront difference depends on how long you stay in the home and how often you run the system.
- **Heat pumps.** A cold-climate heat pump can heat and cool, but the upfront cost is higher and your electrical panel may need an upgrade. See [Do Heat Pumps Work in Sioux Falls Winters?](/guides/heat-pump-sioux-falls-winters) for the full breakdown.

Don't just compare unit prices. A less efficient unit may cost less today and more every month for the next 15 years.

## Sizing, Ductwork, and What Your Home Actually Needs

A proper installation starts with a load calculation, sometimes called a Manual J. This determines the right size unit for your home's square footage, insulation, window area, and layout. Oversizing or undersizing a furnace or AC unit causes comfort problems and shortens equipment life.

If a contractor sizes your new system by looking at the old unit's model number and matching it, that's a shortcut worth questioning. The old unit may have always been the wrong size.

Ductwork is a major wildcard in cost. A home with good, sealed ducts in decent condition needs much less work than one with leaky or undersized runs. Sioux Falls homes in older neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints sometimes have duct systems that were designed for smaller, less efficient equipment, and updates add to the project cost.

If a contractor recommends duct repairs or replacement, ask for specifics: which runs, what the problem is, and whether they'll show you photos before and after. What to Expect](/guides/duct-cleaning-worth-it) covers what a legitimate job looks like.

## Venting, Electrical, and Permits

These are the line items that can quietly add hundreds of dollars and sometimes much more.

**Venting.** Switching from an 80 AFUE furnace to a high-efficiency condensing unit means switching from a metal flue to PVC pipes that typically exit through a side wall. That's new material and labor. If you're keeping the same efficiency tier, you may be able to reuse existing venting, but a good contractor will inspect it rather than assume.

**Electrical.** Central AC requires a dedicated circuit. If your panel doesn't have capacity, adding a circuit or upgrading the panel is a real cost. This is especially relevant for older homes or if you're adding a heat pump, which draws more power than a standard AC unit. Homeowners who have tried to add electric HVAC equipment have run into panel capacity limits, so ask about this upfront.

**Permits.** The City of Sioux Falls requires a separate mechanical permit for heating and cooling work, and a separate electrical permit if electrical work is part of the job. These are not covered by a general building permit. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, the city licenses mechanical and electrical contractors, and you can verify a contractor at 605-367-8672 or on the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list. Permit fees are usually modest, but they should appear on your quote as a line item. If a contractor doesn't mention permits, ask directly whether they'll be pulling them.

An unpermitted installation can create problems when you sell the home or file an insurance claim. It also means no city inspection confirmed the work was done correctly.

## Add-Ons That Affect the Final Invoice

Several add-ons come up frequently during HVAC installs in Sioux Falls, and knowing about them in advance helps you decide before you're standing in front of a salesperson.

- **Whole-home humidifiers.** Sioux Falls winters pull a lot of moisture out of indoor air, and a whole-home humidifier installed on the furnace addresses that at the system level. Many homeowners add one at install time because the labor cost to do it later is higher. See [Is a Whole-Home Humidifier Worth It in Sioux Falls?](/guides/whole-home-humidifier-sioux-falls) if you're on the fence.
- **Air cleaners and media filters.** These range from upgraded filter housings to UV or electronic air treatment systems. Costs and effectiveness vary. Ask what's being proposed and why.
- **Smart thermostats.** Often included or offered at install. They typically add a small cost and can be worth it for the scheduling features, especially if the home is vacant during the day.
- **Extended warranties or labor warranties.** Equipment warranties come from the manufacturer. Labor warranties come from the contractor. These are different things. Ask what each covers and for how long, and read it before you sign.

## How to Compare Bids Line by Line

Getting multiple bids is worth the time, but comparing them honestly requires that they're structured the same way. Ask every contractor to break out the same categories:

- Equipment model and efficiency rating (so you can look it up yourself)
- Labor for installation
- Any ductwork modifications, described specifically
- Venting changes
- Electrical work
- Permit fees
- Disposal of old equipment
- Any add-ons, listed separately

A lower total price that buries a substandard efficiency unit in a lump sum is not a better deal. A higher price that includes duct sealing your home actually needed is not necessarily worse.

If two bids are far apart, ask each contractor to walk you through why. Sometimes the difference is equipment quality or scope of work. Sometimes one contractor missed something the other caught. Sioux Falls homeowners who have gotten second opinions on HVAC quotes have found significant differences for what seemed like the same job. ## Timing and Season

HVAC companies in Sioux Falls are busiest when systems fail, which means July heat waves and January cold snaps. If your furnace or AC is aging but still working, scheduling replacement in the shoulder seasons (spring or fall) often means more contractor availability and sometimes better pricing on equipment.

This isn't guaranteed, but it's worth asking about when you're not making an emergency decision. A system that failed on the coldest day of the year doesn't leave you much room to compare bids carefully.
