If you're replacing siding on your Sioux Falls home, two things need to happen before anyone picks up a nail gun: a permit needs to be pulled and the contractor needs to hold the right city license. Most homeowners don't know this until something goes wrong. The good news is that both are easy to verify before work starts.

This article is a companion to [New Siding in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/siding-guide-sioux-falls), which covers the full picture of a siding project from materials to quotes. Here we focus only on the permit and licensing side, because that's where homeowners get into trouble.

## Does Re-Siding Require a Permit in Sioux Falls?

Yes. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, re-siding is explicitly listed as a project that requires a building permit. This is true even when no structural changes are being made, like a straight panel-for-panel replacement after hail damage.

Painting and similar finish work do not require a permit. But replacing the siding itself does, every time.

This matters for a few reasons:

- A permit creates an inspection record, which protects you if you sell the home.
- Work done without a permit can complicate your homeowner's insurance claim.
- If an unpermitted job causes damage later, you may have limited recourse.

The permit is typically pulled by your contractor, not by you. But you have every right to ask for the permit number before work starts, and you should.

## Which License Does a Siding Contractor Need in Sioux Falls?

The City of Sioux Falls created a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license in 2024. According to the City, this license covers re-shingling, re-siding, and window or door replacement where no structural changes are involved.

Contractors doing broader residential work on one and two family homes and townhomes within city limits must hold a Residential Building Contractor's License. The City does not accept licenses from other states in place of these local credentials.

That second point matters after major hail storms, when out-of-state crews often show up in force. A contractor licensed in Minnesota or Iowa is not automatically qualified to work on your Sioux Falls home. The City's requirement is a local license, full stop.

If your project also involves electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work (say, adding outlets to a detached garage at the same time), those require separate licensed contractors for each trade. Building permits do not cover that work automatically.

## How to Verify a Contractor Is Licensed

The City of Sioux Falls publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors. It's a public document and takes about two minutes to check.

Before you sign anything, look up your contractor's name there. Here's what to do:

- Go to siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.
- Search for the company or contractor name.
- Confirm the license type matches the work (Roofing and Repair or Residential Building Contractor).
- Confirm the license is current, not expired.

If a contractor isn't on that list, ask them to explain why before you go any further. A legitimate contractor will not be bothered by this question.

Also ask whether they will pull the permit themselves. If a contractor suggests you pull the permit as the homeowner, that's worth a direct follow-up question. Homeowners who own and live in a single-family home can pull certain permits themselves under City rules, but doing so for a contractor's work shifts responsibility to you.

## What About Homes Outside Sioux Falls City Limits?

City of Sioux Falls permit and licensing requirements apply within city limits. If your home is in Minnehaha County or Lincoln County but outside the city boundary, different rules may apply.

For unincorporated county areas, check with the relevant county planning and zoning office rather than the City. Requirements vary, and assuming city rules apply when you're just outside the boundary is a common mistake.

If you're in an incorporated city nearby (Tea, Harrisburg, Brandon, and others each have their own building departments), contact that city's building office directly.

When in doubt about whether your address falls under City of Sioux Falls jurisdiction, call City Building Services and ask. They can confirm quickly.

## Questions to Ask Before You Hire

Homeowners who've been through a big siding job, especially after hail, often say the stress wasn't the work itself but the communication and paperwork. Here are the specific questions to put in writing before signing a contract:

- Are you currently licensed with the City of Sioux Falls? Which license type?
- Will you pull the building permit for this project?
- Can I have the permit number once it's issued?
- Will the work be inspected, and who schedules that?
- If you're bringing in a subcontractor for any part of this, are they also licensed with the City?

That last question matters. Some siding companies subcontract installation crews. The license of the company you hire doesn't automatically cover an unlicensed sub.

After a hail storm, you may feel pressure to decide fast. If a contractor is knocking on your door the day after a storm and pushing for a same-day signature, slow down.
