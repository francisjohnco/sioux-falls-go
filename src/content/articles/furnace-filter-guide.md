Swapping your furnace filter is the one piece of HVAC maintenance almost every Sioux Falls homeowner can do without any tools and without calling anyone. It is also the one task that gets skipped the longest, usually because nobody is sure which filter to buy or how often to actually change it. Get it wrong in either direction and you either breathe more dust or you choke your furnace into working twice as hard. The full picture of how your heating system fits together is in the [Heating and Cooling in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/hvac-guide-sioux-falls), but this article goes deep on filters specifically.

## Finding the Right Filter Size for Your Furnace

The size printed on your current filter is not optional. It is usually printed on the cardboard frame in a format like 16x25x1 or 20x25x4, those numbers are width, height, and thickness in inches.

The thickness is where people most often grab the wrong one off the shelf. Most Sioux Falls homes with standard slot furnaces take a 1-inch filter. Some newer or higher-end units have a wide filter cabinet that holds a 4-inch or 5-inch media filter, and those two systems are not interchangeable.

If your filter slot has no filter in it at all, or the filter is so caked you can't read the numbers, measure the slot opening itself. The actual filter will be about half an inch smaller than the slot in each dimension.

## Understanding MERV Ratings Without the Jargon

MERV stands for Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value. The higher the number, the smaller the particles the filter catches. Here is what the common ranges actually mean for a homeowner:

- **MERV 1 to 4:** Fiberglass panel filters. Cheap, almost no particle capture, mostly keeps large debris off the blower wheel. Not recommended for most homes.
- **MERV 5 to 8:** Standard pleated filters. Catches dust, pollen, and pet dander. A MERV 8 is the practical sweet spot for most Sioux Falls homes without special air quality needs.
- **MERV 9 to 12:** Better at capturing finer particles including mold spores and some bacteria. Good choice if someone in the home has allergies or asthma.
- **MERV 13 and above:** Hospital-grade filtration. Catches very fine particles but creates real airflow resistance in systems not designed for it.

The problem with chasing the highest MERV number is airflow. Your furnace blower is sized for a specific static pressure, and a dense MERV 13 or 16 filter in a system designed for a MERV 8 forces the blower to work harder. Over time that strains the motor and can cause the heat exchanger to run hotter than it should.

For most homes in Sioux Falls, a pleated MERV 8 is the honest best answer. If you have pets or allergies, a MERV 11 is reasonable. If your system has a 4-inch or 5-inch media cabinet, those thicker filters are engineered to handle higher MERV ratings without hurting airflow, so a MERV 11 or MERV 13 in that cabinet is fine.

## How Often to Change Filters in Sioux Falls Seasons

Generic advice says every 90 days. That schedule works fine in a mild climate with no pets and nobody doing construction. Sioux Falls is not that situation for most of the year.

The furnace runs hard here. We get plenty of snow every winter, and heating season realistically runs from October through April. During those months your furnace may cycle dozens of times a day, moving a lot of air through that filter fast.

A more realistic Sioux Falls schedule looks like this:

- **1-inch pleated MERV 8, one person, no pets:** Every 60 days during heating season, every 90 days in summer when only the AC fan runs intermittently.
- **1-inch pleated MERV 8, multiple people or one pet:** Every 30 to 45 days during heating season.
- **1-inch pleated MERV 8, multiple pets or someone with allergies:** Every 20 to 30 days.
- **4-inch or 5-inch media filter:** Every 6 to 12 months, depending on pets and dust load. These are genuinely designed to last longer.

Pull the filter out and hold it up to a light. If you can't see light through it, it needed to go last week. That is a faster check than any calendar.

## Sioux Falls Conditions That Burn Through Filters Faster

A few local situations will shorten filter life noticeably and are worth knowing about.

The Big Sioux Aquifer supplies Sioux Falls with hard water, measuring roughly 12 to 15 grains per gallon of hardness. That hardness does not directly hit your filter, but if you have a whole-home humidifier running in winter (common here because heated South Dakota air gets very dry), and the humidifier is not maintained, mineral dust can enter the air stream and load up your filter faster than normal.

Clay soil is common in older Sioux Falls neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints. If you have ever done any yard work, garden edging, or basement work in a clay-heavy yard, fine clay particles work their way in. During a remodel or any work that opens walls, change your filter the day the work is done and again 30 days later regardless of your normal schedule. The [Is Duct Cleaning Worth It? What to Expect](/guides/duct-cleaning-worth-it) article covers what else remodeling dust does to your system.

Pets are the other big factor. Pet dander is light enough to stay airborne for hours, and in a home with two or more shedding animals, a 1-inch MERV 8 filter can look fully loaded in three weeks during winter when windows stay closed.

## Setting a Reminder That Actually Works

The honest reason filters get forgotten is that the furnace is in the basement or a utility closet and nobody walks by it. Out of sight, out of mind.

A few approaches that actually stick:

- **Buy a 6-pack or 12-pack at a time.** When the stack runs out, you notice. Buying one at a time means a trip to the store becomes the barrier.
- **Set a phone reminder on the first of every month during heating season.** Even if you don't change it every month, pulling it out and looking at it once a month takes 30 seconds and tells you whether you need to.
- **Tape the purchase date on the filter frame with a piece of painter's tape** when you install it. When you pull it out, you know exactly how long it's been in.
- **Tie it to something you already do.** Some Sioux Falls homeowners change their filter the same week they change the clocks in fall and spring, which at least catches the two biggest seasonal transitions.

If your furnace has been running fine but suddenly you notice rooms are slower to heat up, the filter is usually the first thing to check before anything else. The [Furnace Not Working? What to Check Before You Call](/guides/furnace-not-working-sioux-falls) article walks through those checks step by step, and a clogged filter is on that list for good reason.

If you want the filter conversation to happen automatically, some HVAC maintenance plans include filter checks at seasonal visits. The [HVAC Maintenance Plans, Are They Worth Paying For?](/guides/hvac-maintenance-plans-explained) article explains what to look for in those agreements.
