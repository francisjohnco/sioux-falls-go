Building a custom home in Sioux Falls involves four distinct buckets of spending: the lot, site work, the house itself, and the soft costs most people underestimate until they get the invoice. Each bucket has its own variables, its own timing, and its own opportunities to control the total. If you are early in the process, the [How to Build a Custom Home in Sioux Falls (2026 Guide)](/guides/build-custom-home-sioux-falls) lays out the full sequence from lot search to move-in. This article goes deep on the numbers so you know what questions to ask before you sign anything.

One thing to know upfront: no published source gives a reliable Sioux Falls-specific average cost per square foot for custom construction, and any website that confidently prints one is usually parroting a national figure that may have little to do with your actual project. What you can do is understand what drives the number up or down, get three written bids from licensed builders, and compare them line by line. That approach will tell you far more than any industry average.

## Lot Costs in Sioux Falls Right Now

The lot is often the first check you write, and in 2026 it is a substantial one. According to SiouxFalls.Business reporting from April 2026, Sioux Falls had an unusually active premium lot market. The Sanctuary, a 200-acre development in northeast Sioux Falls by Deffenbaugh Homes, carried an average lot list price of about $333,000 in spring 2026, and those lots made up roughly 70 to 75 percent of Sioux Falls lot sales above $200,000.

If that price point is above your budget, there are other options. Mapleton Highlands, near Mapleton Golf Club and developed by Van Buskirk Cos., opened with 66 lots and 60 more planned. SiouxFalls.Business reported in April 2026 that most lots there ran $150,000 to $275,000, though lots along the golf course exceeded $550,000. Van Buskirk also had Westwater at Cherry Lake planned for the far west side, with Willow Ridge in northeast Sioux Falls, River Park in Brandon, and Heritage Pond in Crooks also active. Empire Cos. announced a 75-acre development off 57th Street between Highway 11 and Six Mile Road, and SouthLoop near 69th Street and Sycamore Avenue, both mixing single-family homes with villas and townhomes.

Lot price is not just a number, it is a signal. A $150,000 lot in an established neighborhood near McKennan Park or All Saints may come with existing infrastructure but tighter setbacks and architectural covenants. A $275,000 lot in a new outer-ring development may require you to extend utilities or pave a longer driveway. Before you close on any lot, the article [Buying a Building Lot in Sioux Falls, What to Check](/guides/buying-a-lot-sioux-falls) walks through what to verify: covenants, builder requirements, utility specials, soil and drainage, and school boundaries. Those details affect your total cost as much as the sticker price on the land.

## Site Work: The Cost That Surprises People

Site work is everything that happens between closing on the lot and pouring concrete, and it is notoriously hard to estimate without eyes on the specific property. It includes clearing, grading, excavation, temporary power, erosion control, utility connections, and the driveway approach. On a flat lot with utilities stubbed to the property line, site work is relatively straightforward. On a sloped lot, a heavily wooded lot, or a lot with expansive clay soil, it can be a significant line item.

Sioux Falls building code requires footings at 42 inches below grade because of the region's frost depth. That standard protects foundations from freeze-thaw movement, but it also means every foundation, whether a full basement or a frost wall under a slab, involves real excavation. The area's clay-heavy soil compounds this: poor drainage can undermine footings and cause long-term movement, so grading away from the foundation matters from day one. Before any digging begins, South Dakota law requires you to call 811 (South Dakota One Call) so underground utilities are marked. This is not optional, and your builder should handle it as a standard step, but confirm it in your contract.

A walkout basement adds complexity and cost compared to a standard lookout or flat lot excavation, because the contractor is shaping the grade to expose one side of the foundation wall. If you are weighing a full basement against a slab, the article [Basement or Slab on Grade in Sioux Falls?](/guides/basement-or-slab-sioux-falls) compares the real trade-offs in cost, storm shelter, resale, and clay soil risk. Notably, SiouxFalls.Business reported in April 2026 that builders were seeing strong demand for slab-on-grade homes from buyers relocating from Minnesota and Iowa, many in the 53-to-65 age range, suggesting that the premium neighborhoods are increasingly building that option.

## What Moves the House Cost Per Square Foot

The cost of the structure itself is where most budget conversations happen, and it is also where the most variables live. Without a Sioux Falls-specific published average to cite, the most honest thing to say is this: the cost per square foot for a custom home in Sioux Falls depends heavily on five factors, and changing any one of them can move the number substantially.

**Foundation type.** A full basement adds livable or finishable square footage but also adds excavation, concrete, waterproofing, and egress windows. A slab eliminates the basement cost but means you are paying finished-floor prices for every square foot. Neither is automatically cheaper per finished square foot; it depends on how you count the space.

**Finished square footage and footprint shape.** Smaller homes often cost more per square foot than larger ones because fixed costs (mechanical systems, roof structure, exterior cladding) are spread over fewer square feet. Simple rectangular footprints cost less than complex shapes with multiple bump-outs, rooflines, and corners. Every inside corner on a floor plan is a more expensive outside corner on the structure.

**Finishes and selections.** The gap between builder-grade and custom finishes is enormous. Cabinetry, countertops, flooring, windows, plumbing fixtures, and trim packages each have wide price ranges. Allowances in a contract set a budget for each category, but they are frequently underestimated. Ask your builder what they assume in every allowance line and whether that number reflects what you actually want. The article [Fixed Price or Cost Plus, Home Builder Contracts Explained](/guides/fixed-price-vs-cost-plus-home-contract) goes deeper on how allowances work and how they can quietly blow up a budget.

**Labor and material costs in 2026.** SiouxFalls.Business reported in April 2026 that new residential construction value in Sioux Falls was $23.8 million in the first quarter of 2026, compared to $32.3 million in the same quarter of 2025. That pullback, with 604 single-family homes and townhomes under construction on April 1, 2026 versus 821 a year earlier, may affect subcontractor availability and scheduling in ways that cut both directions. A slower market sometimes improves bid pricing; it can also mean fewer experienced subs are actively working.

**Builder type and customization level.** A fully custom builder designing to your specifications from scratch operates differently, and prices differently, than a semi-custom builder working from a plan library. Understanding which kind of builder you are hiring is worth getting clear on before you compare bids.

## Soft Costs: Permits, Design, and Financing

Soft costs are the expenses that do not go into the physical structure but are required to legally build and finance it. They are real money and they belong in your budget from the start.

**Permits.** Building permits in Sioux Falls are issued by Building Services, located on the first floor of City Center at 231 N. Dakota Avenue (phone 605-367-8670). According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, building permit fees start at $40 and increase with the construction value according to the city's 2026 fee schedule. Electrical, mechanical, and plumbing work each require their own separate permits with their own fee schedules. Your builder will typically pull all permits and manage inspections, but it is worth confirming that in your contract, and you should know that work must begin and an inspection must be requested within 180 days of permit issuance, per city rules.

If your project is outside Sioux Falls city limits, in Harrisburg, Tea, Brandon, or Crooks for example, permitting goes through that city or through Minnehaha County or Lincoln County, not through Sioux Falls Building Services. Requirements and fees may differ.

**Design and architecture.** If you are hiring an architect or residential designer separately from your builder, their fees are part of your soft cost total. Even if your builder includes design in their fee, you may pay for surveys, soil borings, or engineering reports depending on the lot.

**Financing costs.** A construction loan works differently from a standard mortgage, and the carrying costs during the build (interest on draws, loan origination, and potentially two closings) are real line items. SiouxFalls.Business noted in April 2026 that more than 75 percent of buyers in the top lot segment paid cash, but most buyers finance. The article [How Construction Loans Work When You Build in Sioux Falls](/guides/construction-loans-sioux-falls) explains the draw process, what lenders want to see, and the difference between a construction-to-permanent loan and a two-close structure.

## How to Get Comparable Written Bids

Getting three bids is the standard advice, but bids are only useful if they are comparing the same thing. Ask each builder to bid off the same set of plans and specifications, or at minimum to itemize their bid by category: foundation, framing, exterior, roofing, windows, mechanicals, interior finishes, and site work. If one bid is notably lower, the question is not whether to celebrate but which line items are thinner and why.

Ask every builder to confirm they are licensed with the City of Sioux Falls. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, you can verify a contractor's license by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list online. You can also look up permit history on the city's permit data site, which updates every Sunday, to see what a builder has pulled recently and whether inspections were completed.

Details are at siouxempireparadeofhomes.com, and the Home Builders Association of the Sioux Empire is involved. Walking parade homes is one of the best ways to see builder quality in person before you commit, and to have an honest conversation with a builder on their own finished work.
