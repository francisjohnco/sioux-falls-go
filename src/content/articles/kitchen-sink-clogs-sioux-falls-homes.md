The kitchen sink is the most clog-prone drain in most Sioux Falls homes, and the reasons go beyond just what you pour down it. Grease hardens faster in cold pipes, the Big Sioux Aquifer delivers water that runs 12.8 to 15 grains of hardness per gallon, and that mineral load builds scale that narrows pipes over years. Add a garbage disposal that gets used as a compost bin and you have a drain that will back up sooner or later. If you want the full picture of how drain cleaning works in Sioux Falls, start with [The Sioux Falls Homeowner's Guide to Drain Cleaning](/guides/drain-cleaning-sioux-falls), then come back here for the kitchen-specific details.

## Why Kitchen Drain Clogs Happen in Sioux Falls

The number one villain is grease, and it behaves worse here than the bottle of dish soap commercials suggest.

When you pour bacon fat, pan drippings, or even cooking oil down the drain, it leaves the pipe as a warm liquid. By the time it reaches the trap or the horizontal run behind your wall, it has cooled and started to stick. Sioux Falls winters drive unheated crawl space and exterior wall temperatures down hard, which speeds that process considerably.

Grease does not travel to the city main and disappear. It coats the inside of the pipe, then catches food particles, coffee grounds, and soap residue on every pass. Over months that layer thickens until water barely moves.

The second factor is hard water scale. The same mineral deposit that ruins your kettle and shower head is quietly narrowing your kitchen drain line. A pipe that is already half-coated in calcium scale does not need much grease to slow to a crawl. This is worth understanding because it explains why a kitchen drain in an older All Saints or McKennan Park home might clog repeatedly even when the household is careful about what goes down it.

## What Not to Put Down a Garbage Disposal

The disposal is one of the most misunderstood appliances in the kitchen. It grinds food, but it does not make everything safe to drain. Here is what causes the most trouble:

- **Grease and cooking oil.** Even small amounts coat the grinding chamber and the drain line.
- **Coffee grounds.** They feel like they rinse away but accumulate in the trap in dense, muddy layers.
- **Fibrous vegetables.** Celery, asparagus, artichoke leaves, and corn husks wrap around the impeller and can jam the unit or escape in stringy clumps that snag other debris.
- **Starchy foods.** Potato peels, pasta, and rice expand when wet and form a paste that is hard to flush through.
- **Eggshells.** The membrane inside can wrap around moving parts; the gritty shell material travels downstream and sticks to grease.
- **Bones and fruit pits.** These can damage the impeller and are not worth the risk.

A good rule is to treat the disposal as a cleanup tool for small food residue left on plates, not as a substitute for the trash or compost bin. Run cold water (not hot) while the disposal runs and keep it running for about 20 seconds after you shut the unit off. Cold water keeps grease solid so it moves through rather than coating the pipe walls.

## What a Homeowner Can Safely Try First

Before calling anyone, there are a few steps that work on a true kitchen drain clog and do not carry much risk of pipe damage.

**Boiling or very hot water.** For metal pipe (common in older Sioux Falls homes with cast iron or galvanized lines), carefully pouring a full kettle of boiling water down the drain in two or three slow passes can soften and move a grease clog. Skip this if you have PVC pipe under the sink, since boiling water can soften PVC joints over time. Very hot tap water is safer for PVC and still moves fresh grease.

**Dish soap and hot water.** Squirt a generous amount of dish soap directly into the drain, then follow with the hottest water your tap produces. The degreasing surfactants in dish soap do actually help break up light grease accumulation.

**A cup plunger.** A standard cup plunger (not a flange plunger, which is for toilets) can dislodge a clog in the trap or the short horizontal run. Seal the other basin if you have a double sink, place the plunger over the drain with an inch or two of water in the basin, and work it firmly up and down 10 to 15 times.

**A hand-turned drain snake.** A 15 to 25 foot hand snake feeds through the drain past the trap and can break up or retrieve a soft clog. This is a reasonable tool to own. If you are unsure about the difference between a hand snake and chemical options, [Chemical Drain Cleaners vs. a Plumbing Snake](/guides/drain-cleaner-chemicals-vs-plumbing-snake) lays out the tradeoffs clearly.

What to avoid: chemical drain cleaners with lye or sulfuric acid. They are hard on aging pipe, leave a residue that a plumber has to work around, and often do not fully clear a grease clog. They just punch a temporary hole through it.

## When the Problem Is Bigger Than a Single Clog

A kitchen clog that comes back within a few weeks of being cleared is telling you something. Either the original clog was not fully removed, or there is a condition in the pipe itself that keeps collecting debris.

In Sioux Falls homes built before the 1970s, cast iron kitchen drain lines are common. Cast iron develops rough interior surfaces as it ages, and those surfaces catch grease faster than smooth PVC. Mineral scale from the local water supply compounds this over time.

If your kitchen drain backs up repeatedly, or if other drains in the house (the basement floor drain, a bathroom sink) slow down at the same time, the problem may be further downstream in the branch line or even the main sewer line. That is a different repair than clearing a trap. [Why Do My Drains Keep Clogging](/guides/why-do-my-drains-keep-clogging) covers the most common causes of repeat clogs and how to narrow down which one you are dealing with.

A professional in this situation will often run a camera to see whether the pipe interior is scaled, corroded, or partially collapsed before recommending a cleaning method. That matters because hydro jetting, which is the most effective method for grease-coated pipe, should not be used on a pipe that is already fragile.

## When to Call a Plumber and What to Ask

Call a licensed plumber or drain cleaning company when:

- Standing water will not move at all, or drains back up into the basin.
- You have tried the safe DIY steps and the drain slows again within a day or two.
- Multiple fixtures are slow or backing up at the same time.
- You hear gurgling from other drains when the kitchen sink runs.
- There is an odor that persists even after the drain runs clear.

When you call, ask a few specific questions before booking:

- Do you charge a flat rate or by the hour, and does the diagnostic visit cost extra if you decide not to proceed?
- Will you tell me what you find before you start work, and provide a written quote?
- Are you licensed in South Dakota? (Plumbers working on drain lines in Sioux Falls are required to hold a South Dakota plumbing license.)
- If the clog comes back within 30 days, what is your policy?

For more on evaluating a company before you book, [How to Choose a Drain Cleaning Company in Sioux Falls](/guides/how-to-choose-a-drain-cleaning-company-sioux-falls) has a full checklist of questions and red flags to watch for.

Nationally, snake service on a kitchen line typically runs somewhere in the range of $100 to $250, while hydro jetting costs more. Those are not Sioux Falls averages, just a general frame of reference. Always get a written quote specific to your situation before any work starts.
