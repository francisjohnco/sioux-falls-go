Hiring a custom home builder is one of the biggest financial decisions most people make, and in Sioux Falls right now, the stakes are real. According to SiouxFalls.Business reporting from April 2026, there were 604 single-family homes and townhomes under construction in the city on April 1, 2026, down from 821 a year earlier, which means builders vary widely in capacity, focus, and experience. The right questions, asked before you sign anything, tell you far more than a glossy brochure ever will. If you want the full picture of how the building process works from lot to move-in, start with the [How to Build a Custom Home in Sioux Falls (2026 Guide)](/guides/build-custom-home-sioux-falls), then come back here with this list in hand.

These 25 questions are grouped by topic so you can work through them in a logical order during your first few meetings with any builder you are seriously considering. A good builder will not be rattled by any of them. In fact, the ones who have been doing this well in Sioux Falls for years will appreciate that you have done your homework.

## Company Background and Licensing

The first thing to establish is whether the builder is properly licensed and genuinely experienced in this market. Sioux Falls licenses residential contractors separately from electricians, plumbers, and mechanical contractors, and you can verify any of those licenses by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs. Do not skip this step.

**1. Are you licensed as a residential contractor in Sioux Falls, and can I verify that myself?**
A good answer is yes, here is my license number, and you can confirm it on the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list or by calling 605-367-8672.

**2. How long have you been building custom homes specifically in the Sioux Falls area?**
A good answer names specific neighborhoods or developments, not just years in business, because experience in another state or even another metro does not automatically translate to South Dakota soil conditions and building codes.

**3. How many custom homes do you typically build per year, and how many do you have under construction right now?**
A good answer is specific and honest; a builder stretched across too many projects at once is a warning sign for communication delays and scheduling problems.

**4. Who will be my primary point of contact throughout the build, and how accessible are they?**
A good answer names a specific person and describes their communication process, including how quickly they typically return calls or messages and how decisions get documented.

**5. Can I speak with three to five homeowners whose homes you completed two to five years ago, not just recent builds?**
A good answer is yes without hesitation; references from two to five years out reveal how the builder handles long-term warranty claims and whether the relationship held up after move-in.

For a thorough walkthrough of how to evaluate builders beyond just this conversation, the [How to Choose a Custom Home Builder in Sioux Falls](/guides/choose-custom-home-builder-sioux-falls) guide covers license checks, Parade of Homes visits, and red flags worth knowing.

## Price and Contract

Money questions make some people uncomfortable, but a reputable builder expects them and answers them clearly. The contract structure matters as much as the bottom-line number, and understanding allowances before you sign can save you from a genuinely painful surprise mid-build.

**6. Is your contract fixed price or cost plus, and can you walk me through exactly how each type works?**
A good answer explains the structure clearly and without defensiveness; if the builder brushes past this question, that is a red flag. The [Fixed Price or Cost Plus, Home Builder Contracts Explained](/guides/fixed-price-vs-cost-plus-home-contract) guide explains both in detail if you want to prepare before this conversation.

**7. What is included in your base price, and what is typically handled through allowances?**
A good answer lists specific line items and gives realistic allowance figures based on what their recent buyers have actually spent, not the minimum number that fits on a marketing sheet.

**8. How do you handle change orders, and what does a change order cost me in both dollars and schedule time?**
A good answer acknowledges that changes happen, explains the written approval process, and gives an honest sense of typical markup and timeline impact.

**9. What does your payment draw schedule look like, and does it align with construction milestones?**
A good answer ties draws to verifiable progress points, like foundation pour, framing complete, rough-in complete, drywall hung, and final, rather than arbitrary calendar dates.

**10. Will you provide a fully itemized bid, and can I get comparable bids from other builders using the same plans?**
A good answer is yes to both; a builder who refuses to itemize or discourages comparison shopping is not confident in their pricing.

**11. What are the most common reasons your projects come in over the original budget, and what do you do to prevent that?**
A good answer is specific and self-aware, mentioning things like allowance overages, late selections, or material price fluctuations, along with the process they use to flag issues early.

## Schedule and Timeline

Building timelines in Sioux Falls have real local constraints. The ground freezes to a depth of 42 inches under building code requirements, and wet South Dakota springs can delay excavation and foundation work by weeks. A builder who cannot speak to these realities in concrete terms has probably not thought carefully enough about your project.

**12. What is the realistic timeline for a home like mine, from signed contract to move-in?**
A good answer gives a range tied to specific phases, not a single number, and accounts for the permit approval timeline through City of Sioux Falls Building Services (located at 231 N. Dakota Avenue) and any seasonal factors.

**13. What time of year do you plan to break ground, and how does that affect the schedule?**
A good answer explains the relationship between start date and foundation work, particularly around frozen ground in late fall and early winter, and gives an honest assessment of whether your target move-in date is realistic.

**14. What has caused the most schedule delays on your recent projects, and how did you handle them?**
A good answer is honest, mentions things like subcontractor availability, inspection scheduling, or buyer selections made late, and describes what they do to protect the schedule when those things happen.

**15. How do you handle subcontractors? Do you use the same ones consistently, and are they also licensed in Sioux Falls?**
A good answer confirms that their electrical, plumbing, and mechanical subs hold current city licenses, which you can verify independently on the city's contractor list.

**16. Under South Dakota One Call (811) rules, who is responsible for getting underground utilities marked before excavation?**
A good answer confirms the builder handles this, as the City of Sioux Falls requires calling 811 before any digging so underground utilities are marked, and the builder should be familiar with this as a matter of routine.

## Construction Quality

Quality questions are where you separate builders who talk well from builders who build well. Ask to visit a home currently under construction, not just finished model homes, so you can see framing, foundation work, and mechanical rough-ins before the walls close up.

**17. Can I visit a home you have under construction right now, not just a finished home?**
A good answer is yes; a builder who only shows finished homes is managing your perception rather than your confidence.

**18. How do you handle the expansive clay soils and freeze-thaw cycles common in Sioux Falls, particularly for drainage and grading around the foundation?**
A good answer demonstrates specific knowledge of local soil behavior, describes their approach to grading away from the foundation, and explains how they handle the 42-inch frost depth requirement for footings.

**19. What insulation and air-sealing strategy do you use, and how does it perform against South Dakota winters?**
A good answer goes beyond minimum code compliance and describes specific materials, R-values, and air-sealing methods, since heating costs in a Sioux Falls winter are a real ongoing expense.

**20. Who inspects your work during construction, and do you encourage buyers to hire an independent inspector?**
A good answer confirms that the builder pulls permits and handles required city inspections at each phase, and a confident builder will not object to an independent inspector doing pre-pour, pre-drywall, and final inspections on your behalf.

**21. How do you handle punch list items at the final walkthrough, and what is your process for getting them resolved before closing?**
A good answer describes a written punch list, a clear timeline for completion, and a process for holding back funds or delaying closing if significant items remain unresolved.

## Warranty and After Move-In

A warranty is only as good as the builder who stands behind it. South Dakota does not mandate a specific statutory warranty structure for new homes, so what you get is entirely what is written into your contract. Read every word of the warranty section before you sign.

**22. What warranty do you provide, and what does it cover for workmanship, systems, and structural defects?**
A good answer breaks the warranty into tiers with specific timeframes for each, distinguishing between cosmetic workmanship, mechanical systems, and structural coverage, and gives you the terms in writing.

**23. What is specifically excluded from your warranty, and what voids it?**
A good answer lists exclusions clearly and without vagueness; common exclusions include owner modifications, normal settlement, and damage from improper maintenance, and you need to know these before you are surprised by them later.

**24. How do I submit a warranty claim, and how quickly do you typically respond?**
A good answer describes a specific process, names who handles warranty calls, and gives a realistic response time commitment in writing, not just a verbal promise.

**25. After the warranty period ends, what is your track record for helping past clients with issues that fall outside coverage?**
A good answer acknowledges that goodwill matters and either describes specific instances where they helped beyond their contractual obligation, or is honest that the relationship is governed by the written warranty, so you know exactly what to expect.
