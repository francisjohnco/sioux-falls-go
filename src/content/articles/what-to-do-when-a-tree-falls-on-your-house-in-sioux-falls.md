That sickening crack followed by a thunderous crash is something no Sioux Falls homeowner ever wants to hear. When a tree falls on your house in Sioux Falls, the next few hours matter immensely. Whether it happened during one of our notorious summer thunderstorms rolling across the prairie, an ice-laden February morning when branches finally gave way, or a derecho that swept through Minnehaha County, knowing exactly what to do can protect your family, minimize property damage, and help you navigate insurance claims successfully. This emergency guide walks you through every critical step.

## Immediate Safety Steps When a Tree Falls on Your House

The moment you realize a tree has struck your home, your first priority is human safety. Nothing else matters until everyone is accounted for and out of danger.

**Get everyone out immediately.** Don't stop to grab valuables or assess damage. A tree impact can compromise structural integrity in ways that aren't immediately visible. Gather family members, pets included, and exit through a door away from the impact zone. If the tree fell on or near your garage, don't use that exit since garage structures are particularly vulnerable to secondary collapse.

**Call 911 if there's any immediate danger.** This includes visible gas leaks (that distinctive rotten egg smell), downed power lines anywhere on your property, fire or smoke, or anyone trapped inside. Sioux Falls Fire Rescue responds to these emergencies and will coordinate with Xcel Energy for electrical hazards.

**Stay at least 35 feet away from any downed power lines.** Here in Sioux Falls, our mature neighborhood trees often intertwine with overhead lines along streets like Grange Avenue, Cliff Avenue, and throughout the Cathedral District. Even if a line looks dead, it can carry lethal voltage. The ground around it can also be energized. Call Xcel Energy's emergency line at 1-800-895-1999 to report downed lines.

**Don't re-enter the home** until a professional has assessed structural safety. I know it's tempting to rush back in for medications, documents, or irreplaceable items, but floor joists, load-bearing walls, and roof trusses can fail without warning after initial impact.

## Assessing the Damage from Outside Your Home

Once immediate safety is secured, take a breath and start documenting from a safe distance. This documentation becomes critical for your insurance claim.

**Photograph everything before anything moves.** Using your phone, capture wide shots showing the full scene, including where the tree came from (the root ball or break point), how it's resting on your home, and any visible damage to the structure. Get close-up shots of specific damage points, but don't approach anything unstable. Photograph the time stamp your phone automatically records.

Look for these specific hazards from outside:

  * Visible holes in the roof or siding where rain can enter
  * Broken windows creating security concerns
  * The tree still shifting or settling on the structure
  * Damage to your gas meter, typically located on exterior walls
  * Water or natural gas hissing sounds
  * Structural walls visibly bowed or displaced

If you see significant roof penetration and rain is imminent (checking KSFY or KELO weather radar), understand that water damage compounds quickly. This is where [emergency tree removal services in Sioux Falls](/guides/emergency-tree-removal-in-sioux-falls-storm-damage-and-24-7-service) become essential since many offer tarping services to prevent secondary water damage while the tree is being removed.

## Contacting Your Insurance Company and What to Expect

Call your homeowner's insurance company as soon as possible, ideally within the first hour after ensuring safety. Most South Dakota policies cover tree damage to structures, but the specifics vary significantly.

**What's typically covered:**

  * Structural repairs to your home
  * Tree removal from the structure itself
  * Temporary living expenses if your home is uninhabitable
  * Damaged personal property inside the home
  * Debris removal, usually with a per-tree cap around $500 to $1,000

**What's often not covered:**

  * Removal of the tree from your yard if it didn't hit a structure
  * Damage to landscaping
  * Preventive removal of other hazardous trees
  * Damage from tree roots

When you call, have this information ready: your policy number, date and time of the incident, description of the damage, and contact information. The adjuster will likely be assigned within 24 to 48 hours, though after major Sioux Falls storms this can stretch longer as they prioritize cases.

Ask specifically: "Do I need to wait for an adjuster before authorizing emergency tree removal and tarping?" Most policies allow immediate action to prevent further damage, but get this confirmed and documented. Request a claim number before ending the call.

## Finding Emergency Tree Removal Services in Sioux Falls

A tree on your house requires professional removal, not a neighbor with a chainsaw and good intentions. The stakes are too high. An inexperienced cut can shift thousands of pounds of weight, cause the tree to roll, or drop sections through already-weakened roof areas.

When searching for [tree services in Sioux Falls](/local-tree-services), prioritize these factors for emergency situations:

**24/7 availability:** True emergencies don't wait for business hours. Reputable Sioux Falls tree companies offer round-the-clock emergency response, especially during storm season from May through September.

**Crane capability:** Many tree-on-house situations require crane-assisted removal to lift sections away from the structure rather than dragging them across your roof. Ask specifically if they have crane access or subcontract for it.

**Insurance and licensing:** This is not the time to cut corners. Verify current liability insurance (minimum $1 million) and workers' compensation coverage. South Dakota doesn't require tree service licensing, which makes your verification even more important. ## Temporary Protection While Waiting for Removal

Depending on the situation, removal might happen within hours or might take several days if your emergency falls during a widespread storm event affecting all of Sioux Falls. Protecting your home during this gap prevents compounding damage.

**Tarping exposed areas:** Most emergency tree services offer tarping as part of their response. Heavy-duty tarps (6 mil minimum, blue tarps are standard) secured with furring strips and screws protect against rain infiltration. Don't attempt roof tarping yourself if it requires climbing onto a compromised structure.

**Boarding windows:** If the impact broke windows, boarding them with plywood prevents additional weather damage and deters opportunistic theft during your absence. Half-inch plywood secured with screws into the window frame is standard.

**Shutting off utilities selectively:** If you can safely access your electrical panel and the damage is near certain circuits, shutting those circuits off reduces fire risk. For gas, only shut off at the meter if you smell gas or the line appears damaged, since relighting the pilot requires a utility visit.

**Documenting ongoing conditions:** Continue photographing and taking video as conditions change. If rain comes before tarping is complete, document water intrusion. This becomes insurance evidence.

## Understanding Why Trees Fall on Sioux Falls Homes

While dealing with the immediate crisis, you might wonder why this happened. Understanding the cause can affect your insurance claim and help prevent future incidents.

**Storm damage:** Sioux Falls sits squarely in severe weather territory. Our summer thunderstorms can produce straight-line winds exceeding 70 mph, and our occasional derecho events bring winds over 100 mph. Even healthy trees fail under these conditions. The June 2014 storm and the August 2020 derecho demonstrated that no neighborhood is immune.

**Ice loading:** Our South Dakota winters bring ice storms that coat branches with inches of frozen weight. A mature tree can accumulate tons of ice weight. Combined with sustained winds across our open prairie landscape, branches and entire trees snap.

**Root failure:** Saturated soils during spring snowmelt or after heavy rains (like we often see in May and June) reduce root anchoring. A tree that stood for 50 years can topple when root ball integrity fails.

**Disease and decay:** Dutch elm disease, emerald ash borer (now confirmed in South Dakota), and various fungal infections weaken trees over years. By the time external symptoms appear, internal structure may be severely compromised. Regular inspection, covered in our [complete guide to professional tree care](/guides/complete-guide-to-professional-tree-care-in-sioux-falls), helps identify these risks before failure.

**Age and species:** Sioux Falls has many silver maples and Siberian elms planted decades ago. These species are prone to branch failure, especially when mature. Cottonwoods along the Big Sioux River corridor also pose elevated risk due to their brittle wood and massive size.

## After the Tree Is Removed and What Comes Next

Once emergency removal is complete and your home is temporarily protected, the recovery process begins.

**Work with your adjuster:** The insurance adjuster will assess damage and provide a settlement estimate. You're not obligated to use their preferred contractors. Get multiple repair estimates from licensed South Dakota contractors to ensure the settlement covers actual repair costs.

**Address structural repairs first:** Before any cosmetic work, ensure roof framing, wall studs, and foundational elements are properly repaired. Consider hiring an independent structural engineer for significant impacts, especially if the tree struck a load-bearing wall or caused visible foundation cracking.

**Evaluate remaining trees:** After experiencing a tree emergency, most homeowners look at their remaining trees differently. Having a certified arborist assess other mature trees on your property is wise. Understanding the [best time to trim trees in South Dakota](/local-tree-services) helps you plan proactive maintenance that reduces future risks.

**Consider stump removal:** The remaining stump from the fallen tree is typically a separate charge. You can leave it, have it ground down, or have it fully removed. Grinding is most common and allows replanting nearby if desired.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Who is responsible if my neighbor's tree falls on my house in Sioux Falls?

In most cases, your own homeowner's insurance covers damage to your structure regardless of where the tree originated. You would file a claim with your insurance company, not your neighbor's. The exception is if your neighbor knew the tree was dead or hazardous and ignored your documented requests to address it. In that case, you might have grounds for a liability claim against their policy. Document any pre-incident communications about tree concerns.

How long does emergency tree removal typically take in Sioux Falls?

Response time varies by severity and demand. During normal conditions, emergency tree services can typically arrive within 2 to 4 hours, sometimes sooner. After major storms affecting multiple neighborhoods, wait times can extend to 24 to 72 hours as companies work through emergency queues. Tree-on-house situations typically receive priority over trees that are down but not threatening structures.

Can I stay in my house if a tree fell on the roof but didn't penetrate inside?

Even if there's no visible interior damage, we strongly recommend against staying until a professional assesses structural integrity. Impact forces can crack roof trusses, displace rafters, and create hidden hazards. The weight of the remaining tree continues stressing the structure. Your insurance may cover temporary lodging, making this a safety decision without immediate financial consequence.

What if a tree falls on my house during a winter storm when roads are closed?

Document everything via photos, call your insurance to report the claim, and contact emergency tree services to get on their queue even if immediate response isn't possible. If you can safely do so without going onto the roof, use tarps to limit snow or ice intrusion from the ground level. Don't attempt any work that requires accessing the damaged structure until conditions allow professional help.

Will my insurance rates increase after filing a tree damage claim?

Tree damage claims are typically classified as weather-related or "act of God" events rather than negligence, which affects how insurers view them. Most homeowners see minimal to no rate increase for a single weather-related claim, though this varies by carrier and your claims history. Ask your agent directly about rate implications, and know that shopping for new coverage is always an option.

How much does emergency tree removal from a house cost in Sioux Falls?

Emergency tree-on-house removal typically ranges from $1,500 to $5,000 or more depending on tree size, position, crane requirements, and after-hours timing. This is significantly higher than standard tree removal due to the complexity, urgency, and additional safety protocols required. Most homeowner's insurance covers this cost minus your deductible when the tree damaged the structure.
