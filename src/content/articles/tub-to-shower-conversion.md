If you have a tub you have not used in years, you are not alone. Plenty of Sioux Falls homeowners have finally pulled the trigger on a tub to shower conversion after putting it off, worried it would take weeks and cost a fortune. The good news is that it usually takes far less time than people expect, and you have two very different paths to choose from. Before you decide, it helps to understand what each one actually involves. The [Remodeling a Bathroom in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/bathroom-remodel-guide-sioux-falls) covers the full remodel picture; this article focuses entirely on comparing prefabricated one-day systems with custom tile for a walk-in shower conversion.

## What a One-Day Prefabricated System Actually Is

A prefabricated or "liner" system uses acrylic or composite panels measured and cut to fit your existing tub alcove. The old tub is removed, the panels go over or against your existing walls, and a new shower base drops in. Many companies advertise a single-day installation, and that timeline is often accurate for a straightforward alcove with no plumbing moves.

The appeal is obvious. You are not living without a shower for days while tile sets and grout cures. Sioux Falls homeowners who have gone this route often say they were surprised the whole thing was done by afternoon.

What you are trading for speed is flexibility. The panels come in a limited range of colors and textures, and they will not look like a tile shower. They also cannot be easily repaired if one panel cracks or stains years down the road.

Questions to ask before signing:

- What is the panel thickness and what is behind it (is there any backer board, or does it go straight onto drywall)?
- How is the seam between the base and the wall panels waterproofed?
- What does the warranty cover, and for how long?
- Does the quote include removing and disposing of the old tub?

## What Custom Tile Involves and Where It Wins

A custom tile walk-in shower conversion is a multi-stage project. Demo comes first, then a plumber rough-ins the new drain location if it is moving, then a waterproofing membrane goes on before any tile touches the wall or floor. Tile, grout, and caulk each need cure time before the shower can be used.

The result can look exactly the way you want it. Large-format tiles, a bench, a niche for shampoo, a frameless glass door. Homeowners who go this route usually say the finished shower looks nothing like the cramped tub corner that was there before.

One real advantage in Sioux Falls specifically: our water is hard. According to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report, total hardness runs around 15 grains per gallon for city-treated water and around 10 grains per gallon for Lewis and Clark water. That mineral load leaves scale on glass doors and grout. With tile, you choose your grout color, finish, and sealant with that in mind. Larger tiles mean fewer grout lines to scrub. The [Hard Water and Your New Bathroom in Sioux Falls](/guides/hard-water-bathroom-sioux-falls) guide goes deeper on what that means for finishes and cleaning habits.

Custom tile also allows for a true curbless or low-threshold entry, which matters a lot for accessibility. If you are planning for the long term, that is worth factoring in early. This [Sioux Falls shower remodel guide](https://siouxfallsrevamp.com/add-value-to-your-home-with-a-custom-sioux-falls-shower-remodel/) walks through how custom showers affect home value and what decisions tend to matter most.

## Waterproofing Behind the Walls, the Part Nobody Sees

This is where the two options differ the most and where the most expensive surprises happen.

Prefabricated systems vary widely in what goes behind the panels. Some installers apply waterproofing membrane behind the panels, others do not. If you ever get a leak at a seam or behind the base, it can travel into the wall or the floor before you notice anything. Sioux Falls homeowners have found rot and damaged subfloor after a repair that started as cracked grout or a loose caulk line.

With custom tile, a reputable remodeler applies a continuous waterproofing membrane to the walls and floor before any tile goes in. That membrane is what keeps water where it belongs. Ask any remodeler, prefab or tile, to show you in writing what waterproofing method they use, and what they will do if they find damaged subfloor or framing once the tub is out.

If a shower leaks into the basement or the ceiling of the room below, the damage spreads fast. The article [What Remodelers Find Under Old Bathroom Tile](/guides/hidden-water-damage-bathroom) covers the warning signs and what to expect if hidden damage turns up during demo.

Key waterproofing questions to ask any installer:

- What membrane or waterproofing product do you use, and where exactly does it go?
- Is the shower pan or base a separate product, and how does it seal to the wall system?
- Do you flood-test the pan before tiling?
- What happens if water damage turns up during demo, and how are change orders priced?

## The Whirlpool Tub Question and Resale Reality

A lot of Sioux Falls homes from the 1990s and early 2000s have a large whirlpool or jetted tub in the master bath that has not been used in years. It takes up most of the room, it costs money to heat the water for a full fill, and the jets need to be cleaned regularly to prevent mold growth inside the lines.

Replacing an unused whirlpool with a walk-in shower is one of the most common reasons homeowners call a remodeler. The tub footprint is usually large enough to build a genuinely spacious shower, often with a bench and a niche.

The resale question is worth thinking through honestly. The old advice was that every home needed at least one tub to appeal to buyers with young children. That is still a factor in some markets, but it is less absolute than it used to be. If your home has a second full bath with a tub, converting the master whirlpool to a shower is generally considered a reasonable trade. If the master bath is the only bathing option in the house, talk to a local real estate agent before you remove the only tub.

If resale is a concern:

- Ask a local real estate agent how comparable homes in your neighborhood (McKennan Park, All Saints, or wherever you are) have sold with or without a master tub.
- Look at whether a shower-only master bath has affected days on market in your price range.
- Consider whether a freestanding soaking tub in the same space gives you the best of both.

## Permits, Licensing, and What the City of Sioux Falls Requires

A tub to shower conversion is not just cosmetic. If the drain location moves, that is plumbing work. If you are adding a vent fan or new lighting, that is electrical work. Both require licensed contractors and separate permits in Sioux Falls.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Services, a building permit does not cover electrical or plumbing work unless licensed contractors perform that work. The City requires a Residential Building Contractor's License for anyone working on one and two family homes in city limits, and plumbing contractor licenses from other states are not accepted. The City publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list you can check before hiring.

If your home is outside city limits in Minnehaha County, permits and inspections go through the county's Planning and Zoning office instead.

Before you sign any contract, ask your installer or remodeler:

- Are you licensed with the City of Sioux Falls for this type of work?
- Will you pull the building, plumbing, and electrical permits, or am I responsible for those?
- Will the work be inspected before walls are closed up?

Unpermitted work can create problems when you sell. A buyer's inspector may flag it, and you may have to bring it up to code at that point, often at higher cost than doing it right the first time.
