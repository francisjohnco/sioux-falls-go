Water damage moves fast. Mold can begin forming within 24 to 48 hours of a wet event, and the company you call in those first hours will be in your home for days, handling your insurance claim, and making decisions that affect what your house looks like for years. Getting that choice right matters more than most homeowners realize until they're in the middle of it.

Before you read further, the [Water Damage in Your Sioux Falls Home, What to Do](/guides/water-damage-guide-sioux-falls) guide covers the full picture from start to finish. This article focuses on one piece of that: how to find and vet a water damage restoration company, what to expect once you hire one, and what to do if things go wrong.

## What to Look for Before You Hire a Restoration Company in Sioux Falls

Credentials are the first thing to check, and they are not all equal.

The industry benchmark is the IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification). An IICRC-certified firm follows documented drying standards and can provide readings and logs that your insurance adjuster will recognize. Ask any company you're considering whether they hold current IICRC certification, not just whether a technician took a class years ago.

Licensing matters too. South Dakota does not issue a single statewide "restoration contractor" license, but companies doing structural repairs need to follow City of Sioux Falls Building Services permit requirements. Ask whether the company will pull permits for any rebuild work and who is responsible for inspections.

Questions to ask before you sign anything:

- Are you IICRC certified, and can you show documentation?
- Do you carry general liability and workers' compensation insurance?
- Who specifically will be on-site, and are they your employees or subcontractors?
- Will you handle the insurance paperwork directly with my adjuster?
- What does your contract say about scope changes and supplemental billing?

Do not let urgency pressure you into skipping these questions. A reputable company will answer them without hesitation.

## Response Time and What "24/7" Actually Means

Sioux Falls homeowners whose basements flooded have consistently described crews arriving within hours as one of the most important parts of a good experience. A company advertising 24/7 emergency service should be able to tell you, right now on the phone, how long before a crew with equipment arrives at your address.

Fast response is not just about comfort. Every hour of standing water increases the chance of structural damage and mold growth. Equipment needs to be on-site, running, and monitored within the first day to meet professional drying standards.

When you call, ask directly:

- What is your current estimated response time for my zip code?
- Will you send a crew with drying equipment tonight, or just an estimator?
- Who do I call if something changes overnight?

If the answer is vague or they say someone will "be in touch," keep calling.

## Working With Your Insurance Adjuster

One of the most common things Sioux Falls homeowners say they wanted after a water event was someone who could talk to the insurance adjuster so they did not have to manage every conversation themselves. Many restoration companies offer exactly this, but the quality varies.

A good company will document everything the adjuster needs: moisture readings at multiple depths, equipment logs showing hours of operation, photographs at each stage of drying, and a written scope of work before repairs begin. This documentation is what gets your claim paid accurately. Without it, you may face disputes over what was damaged or whether drying was thorough enough.

The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to keep their own log of every contact with the insurer, ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer, and not make permanent repairs before the insurer has seen the damage. That last point matters: a restoration company that starts tearing out and rebuilding before your adjuster has documented the loss can create real problems for your claim.

For a full look at navigating the claims process, [Water Damage Insurance Claims, What Homeowners Should Know](/guides/water-damage-insurance-claim) walks through documentation, coverage questions, and your rights under South Dakota law.

If you have questions or a dispute, the SD Division of Insurance can be reached at 605-773-3563.

## Drying Documentation and Why It Protects You

Professional drying is not just running fans and coming back in a week. It involves daily moisture readings taken at the wall cavity, subfloor, and framing levels, with those readings logged and time-stamped. The drying is complete when materials reach their target moisture content, not when things feel dry to the touch.

Ask the company to explain their documentation process before work begins:

- How often will you take readings, and will I see the logs?
- What equipment will be in my home, and how will you track it?
- How will I know when drying is officially complete?
- Will I receive a final moisture report I can keep for my records?

This documentation matters for more than the current claim. If you sell your home later, a buyer's inspector may ask whether the damage was properly dried and documented. Having the logs is a real asset.

For a detailed explanation of what the drying process looks like inside your walls and floors, [How Professional Drying Works After Water Damage](/guides/drying-process-explained) covers the equipment, the timeline, and what good versus insufficient drying looks like.

## Communication: What Good Looks Like and What to Watch For

Sioux Falls homeowners who had good experiences consistently described one thing: they were kept informed at every stage. They knew what was happening, why, and what came next. Homeowners who had bad experiences described the opposite: not knowing who was in their house, finding out about scope changes after the fact, and struggling to get anyone on the phone.

Before work begins, ask the company to tell you exactly how they communicate:

- Who is my single point of contact for questions?
- How will you notify me before entering my home each day?
- How quickly will you return calls or texts?
- Will I be told about any changes to scope or cost before they happen?

A company that cannot answer these questions clearly before they start work will not suddenly become communicative once your house is torn apart.

## Red Flags and What to Do If You Need to Switch Companies

Some situations call for switching mid-project. Homeowners have described starting with one company and realizing too late that the crew was not certified, the work was not being documented, or the company was billing the insurer for equipment that was not actually on-site.

Red flags worth taking seriously:

- Pressure to sign immediately, before you've had time to read the contract
- No written scope of work before demolition begins
- Equipment left unmonitored for days with no readings taken
- Requests that you sign away your insurance rights (called an Assignment of Benefits)
- Invoices sent directly to your insurer without your review
- Sudden scope expansions you hear about after the fact

If you are in a bad situation mid-project, you are not locked in without recourse. Review your contract for cancellation terms. Contact your insurance adjuster to explain the situation before making any changes. Document what has and has not been done with photographs and your own written notes. The SD Division of Insurance (605-773-3563) can advise you on your rights if a dispute involves your claim.

Switching companies mid-job is complicated, but it is sometimes the right call. The most important thing is to stop further damage from happening, document the current state thoroughly, and get a new written scope before any new crew begins work.
