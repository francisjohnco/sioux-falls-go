Sioux Falls plumbing has a few quirks you won't read about in a generic homeowner's guide. The Big Sioux Aquifer delivers some of the hardest water in the region, winters can freeze an exposed pipe in hours, and the City requires licensed contractors for most plumbing work inside city limits. This guide pulls everything together so you know what to watch for, when to call a plumber, and what to ask when you do.

## What Makes Sioux Falls Plumbing Different

The water here is hard. According to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report, total hardness runs about 15 grains per gallon for water treated at the City's own plant, and about 10 grains per gallon for Lewis and Clark water. Which source feeds your home, and exactly how hard your water is, can vary by neighborhood and even by street.

That hardness matters because mineral scale builds up inside pipes, water heaters, and fixtures over time. A water heater that might last 12 years in a soft-water city can underperform faster here if sediment is left unmanaged.

Then there is the winter. Sioux Falls gets plenty of snow every winter, and temperatures can drop far enough to freeze pipes inside exterior walls, in crawl spaces, or in garages. The freeze-thaw cycle also works on the expansive clay soil under older neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints, which can shift and stress underground lines over decades.

Understanding those two forces, hard water and hard winters, explains most of the plumbing problems Sioux Falls homeowners actually face.

## Plumbing Emergencies: What Counts as One and What to Do First

Not every problem needs a midnight call, but some do. A burst pipe, water actively flooding a space, sewage backing up into the basement, or a gas smell near a water heater are genuine emergencies. Know where your main shutoff is before something goes wrong, because finding it in a panic adds minutes you don't have.

Sioux Falls homeowners have described water backing up all the way into their basements from tree roots in the main sewer line. That is an emergency. A slow drain in one sink usually is not.

When you do call after hours, ask upfront what the after-hours rate is and whether it is a flat fee or an hourly rate with a minimum. Getting that answer before the technician arrives avoids a surprise on the invoice.

## Water Heaters: Repair, Replace, and Permits

Water heater failures have a way of happening at night or on a Sunday. When yours starts leaking, making popping sounds from sediment buildup, or simply stops producing hot water, the first question is whether repair or full replacement makes more sense.

Sioux Falls homeowners have been told a heater was dead when a second opinion found a small, fixable part. That happens. Age, the nature of the problem, and whether the unit is leaking from the tank itself all factor into that call.

One thing many homeowners skip: according to KELO News, citing the City of Sioux Falls permitting support manager, water heater change-outs are among the projects that require a city permit, and that permit step often gets missed. Pulling the permit protects you at sale and ensures the installation is inspected.

## Frozen Pipes in Winter

An exposed pipe in an exterior wall, a crawl space without adequate insulation, or a garage with plumbing can freeze when temperatures drop fast. The danger is not just a blocked pipe, it is the pressure buildup that can split the pipe and release water into the wall or ceiling after the thaw.

If you suspect a frozen pipe, the most important first step is knowing where your main water shutoff is and being ready to use it. Opening the faucet on the affected line relieves pressure as the pipe thaws.

What you use to thaw matters. A hair dryer on a low setting, applied from the faucet end toward the frozen section, is one of the safer methods. Open flame of any kind near a pipe is not.

## Hidden Leaks: The Problem You Don't Know You Have

A hidden leak can run for weeks or months inside a wall, under a slab, or in a ceiling before the homeowner sees any visible damage. By then, the repair usually involves drywall, insulation, and sometimes mold remediation on top of the plumbing fix.

Sioux Falls homeowners have described leaks that were genuinely hard to locate until a technician used the right equipment to trace the source. Thermal imaging and acoustic listening tools are now common in a good plumber's kit.

You can do an early check yourself with the meter test:

- Turn off every water source in the home, including icemakers and irrigation.
- Note the reading on your water meter.
- Wait 30 minutes without using any water.
- If the meter has moved, water is leaving the system somewhere.

Other signs to watch for:

- An unexplained jump in your water bill
- A musty smell in a room without obvious moisture
- Warm spots on a floor (which can mean a hot-water line leak)
- Staining on ceilings or baseboards that comes back after you paint over it

The earlier a hidden leak is found, the less damage it causes. The guide [Hidden Plumbing Leaks and How They Get Found](/guides/hidden-plumbing-leaks) explains what warning signs to watch for, how plumbers trace the source, and why waiting costs more than acting early.

## Hard Water and What It Actually Does to Your Plumbing

Fifteen grains per gallon is considered very hard water by any measure. At that level, scale accumulates inside water heater tanks, coats fixture aerators and showerheads, and reduces the efficiency of appliances connected to the water supply.

A water softener can address most of that, but sizing and installing one correctly matters. Too small a unit will not keep up with your household's use; too large a unit wastes salt and water on regeneration cycles that run more often than necessary.

A few things a water softener does not fix:

- Water that already smells or tastes off for other chemical reasons
- Existing scale already built up inside pipes (that requires descaling or replacement)
- Corrosion caused by other water chemistry factors

Ask your plumber to test your specific water hardness before recommending a softener size, because, as the City's report notes, hardness varies by which treatment source feeds your address.

## Plumber or Drain Cleaner: Knowing Which Call to Make

This is one of the most practical decisions a homeowner faces, and getting it wrong costs money in both directions. Calling a licensed plumber for a simple clog you could have cleared for less is one problem. Calling a drain company for a broken pipe that needs a repair permit is another, and it can create liability issues if the work is done without proper licensing.

Simple clogs and slow drains are almost always a drain cleaning company call, not a plumbing call. Routine main line cleaning to clear roots before they back up is another example where drain cleaning is the right, and usually less costly, service.

You need a licensed plumber when:

- The camera shows a broken, collapsed, or badly offset pipe
- You have a leak anywhere in the system
- The job involves a water heater, gas line, or anything requiring a city permit
- A frozen or burst pipe is involved

One important note from the City of Sioux Falls: plumbing contractor licenses issued by other states are not accepted within city limits. If a drain company tells you they can handle a pipe repair, ask whether they hold a City of Sioux Falls plumbing contractor license. You can verify any contractor on the City's currently licensed contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.

## Permits and Licensed Plumbers in Sioux Falls

The City of Sioux Falls requires a license for any contractor doing plumbing work within city limits. That license is specific to Sioux Falls: out-of-state licenses are not accepted.

Before you hire anyone for a plumbing job that is more than a minor repair, verify the contractor on the City's published list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors. That list is current and public.

If you own and live in a single-family home, the City does allow you to do your own plumbing under a homeowner plumbing permit. But you are then responsible for code compliance and for scheduling the required inspections. For anything beyond simple fixture swaps, most homeowners find that hiring a licensed plumber and having the work inspected is the lower-risk path, especially when it comes time to sell the home.

Skipping a permit on a water heater, a sewer line repair, or a remodel that moves plumbing can create real problems at closing. Buyers' inspectors and lenders sometimes ask about permit history, and unpermitted work can require corrective action after the fact.

## How to Choose a Plumber in Sioux Falls

The gap between quotes for the same job can be significant. Sioux Falls homeowners have been genuinely surprised by how far apart two estimates can land for identical work. That spread is not always a sign that someone is overcharging; it can reflect differences in materials, warranty terms, or how thoroughly the technician diagnosed the problem.

A few things worth asking before you agree to any work:

- Are you licensed with the City of Sioux Falls? (Verify at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.)
- Will you pull the required permit, and is that included in the quote?
- What exactly is included in this price, and what could change it?
- Can you explain the problem and show me what you found before you start?

Homeowners consistently report that the technicians they trusted most were the ones who took time to explain what they found and what the options were before picking up a tool. That is a reasonable standard to hold any plumber to.
