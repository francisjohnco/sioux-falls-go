A Sioux Falls snow alert is not just a weather advisory. According to the City of Sioux Falls, it means the City is actively planning to plow all streets, and vehicles left on those streets can be ticketed or towed. If you are not sure where to move your car, or even where to look for the alert, this article walks through exactly what to do before, during, and after a storm. For everything else about managing snow at your home, start with [Snow Removal in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/snow-removal-guide-sioux-falls).

## What a Sioux Falls Snow Alert Actually Means

A snow alert is a formal declaration by the City, not a suggestion. When the City issues one, it triggers specific parking rules across public streets.

The purpose is practical. City plows need to run curb to curb on every street, and parked vehicles block the route and leave strips of packed snow that cause problems for weeks.

What the alert does not mean:

- Your alley is automatically included in the same restrictions
- Private parking lots have the same rules (they do not, those are managed by the property owner)
- The alert applies only to major roads (it covers all City streets)

The alert can be issued before snow begins, once accumulation is forecast. That means you may need to move your car when the streets still look dry.

## The Snow Alert Tracker and How to Use It

The City of Sioux Falls runs a Snow Alert Tracker that shows zone maps and plow progress in real time. This is the most useful tool most residents never bookmark.

You can see which zones have been plowed, which are in progress, and which have not been touched yet. This matters if you are waiting to know when it is safe to park on your street again.

Where to find it:

- Go to the City of Sioux Falls website and search "Snow Alert Tracker"
- Bookmark the page before winter, not during the first storm when everyone is searching at once
- The City also posts updates on its official social media channels during active alerts

Do not rely on a neighbor's text or a Facebook post to know when the alert is lifted. Check the tracker or the City's official channels directly, because zones clear at different times.

## Ticketing, Towing, and What It Costs You

The City can ticket and tow vehicles left on City streets during a snow alert, according to the City of Sioux Falls. Both can happen, not just one or the other.

Towing during a snow alert means you will pay towing fees and storage costs to retrieve your vehicle, on top of any ticket. Those fees are set by the towing company under City contract and are not capped at a small amount.

A few things residents get wrong:

- Assuming a light snow means the alert will not be enforced (it can be)
- Thinking parking on a side street in a quiet neighborhood is fine (all City streets are included)
- Moving the car to the other side of the street to satisfy alternate-side rules (during a snow alert, both sides are off limits)

If you are unsure whether an alert is active, the safest move is to get the car off the street entirely until the tracker shows your zone is complete.

## Where to Actually Park During an Alert

This is the question most people have when they first hear an alert has been declared. Your options depend on what your property has.

If you have a driveway or garage, use it. Pull as far in as your space allows so the plow can clear the street edge near your curb cut without obstruction.

If you do not have off-street parking:

- Ask a neighbor with a driveway if you can park there temporarily
- Look for a City parking ramp or lot (many are free overnight, check the City's parking page for current rules)
- A church or business lot nearby may allow overnight parking during storms, but ask first rather than assume

One thing to avoid: do not park on the sidewalk or on your lawn in a way that blocks a curb ramp. City ordinance requires those ramps to stay accessible, and you do not want to create a separate problem while solving a parking one.

## Planning Ahead So an Alert Does Not Catch You Off Guard

The homeowners who have the least stress during a snow alert are the ones who made a plan in September, not the ones scrambling at 10 p.m. when the declaration comes through.

A few things worth doing before the season starts:

- Bookmark the Snow Alert Tracker now
- Sign up for City of Sioux Falls emergency alerts or Notify Me notifications if the City offers them in your area
- Talk to neighbors about sharing driveway space during alerts if either of you lacks off-street parking
- If you have a service provider for snow removal, confirm in writing whether their contract covers plowing you out after the City plow comes through and pushes snow back across your driveway apron

That last point matters more than most people expect. A City plow pass often leaves a ridge of packed snow across the bottom of every driveway on the block. Knowing whether your contractor comes back for that, or whether you are on your own, is worth a conversation before the first storm. See [What a Good Snow Removal Contract Says](/guides/snow-removal-contract-checklist) for the specific questions to put in writing.

Snow alert nights also tend to be when sidewalk obligations come into focus. The City requires sidewalks to be cleared within 48 hours of when snow stops falling, and Dakota News Now, as reported by KSOO in 2025, noted that an uncleared sidewalk can bring a $100 penalty and the City can hire a removal company at the owner's cost. If a big storm coincides with an alert, your sidewalk clock starts ticking the moment snow stops. The full breakdown of that rule is in [The 48-Hour Sidewalk Rule, Explained](/guides/sidewalk-snow-48-hours).
