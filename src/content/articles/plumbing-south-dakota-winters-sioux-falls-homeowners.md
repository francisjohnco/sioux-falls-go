Sioux Falls winters aren't just cold — they're the kind of cold that turns a minor plumbing oversight into a genuine emergency overnight. The National Weather Service's Sioux Falls office regularly logs January lows in the mid-teens, and a hard freeze that lasts multiple days is what actually causes pipes to fail, not a single cold night.

## Why the frost line matters more here than you'd think

The City of Sioux Falls requires water lines to be buried at least 42 inches deep — a local building code amendment specifically because of how deep the ground actually freezes here in a typical winter. Any exposed or shallow-buried line, especially in an unheated garage, a crawlspace, or an exterior wall on the north side of a house, is at real risk once temperatures drop and stay down.

## The three things that actually prevent a frozen pipe

1. **Disconnect and drain exterior hoses before the first hard freeze**, typically by early-to-mid November. A hose left attached can trap water in the spigot line and cause it to split, even on a "frost-free" spigot.
2. **Know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it.** In an actual freeze emergency, the few minutes it takes to find the shutoff valve for the first time is the difference between a wet floor and a flooded basement.
3. **Keep a trickle of water running on the coldest nights** through any faucet on an exterior wall or above an unheated space — moving water is far less likely to freeze than standing water.

## What to actually do if a pipe freezes

Turn off the main water supply first, then apply gentle heat (a hair dryer or a space heater at a safe distance, never an open flame) to the suspected frozen section, working from the faucet end toward the blockage. If you can't find the frozen section or the pipe has already split, that's the point to call a plumber rather than keep troubleshooting — a burst pipe with the water still on can do thousands of dollars in damage in the time it takes to figure that out yourself.

## The other side of winter: spring thaw

The same freeze-thaw cycle that threatens pipes in January can also stress older sewer lines and foundations as the ground shifts through repeated freezing and thawing — expansive clay soil around Sioux Falls swells when wet and contracts when dry, and a sewer line that was fine in November can develop a new problem by March. If you notice slow drains or unusual sounds from your plumbing right around thaw season, it's worth having it looked at before it becomes a bigger job.
