Sioux Falls homes go through a real humidity swing every year — dry winter air from constant indoor heating, followed by a more humid summer — and that swing has real consequences for certain flooring materials.

## Why solid hardwood shows seasonal changes here

Solid hardwood expands when humidity is higher and contracts when air is dry, and Sioux Falls's winter heating season creates genuinely low indoor humidity for months at a time. Small gaps appearing between hardwood boards in January or February are a normal seasonal response, not usually a sign of a problem — most gaps close back up as summer humidity returns.

## What actually helps

- **Running a humidifier during heating season**, targeting 30-50% relative humidity, reduces the extremity of the seasonal gapping.
- **Avoiding placing hardwood directly next to heating vents**, where the localized dryness is most extreme.
- **Choosing engineered hardwood over solid** for rooms where humidity control is inconsistent, since engineered products are more dimensionally stable.

## Basements need different consideration

Below-grade spaces in Sioux Falls see different moisture patterns than above-grade rooms — groundwater levels shift seasonally, and a basement that feels dry in winter can see more moisture pressure in spring thaw. This is the main reason vinyl plank or tile are generally recommended over solid hardwood for basement flooring here, regardless of the aesthetic preference.

## If you're installing new flooring, timing can help

Installing hardwood flooring during a moderate-humidity season (spring or fall) rather than the depth of winter or summer gives the material a chance to acclimate closer to its long-term average condition, which can reduce how dramatic the seasonal movement looks in year one.
