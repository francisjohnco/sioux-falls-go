A replacement window warranty sounds reassuring until something goes wrong and you find out the problem you have is not the problem the warranty covers. Sioux Falls homeowners have dealt with fogged-up glass, windows that stick or won't lock, and frames that let cold air seep through, only to learn that their warranty language pointed somewhere else entirely. Before you buy, it helps to understand exactly what is promised and by whom. For the full picture of the replacement process, start with [Replacing Windows in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/window-replacement-guide-sioux-falls).

## The Two Warranties on Every Window Job

When you replace a window, you get at least two separate warranties, and they almost never cover the same things.

The first is the **manufacturer's warranty**, which covers the window itself: the frame, the hardware, the glass unit, and sometimes the finish or coating.

The second is the **installer's warranty**, which covers the labor and the work done to fit the window into your home. If the window leaks air because it was not shimmed and sealed correctly, that is an installation problem, not a product defect.

These two warranties are issued by two different parties. If something goes wrong, figuring out whose responsibility it is can take time, and both sides may point at the other. Getting both warranties in writing before the job starts is not optional.

## What the Manufacturer's Warranty Usually Covers

Most replacement window warranties are **limited lifetime warranties**, but "lifetime" often means the life of the original owner, not the house. Read that definition carefully.

Manufacturers typically cover:

- Glass seal failure (the fog or condensation that appears between panes)
- Defects in the frame or sash material
- Hardware defects, including locks and balance systems
- Finish fading or peeling, often for a shorter period than the glass

What they typically do not cover:

- Damage from improper installation
- Condensation on the inside or outside surface of the glass (that is a humidity or ventilation issue, not a product failure)
- Screens
- Damage from impact, storms, or homeowner misuse
- Normal wear over time

The fogged-glass problem that Sioux Falls homeowners mention often is usually a failed seal on the insulated glass unit. That is typically a covered defect, but the manufacturer will want documentation that the window was installed correctly, which loops right back to the installation warranty.

## Glass Seal Coverage, the Detail That Matters Most Here

In a cold climate, the insulated glass unit works hard. The gas between the panes (usually argon) expands and contracts with temperature swings, and over many years seals can fail. When they do, moisture gets in and you see that cloudy, foggy look that never wipes away.

Most manufacturers cover seal failure, but the terms vary considerably:

- Some cover full replacement of the glass unit at no charge for a set number of years, then pro-rate the coverage after that.
- Some cover parts but not labor to reinstall.
- Some require that the original installer document the installation method in order to honor the claim.

Ask the installer specifically: "If the seal fails in year five, what does the manufacturer cover, and what would I pay out of pocket?" Get the answer in writing, not just verbally.

## Installation Warranties and Why They Are Often Shorter

Installer warranties are usually shorter than manufacturer warranties, sometimes one year, sometimes five, and they vary widely by contractor. That gap matters because installation problems often do not show up immediately.

A window that is not properly shimmed, flashed, or insulated around the frame may pass a quick visual check but show drafts or moisture intrusion after the first winter. Some Sioux Falls homeowners have described feeling like their new windows performed worse than the originals, which almost always points to installation, not the window itself.

Ask every contractor these questions before signing:

- How long does your installation warranty last?
- Does it cover air leakage and water infiltration, or only visible defects?
- If I have a problem, do I call you or the manufacturer first?
- Is that warranty in writing as part of the contract?

One thing worth knowing: according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, window and exterior door replacement requires a building permit in Sioux Falls. A permit means an inspection, and a passed inspection creates a record that the work met code at the time. That documentation can matter if you ever need to make a warranty claim or sell the home. You can confirm current requirements with City Building Services and check licensed contractors at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.

## Transferability and What Happens When You Sell

If you plan to sell your home, the transferability of your window warranty is worth asking about now, not at closing.

Some manufacturers allow the warranty to transfer to a new owner, sometimes automatically, sometimes for a fee. Others do not transfer at all. An installer's labor warranty almost never transfers.

A transferable warranty can be a selling point, but only if the paperwork is in order. Buyers and their agents will ask for it, and a warranty that exists on paper but was never registered may not be honored.

Ask before you buy:

- Is this warranty transferable to a future owner?
- Is there a fee or a time limit on the transfer?
- What documentation does the new owner need?

## Registration, Paperwork, and What to Get in Writing

Many manufacturer warranties require registration within a set number of days after installation. Miss that window and you may lose coverage entirely or fall back to a shorter default warranty.

Here is a simple checklist of what to collect and keep:

- The signed contract, with both the manufacturer warranty terms and the installer warranty terms spelled out
- The building permit (required by the City of Sioux Falls for window replacement)
- The window brand, model, and series for each opening replaced
- Confirmation of warranty registration with the manufacturer, with a registration number if one is issued
- The installer's license number (the City publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors)
- Photos of the completed installation before interior trim is replaced, especially around the flashing and rough opening

Keep these together somewhere you can find them. If you ever have a window that will not lock, a seal that fails, or a frame that feels cold to the touch on a January night, that documentation is what gets a claim processed.
