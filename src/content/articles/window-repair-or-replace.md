A window problem is not always a window replacement problem. Some issues have a repair that costs a fraction of new glass, and some issues look minor but mean the whole unit has already failed. Knowing the difference saves real money and keeps you from buying something you did not need. This guide walks through the most common complaints Sioux Falls homeowners bring up and what each one actually signals. For a broader look at the whole process, start with [Replacing Windows in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/window-replacement-guide-sioux-falls).

## Fog Between the Panes, What It Actually Means

Fog or haze trapped between two panes of glass is one of the clearest signs of a failed seal. The space between the panes holds a gas (usually argon or krypton) that improves insulation. When the seal breaks, moisture works in and you see the condensation, often worst on cold mornings.

This is not a surface you can wipe away. The fogged unit is done, and no amount of cleaning fixes a failed seal.

The question is whether the failed unit is the whole window or just the glass. Many windows are built so the glass unit (called an insulated glass unit or IGU) can be replaced on its own without pulling the frame. If the frame is structurally sound, square, and not rotting, an IGU swap can restore the window without a full replacement.

Ask the contractor these questions:

- Is the frame still square and tight to the rough opening?
- Is the IGU available for this make and model, or is the window too old to source parts?
- Will replacing the glass unit bring the window up to current insulation ratings?

If the frame is older and the parts are hard to find, full replacement may cost only a little more and gives you a window that will not fail again in two years.

For more on how fogged glass connects to the broader condensation picture, [Window Condensation in Winter, When to Worry](/guides/window-condensation-winter) covers the difference between a failed seal, indoor humidity issues, and outside dew.

## Drafts and Cold Air, Repair or Replace?

Drafts are the complaint Sioux Falls homeowners mention most. With winters that push well below zero and a frost line that goes 42 inches into the ground, a leaky window is not a small comfort problem.

But a draft is not always a window problem. Before anything else, check:

- The weatherstripping around the sash. It compresses over time and can be replaced inexpensively.
- The caulk line between the frame and the exterior trim. Sioux Falls freeze-thaw cycles work caulk loose every few years.
- The interior stops and the gap between the frame and the wall. Poor original installation leaves gaps that air moves through freely.

If weatherstripping and caulk are fresh and the draft persists, the frame itself may have warped or the original installation may have never been properly sealed. At that point, a repair technician can sometimes add low-expansion foam behind the interior casing, but only if there is access.

If the window is an older single-pane or early double-pane unit with a low insulating value, a repair may stop the air movement but leave you with a glass surface that still radiates cold into the room. That is a physics problem, not a weatherstripping problem. ENERGY STAR Version 7.0 (effective October 23, 2023) sets a U-factor of 0.22 or lower for the Northern climate zone. If your window is nowhere near that rating, no repair changes what the glass itself lets through.

A new frame will not fix a draft caused by bad installation, either. Local homeowners have described new windows that were still drafty because the installer did not properly seal the rough opening. That is an installation failure, not a product failure. When you get quotes, ask specifically how the installer seals the gap between the new frame and the rough opening, and get it in writing. [Why Window Quotes Differ and How to Compare Them](/guides/comparing-window-quotes) covers exactly what to ask on that point.

## Broken Locks, Stiff Sashes, and Hardware That Will Not Move

A window that will not lock is a security and ventilation problem, but it is usually a repair, not a replacement. Hardware wears out. Locks, sash lifts, and tilt latches are replaceable parts on most window brands. A window technician can often fix a failed lock in a single visit.

Sash balance failure is different. If a double-hung sash drops or will not stay up, the balance mechanism (a spring or block-and-tackle system inside the frame) has likely broken. Many frames allow balance replacement without replacing the whole window.

What makes a window hard to open and close:

- Swollen wood frames from moisture (see the rot section below)
- Paint that has been applied over the sash joint and dried it shut
- Failed or misaligned balances
- A frame that has racked out of square because the house has settled

A racked frame is worth paying attention to. If the opening itself has moved, a new window installed into the same opening will eventually show the same problem. Ask whether the rough opening is still square before deciding between repair and replacement.

## Wood Rot, the Line Between Repair and Replacement

Rot is the signal that tends to push a repair conversation toward replacement. Wood rot means moisture has been getting past the paint or sealant long enough to break down the wood fibers. Soft spots, discoloration, and paint that peels in sheets are all signs.

Small surface rot (less than a quarter of the frame, dry to the probe, not reaching the sill pan) can sometimes be treated with epoxy consolidant and filler. It is not a permanent fix but it can extend life by several years if the moisture source is corrected at the same time.

Rot that has reached the sill pan, the rough sill underneath, or the structural framing around the opening is a different situation. At that point you are looking at a full-frame replacement and likely some carpentry on the wall framing itself. That is structural work, and according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, structural changes require a building permit and a licensed Residential Building Contractor, not just a window and door license. You can verify current license types at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.

Sioux Falls homes with wood windows that date to the 1950s and 1960s, particularly in older neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints, often have multiple layers of paint and long deferred maintenance. Getting a contractor to probe the frame with a sharp tool at the corners and sill before you decide anything is worth the time.

## When Replacement Is Simply the Better Money

Sometimes a repair is technically possible but replacement makes more financial sense. Consider replacement when:

- The window is a single-pane unit. No repair changes the insulation value of one pane of glass.
- The seal has failed more than once on the same unit. It will fail again.
- Hardware is discontinued and parts cannot be sourced.
- The frame is out of square, the rot is deep, or the original install was never done properly.
- You are already doing related work (siding, trim, interior remodel) that gives an installer easy access.

Replacement is not automatically the answer either. A poorly done replacement can leave you with worse performance than your old window. That is not a hypothetical complaint: local homeowners have described feeling better about their original windows after a poor install introduced new drafts and noise. The window product matters, but so does the installation.

Whatever you decide, get a written quote that describes specifically what is being done, how the gap between the frame and wall will be sealed, and what warranty covers both the product and the labor separately.
