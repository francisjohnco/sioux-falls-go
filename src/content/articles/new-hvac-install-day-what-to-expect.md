Getting a new furnace or air conditioner is one of the bigger home investments you'll make, and most homeowners have no idea what actually happens on installation day until the crew shows up. Knowing the sequence, what to watch for, and what questions to ask makes the whole thing less stressful and helps you catch problems early. This article walks through the full day, from prep the night before to the paperwork you need to keep. For the broader picture on equipment choices, sizing, and costs, start with [Heating and Cooling in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/hvac-guide-sioux-falls).

## Getting Ready the Night Before

The biggest thing you can do before the crew arrives is clear a path.

Move anything stored near the furnace, in the utility room, or in front of the air handler. The crew will need to bring out the old unit and bring in the new one, and tight corners slow everyone down.

If the job involves the outdoor AC condenser, make sure the area around it is accessible. Move patio furniture, storage, or anything else within a few feet of the unit.

Also check these before the morning:

- Confirm the appointment window and ask how many technicians will be on site
- Ask whether they'll be pulling the mechanical permit that day or if it was already filed
- Find out what they need from you during the job (gas shutoff location, thermostat access, electrical panel)
- Make a plan for pets and kids, since the crew will be moving in and out for hours

A quick note on permits: according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, heating and cooling work requires a separate mechanical permit, pulled by a licensed mechanical contractor. It is not covered by a general building permit. You can verify your contractor's license at 605-367-8672 or on the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list. If your contractor says no permit is needed for a full replacement, that is worth questioning directly.

## How Long the Job Takes

Most full furnace replacements take three to five hours. A combined furnace and AC replacement, especially if any ductwork or venting needs to change, can run a full day.

A few things that stretch the timeline:

- Venting changes (older homes in neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints sometimes have venting that doesn't work with high-efficiency equipment)
- New electrical circuits for a heat pump or high-efficiency unit (ask in advance if the electrician is a separate crew coming later)
- Ductwork modifications or new connections at the plenum
- Unexpected issues found once the old unit comes out, like corroded flue connections or damaged duct sections

Ask your contractor for a realistic window before the job starts, not just "half a day." If they discover additional work is needed, they should walk you through it and give you a revised estimate before proceeding, not just bill you for it at the end.

## Floor Protection and Jobsite Cleanliness

This is a real concern for homeowners. Reviews from Sioux Falls residents specifically mention caring about boot covers and floor protection, and also about sticky residue left behind when floor protection is removed poorly.

A professional crew should:

- Lay runners or protective film from the entry door through to the work area
- Wear boot covers or swap to clean footwear when moving through finished areas
- Remove old equipment without dragging it across hardwood or tile

Before the crew starts, ask what they use to protect floors and stairs. If you have hardwood near the utility room, mention it and show them the path they'll be using.

After the job, do a walkthrough before they leave and check for adhesive residue, scuffs on walls from moving equipment, or debris in the utility area. It is much easier to get this addressed on the spot than to follow up later.

## Permits, Inspection, and What Happens Next

The mechanical permit is not just paperwork. It triggers an inspection by the City of Sioux Falls, which means a licensed inspector will verify that the installation meets code, including things like proper venting, gas connections, and clearances.

The inspection typically does not happen on installation day. It is usually scheduled for a later date, sometimes within a few days to a couple of weeks depending on the city's schedule. Your contractor should handle scheduling it, but ask them directly:

- When will the inspection be scheduled?
- Do you need to be home for it?
- What happens if something needs to be corrected?

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, the city's permit site updates every Sunday, so you can look up your permit to confirm it was actually pulled. If you never see a permit associated with your address, that is a problem worth raising with your contractor right away.

Do not let any contractor skip the permit "to save time" or reduce cost. The inspection protects you, and an uninspected installation can create issues when you sell the home.

## The Startup and System Walkthrough

Once the new equipment is installed, a good technician will do a startup sequence before they leave. This is not just flipping the system on. It should include:

- Checking refrigerant pressure on a new AC (if the system is charged on site)
- Testing ignition, flame, and heat output on a new furnace
- Verifying that the thermostat communicates correctly with the new system
- Checking airflow at registers in several rooms
- Inspecting the condensate drain line on a high-efficiency furnace (these drain differently than older units)

Then comes the walkthrough with you. This is your time to ask questions, so take it seriously. Homeowners who say they valued technicians who explain options and teach them how to use the new system are describing exactly this moment.

Ask about:

- How to use any new thermostat features (especially if the thermostat was replaced)
- What the normal sounds and cycles look like for this equipment
- Filter size, location, and how often to change it (this matters more in Sioux Falls winters when the system runs constantly)
- What the blinking light codes mean on the control board
- Who to call if something seems wrong in the first few days

If a whole-home humidifier was added as part of the installation, ask for a walkthrough of that separately. For more on how those work and what maintenance they need, [Is a Whole-Home Humidifier Worth It in Sioux Falls?](/guides/whole-home-humidifier-sioux-falls) covers it in detail.

## Warranty Registration and Paperwork to Keep

Manufacturers almost always require registration to activate the full warranty, and most have a short window, often 30 to 60 days, though terms vary by brand and policy. Some contractors register the equipment on your behalf as part of the installation. Ask whether they're doing that or whether you need to do it yourself.

Either way, before the crew leaves, collect and file these documents:

- The mechanical permit number and the inspection date (or how to find it)
- Manufacturer warranty documents for the furnace and AC unit
- Any extended labor warranty from the contractor (terms vary, so read the fine print)
- The model numbers and serial numbers for all new equipment (photograph the data plates if nothing is written down)
- The written quote or contract showing what was installed and what was paid

Store these somewhere physical, not just in email. If you sell the home, buyers will want this documentation. If the equipment fails under warranty, you will need the serial number and purchase date.
