If you own property in Sioux Falls, the sidewalk in front of your home is your responsibility the moment the snow stops falling. Under City Ordinance 96.100, you have 48 hours to clear it completely, edge to edge, down to the concrete. Miss that window and you are looking at a penalty and possibly a City contractor showing up at your expense.

This article covers exactly what the rule requires, what happens when it's violated, and how to plan ahead so you're never caught scrambling. For a broader look at winter prep as a homeowner, start with the [Snow Removal in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/snow-removal-guide-sioux-falls).

## What the 48-Hour Sidewalk Rule Actually Requires in Sioux Falls

The clock starts when the snow stops, not when it starts. That matters a lot during multi-day storms, because according to City Ordinance 96.100, the 48-hour window begins at the end of snowfall, not at the first flake.

The City's standard is specific:

- Clear the full width of the sidewalk, edge to edge
- Clear down to the concrete surface, not just a path through the middle
- If your property sits on a corner lot, you are also responsible for curb ramps at the intersection

That last point catches a lot of corner-lot homeowners off guard. A curb ramp left packed with snow is not a minor detail, it's part of the same ordinance and subject to the same enforcement.

The good news about timing: according to the City of Sioux Falls, reports filed before the 48-hour mark are not investigated. So if a neighbor calls in a complaint at hour 30, nothing will happen yet. The City gives you the full window.

## What Happens If You Don't Clear It

The enforcement process has two stages, and both cost you money.

According to Dakota News Now, as reported by KSOO in 2025, an uncleared sidewalk can bring a $100 penalty. That alone is enough reason to stay on top of it.

The bigger financial hit is stage two. City Ordinance 96.103 gives the City the authority to remove snow and ice itself if the property owner doesn't comply. Under Ordinance 96.104, the City can then specially assess the cost of that removal directly to the property, meaning it shows up on your property tax bill, not as a simple invoice you can dispute.

The City does not send a warning and wait a week. Once the 48-hour window closes and a complaint is filed, the process moves quickly.

Anyone can report an uncleared sidewalk online through the City's tracking system, or by calling Property Maintenance at 605-978-6900.

## Where the Snow Has to Go (and Where It Can't)

This is where a lot of homeowners unknowingly create a second problem. You clear the sidewalk, great, but where does the snow end up?

According to the City of Sioux Falls, snow must be placed in your yard, not pushed onto sidewalks, into the street, or into the right of way. Depositing snow in the street is a separate ordinance violation.

Practically speaking, this means:

- Throw or blow snow toward your lawn, not toward the curb or the street
- Don't push your walk's snow onto a neighbor's already-cleared section
- Don't create a pile at the end of your driveway that spills into the travel lane

If you're using a snow blower, aim the chute toward your yard and watch wind direction so you aren't blowing it right back onto the sidewalk or into the street. The guide on [where to put all that snow](/guides/where-to-put-snow) covers sight lines, mailbox clearance, and drainage in more detail.

## Planning Ahead So the Rule Doesn't Catch You Off Guard

The 48-hour window sounds generous until you're at work during the storm, traveling, or dealing with a second storm that hits before you've recovered from the first.

A few situations that trip up Sioux Falls homeowners every year:

- A storm ends late Friday night and 48 hours lands on Sunday evening, when it's easy to lose track
- A second storm drops more snow at hour 40, resetting parts of the walkway you already cleared
- An older homeowner or someone with a health condition genuinely cannot shovel safely

For older homeowners or anyone who physically cannot shovel, this rule isn't just inconvenient, it's a real worry. If that describes you or someone on your block, the article on [helping neighbors who cannot shovel](/guides/helping-neighbors-snow) has practical options including community snow angel programs.

If you hire a snow removal service, confirm in writing that sidewalks are included in the scope of work, what their trigger depth is, and how quickly they return after a second storm. A service that only does driveways leaves you on the hook for the walkway, and the City does not care whose contractor skipped it.

## Corner Lots, Curb Ramps, and a Few Things Worth Double-Checking

Corner lots in older Sioux Falls neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints can have long stretches of sidewalk along two street frontages, plus one or more curb ramps at the intersection. That's a significant amount of area, and all of it falls under Ordinance 96.100.

Before the first big storm, walk the perimeter of your property and identify:

- The full length of sidewalk along each street frontage
- The location of every curb ramp your lot includes
- Any spots where ice tends to build up, low spots, areas in shade, downspout drainage paths

Curb ramps are especially important because they affect wheelchair and stroller access. Leaving them packed or icy is both an ordinance issue and a genuine hazard for your neighbors.

If you are shoveling by hand and want to protect your back during heavy clearing, the guide on [shoveling without hurting yourself](/guides/shoveling-safely) covers warm-up, lifting technique, and when to stop.

One last thing worth knowing: a snow alert is a separate City declaration about street plowing, not about sidewalk deadlines. The two systems run independently. For how snow alerts affect where you park and how to track the City's plowing progress, see the article on [snow alerts and where to park](/guides/snow-alert-parking).
