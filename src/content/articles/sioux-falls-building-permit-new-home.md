Building a new home in Sioux Falls means dealing with permits before a single shovel of dirt moves. The process is more straightforward than most people expect, but the details matter: missing a deadline, skipping a sub-permit, or assuming your county lot falls under city rules can slow your project down by weeks. This article walks through exactly how the permit process works, what it covers, and where the common mistakes happen. For the full picture of planning and building your home, start with [How to Build a Custom Home in Sioux Falls (2026 Guide)](/guides/build-custom-home-sioux-falls).

## Who Issues Building Permits in Sioux Falls

The City of Sioux Falls Building Services office handles all residential building permits for homes inside city limits. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, the office is located on the first floor of City Center at 231 N. Dakota Avenue, and you can reach them at 605-367-8670.

Your builder will almost always be the one pulling the permit. That is normal and expected: licensed residential contractors apply on behalf of the homeowner, coordinate the required inspections, and are responsible for keeping the work on schedule.

If you want to verify your builder is properly licensed before the project starts, the City of Sioux Falls maintains a Currently Licensed Contractors list online. You can also call Building Services directly at 605-367-8672. The [How to Choose a Custom Home Builder in Sioux Falls](/guides/choose-custom-home-builder-sioux-falls) guide walks through how to use that license check as part of your overall vetting process.

## What the Building Permit Covers and What Needs Separate Permits

The main building permit covers the structural work: foundation, framing, roofing, insulation, and the overall construction of the home. It does not automatically cover the trades.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, electrical, mechanical, and plumbing work each require their own separate permits, with their own fee schedules. That means a new home typically involves at minimum:

- A general building permit (structural work)
- An electrical permit
- A plumbing permit
- A mechanical permit (heating, cooling, ventilation)

Each of those permits has its own inspection sequence. Your builder and the respective subcontractors each handle the permit for their scope of work. If you are curious why the timeline has so many moving parts, the [How Long It Takes to Build a House in Sioux Falls](/guides/how-long-to-build-a-house-sioux-falls) guide maps out each phase and how inspections fit in.

## What the Application Asks For

When your builder applies for the building permit, the application requires some specific information. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, applicants must provide an approximate cost of the work, and site plans, foundation plans, and floor plans may be required as part of the application.

That means your design needs to be reasonably complete before you can apply. A rough sketch or early concept does not get you a permit. This is one reason the design and planning phase comes first, and why changes made late in the process can push your start date back.

Building permit fees, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, start at $40 and rise with the construction value according to the city's 2026 fee schedule. The electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits each have their own separate fee schedules. For actual numbers, ask Building Services or have your builder pull the current fee schedule, since these can be updated.

Before any digging begins, the City of Sioux Falls requires calling 811, which is the South Dakota One Call line, so underground utilities are marked. This is not optional, and it protects you, your workers, and your neighbors.

## The 180-Day Rule and Inspection Requirements

One of the most important deadlines in the permit process is what people usually call the 180-day rule. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, work must begin and an inspection must be requested within 180 days of the permit being issued.

If you pull a permit and then hit a delay, whether that is a financing issue, a lot problem, or a contractor schedule conflict, and you do not request an inspection within that window, your permit can expire. Getting it reinstated adds time and cost.

For most custom builds this is not a problem, because the builder coordinates the schedule with the permit timeline from the start. But if you are managing any part of the process yourself, or if something unexpected stalls the project early on, keep this deadline on your radar.

Sioux Falls building inspections happen at set stages: foundation before concrete is poured, framing before walls are closed up, rough-in inspections for each trade, and a final inspection before occupancy. Your builder schedules these, and work cannot proceed past a stage until the prior inspection is passed. The city's permit data site, which according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs updates every Sunday, lets you look up the status of any permit issued in the city.

## Sioux Falls Building Code and Why It Matters Here

Sioux Falls building code requires footings at a minimum of 42 inches below grade to get below the frost line. This is not a suggestion. The area's freeze-thaw cycles and expansive clay soils mean that footings placed too shallow will heave and crack.

Those same clay soils affect drainage and grading requirements, which are reviewed during the permit and inspection process. Getting the grade right around the foundation is part of what inspectors check, not just an aesthetic detail.

The city's code requirements are one reason a licensed, local builder matters. Someone who has built in Sioux Falls knows these standards, knows what inspectors look for, and is not learning on your project.

## How Permits Work Outside Sioux Falls City Limits

If your lot is outside the Sioux Falls city limits, Building Services at 231 N. Dakota Avenue is not your permit office. Jurisdiction depends on exactly where your property sits.

Homes in unincorporated areas of Minnehaha County go through Minnehaha County Planning. Homes in Lincoln County go through Lincoln County. And if your lot is within the limits of a suburb, the permit comes from that city's office: Brandon, Harrisburg, Tea, Crooks, and similar communities each have their own permitting process.

This matters practically because the requirements, timelines, fees, and inspection processes can differ from the city's. Some areas outside city limits also involve well and septic permitting instead of city water and sewer connections, which adds another layer.

As of spring 2026, SiouxFalls.Business reported that active developments outside city limits include River Park in Brandon (Van Buskirk Cos.) and Heritage Pond in Crooks, among others. If you are building in any of those communities, confirm the permit process directly with your builder and the relevant county or city office early.
