Garage door springs do one job: counterbalance a door that can weigh anywhere from 130 to 400 pounds so your opener (or your arms) don't have to lift it alone. When a spring fails, the door either won't move or becomes dangerous to operate. Understanding the difference between torsion and extension springs helps you have a smarter conversation with any repair company and know what questions to ask before work starts. For a full picture of garage door repair in Sioux Falls, start with [Garage Door Repair in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/garage-door-repair-sioux-falls).

## How Each Spring Type Works

Torsion springs sit horizontally above the door opening, mounted on a steel shaft that runs the width of the door. When the door closes, the spring winds up tightly, storing energy. When the door opens, it unwinds and uses that stored torque to lift the door. The energy is controlled and contained along that central shaft.

Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks on each side of the door, one spring per side. They stretch (extend) when the door closes and contract when it opens. The force they store is linear, and because they're under significant tension, they need safety cables threaded through them to contain a broken spring if it snaps.

Knowing which system you have is straightforward. Stand inside your garage with the door closed and look above the door:

- One or two coiled springs centered above the door on a metal rod: that's a torsion system.
- Two long springs running parallel to the ceiling tracks on each side: those are extension springs.

Most attached garages on homes in neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints with standard 7-foot or 8-foot doors were built with one of these two systems. Older homes and lighter single doors often got extension springs; heavier double doors and newer construction more commonly use torsion systems.

## Lifespan in Cycles, Explained Simply

Spring lifespan is measured in cycles, not years. One cycle is one complete open-and-close sequence. A standard spring is rated for about 10,000 cycles. If you use your garage door four times a day, that works out to roughly seven years before the spring reaches its rated life. High-cycle springs (20,000 to 30,000 cycles) cost more upfront but last significantly longer.

Sioux Falls winters add another variable. A National Weather Service meteorologist described the February 2021 cold snap as the longest stretch of extreme cold since about 1996, according to SiouxFalls.Business. Cold makes metal contract, and springs already near the end of their life tend to snap on the coldest mornings, right when you need the door to work. That's not coincidence; it's physics.

A spring that's been cycling for six or more years is worth discussing with a technician even if it hasn't broken yet. Replacing both springs at the same time (on a two-spring torsion system) or both extension springs together is standard practice because if one is worn, the other is close behind.

## Safety Differences Between the Two Types

This is where the comparison really matters. Torsion springs are generally considered safer for a few reasons:

- The energy is stored and released along a contained shaft, so a broken spring stays in place rather than launching across the garage.
- The controlled torque produces smoother, more balanced door movement, which puts less wear on the opener and hardware.
- There is one central point of adjustment and service rather than two independent springs.

Extension springs under full tension store a lot of energy in a stretched coil. Without the safety cables, a snapped extension spring can fly across the garage with serious force. If you have extension springs and the safety cables are missing or frayed, that's worth flagging immediately. Any competent technician will note it.

Neither type should be adjusted or replaced by a homeowner. The stored energy in both systems, even on a residential door, is enough to cause serious injury. This is covered in more detail in [Garage Door Repairs You Should Never Do Yourself](/guides/garage-door-repairs-never-diy).

## Upgrading from Extension to Torsion Springs

Switching from extension to torsion is a real upgrade some Sioux Falls homeowners make, especially when replacing a lightweight single door with a heavier insulated double door or when the existing extension springs have failed and the homeowner wants a longer-term solution.

The upgrade requires a compatible shaft, drum, and cable system to be installed above the door, which is why it's not a simple swap. It typically involves more parts and more labor than a like-for-like spring replacement, so expect the cost to reflect that. Nationally, the difference in total repair cost between a straight extension spring replacement and a full conversion to torsion can be significant; get a written, itemized quote before any work starts so you can compare it line by line.

One question worth asking: does the opening or header need any modification for the new hardware? Widening a garage door opening or changing the structural header requires a building permit in Sioux Falls, according to City of Sioux Falls home improvement guidance from April 2026. A same-size replacement with new hardware generally doesn't, but the city advises calling Planning and Development Services at 605-367-8670 if you're unsure. The installer should be able to tell you whether any permit applies to your specific job.

When hiring anyone for spring work, ask which city license the company holds. The City of Sioux Falls licenses residential contractors and since 2024 has also offered a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license for certain smaller jobs, according to the city's February 2024 and April 2026 announcements. You can verify any contractor at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors or by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672.

## Cold Weather, Lubrication, and What Spring Type Means for Maintenance

Both spring types need lubrication, but what you use and where you apply it matters. The right product for springs is a lithium-based or silicone-based lubricant applied to the coils, not a penetrating spray like WD-40. For extension springs, you also want to check the pulleys and the safety cables at each service. For torsion springs, the bearings on each end of the shaft need attention.

Sioux Falls homeowners deal with freeze-thaw cycles, blowing snow, and stretches of well-below-zero air that cause metal to contract and grease to thicken. Cold-thickened lubricant on a torsion spring bearing or extension spring pulley can slow the door enough to trigger an opener error or cause the door to bind. Lubricating in the fall before temperatures drop is smarter than waiting for a problem to surface at 7 a.m. on a January morning.

Several Sioux Falls garage door companies offer evening, weekend, or emergency service for exactly that scenario, while others keep standard weekday hours. Before booking anyone, ask specifically about after-hours fees so the number on the invoice doesn't surprise you.
