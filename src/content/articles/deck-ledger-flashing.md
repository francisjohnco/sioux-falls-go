If a deck is going to fail, the ledger board is usually where it starts. That single horizontal board bolted to your house carries the entire load of the deck and transfers it to your home's rim joist or band joist. When something goes wrong there, the consequences range from slow rot behind your siding to a sudden, catastrophic collapse.

Before you dig into this article, the [Building a Deck in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/deck-guide-sioux-falls) gives you the full picture from permits to final inspection. This article focuses on one piece of that puzzle, because it deserves its own attention.

## What the Ledger Board Actually Does

The ledger is a pressure-treated board that is fastened directly to the house framing. It acts as one side of your deck's frame, with the outer beam and posts carrying the other side.

Every time someone walks across your deck, the weight travels through the decking boards, through the joists, and into the ledger. That load then goes into your home's structure.

That is a lot of work for one board in a wet, freeze-thaw environment. When the attachment or the waterproofing is not done correctly, the ledger becomes the first place water gets trapped, wood rots, and hardware fails.

## Why Deck Flashing Is Not Optional

Deck flashing is a layer of metal or flexible membrane that sits between the ledger board and the house. Its job is to redirect water away from the connection point before it can soak into the framing.

Without proper deck flashing, water gets behind the ledger every rain, every snowmelt. The house sheathing stays wet. The rim joist behind it stays wet. Rot follows, often invisibly for years.

The tricky part is that flashing errors are almost always hidden once siding goes back on. By the time you see staining, soft wood, or insects, the damage behind the wall can be extensive.

A correctly flashed ledger has:

- A continuous layer of flashing tucked under the siding above the ledger
- Flashing that laps over the face of the ledger to direct water outward
- No gaps, punctures, or exposed fastener holes that let water in
- Sealant or tape at any penetrations where bolts pass through

Ask your contractor to walk you through how they flash the ledger before work starts, not after. If the answer is vague, that is worth pressing on.

## How the Ledger Gets Attached

The ledger is fastened through the house sheathing into the rim joist or band joist with lag screws or through-bolts. The pattern and spacing of those fasteners matters, and it is something the building inspector checks.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, decks require a building permit. Part of what that permit process does is put an inspector on the job at key stages, including before the ledger gets covered up. That inspection is one of the most important ones for exactly this reason.

Fastener problems that show up in older decks include:

- Lag screws that were too short to reach solid framing
- Screws installed at odd angles to avoid blocking inside the wall
- Hardware that was never rated for exterior or pressure-treated lumber contact
- Too few fasteners for the deck's size and load

If you have a deck that is 15 to 20 years old, the kind many Sioux Falls homeowners are now thinking about replacing before retirement summers, the ledger attachment hardware is worth a close look. The [Is Your Old Deck Safe? A Spring Checklist](/guides/old-deck-safety-check) walks through how to assess an aging deck, including the ledger.

## The Rot You Cannot See

Rot behind a ledger board is one of the more unsettling problems in residential construction because it hides so well. The deck surface above looks fine. The boards feel solid underfoot. But behind the siding, the rim joist is soft or missing entirely.

Signs that something may be wrong at the ledger include:

- Siding that is stained or bubbling near the deck attachment point
- A gap opening up between the ledger and the house
- Deck boards that feel springy or soft near the house wall
- Visible rust streaking from lag screw locations
- Insects, particularly carpenter ants, concentrated near the ledger

None of these is proof of hidden rot on its own, but any one of them is a reason to have a contractor remove a small section of siding and look at the actual framing. Catching a rotted rim joist early is a repair. Catching it after the ledger has pulled loose is a much larger project.

Pressure-treated lumber helps resist rot, but it is not impervious to it, especially when water is trapped against it year after year with no way to dry out.

## What to Ask Your Contractor Before They Start

The ledger board and its flashing are almost always hidden before you have a chance to inspect them yourself. That makes the conversation before work starts the most important one you will have.

Here are specific questions to ask:

- What flashing system do you use at the ledger, and can you show me an example or a photo?
- Will you remove existing siding to properly tuck the flashing, or are you flashing over it?
- What fastener type and pattern do you use, and is it sized for this deck's square footage?
- Will the ledger connection be inspected before it is covered?

On that last point, the City of Sioux Falls requires a Residential Building Contractor's License for all contractors working on residential homes within city limits, and the City does not accept licenses from other states. You can confirm any contractor's license on the publicly available Currently Licensed Contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors before anyone starts work.

If you want a broader list of questions to bring to a contractor conversation, [Questions to Ask a Deck Builder](/guides/choosing-deck-builder) covers the full range from permits to cleanup to warranty.

A permit also gives you a licensed inspector who reviews the ledger connection at the right stage of the build. That inspection is a layer of protection that benefits you, not just the contractor. For more on how the permit process works from a homeowner's perspective, [Deck Permits in Sioux Falls, What to Know](/guides/deck-permit-sioux-falls) has the details.

The ledger board will not be visible once your deck is finished. Getting it right happens before the first board goes down, not after.
