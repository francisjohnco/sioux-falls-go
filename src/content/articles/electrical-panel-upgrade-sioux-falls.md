Your electrical panel is easy to ignore until something stops working. For a lot of Sioux Falls homeowners, the first hint of a problem is a tripped breaker that won't reset, a contractor who says the panel "won't support" a new heat pump, or a home inspector flagging an outdated box before a sale. If any of that sounds familiar, this article will walk you through what to look for, what it means, and what to ask an electrician before anything is quoted or scheduled. For a broader look at how home electrical work is handled in Sioux Falls, start with [Home Electrical Work in Sioux Falls, What Homeowners Should Know](/guides/home-electrical-guide-sioux-falls).

## Signs Your Panel Is Maxed Out

Panels don't usually fail all at once. They show up in smaller ways first.

Watch for these warning signs:

- Breakers that trip regularly, especially under normal loads
- A breaker you have to reset more than once a week
- Lights that dim when a large appliance kicks on (the dryer, the AC, the well pump)
- No room left in the panel for new breakers
- A panel rated at 100 amps in a home with central air, an electric range, or a home office

That last point matters more than many homeowners realize. A 100-amp service was standard for decades, but modern households run significantly more load than a home built in the 1960s or 1970s was designed for.

Tandem breakers (two breakers in one slot) can be a temporary fix, but they also signal that someone already ran out of room. If your panel is full of them, that is worth asking an electrician about directly.

For more on what warning signs mean and when to treat them as urgent, see [Electrical Warning Signs You Should Never Ignore](/guides/electrical-warning-signs-home).

## What Makes Sioux Falls Homes Especially Load-Sensitive

Older neighborhoods in Sioux Falls, including parts of McKennan Park and All Saints, often have homes built when electric loads were a fraction of what they are today. Those homes may have been updated over the years, but the panel sometimes wasn't.

Sioux Falls winters also push electrical systems harder than the national average. With plenty of snow every winter and temperatures that regularly drop well below zero, heating systems run long and hard. A heat pump that might be a modest load in a milder climate is working overtime here from November through March.

That continuous demand adds up. If your panel is already stretched, a cold snap is exactly when you will notice it.

## Big Additions That Usually Require a Panel Upgrade

Certain projects almost always prompt the question of whether the existing panel can handle the new load. Here are the most common ones:

**Heat pumps.** A heat pump sized for a Sioux Falls winter is a significant electrical load. Many older 100-amp panels cannot support one without an upgrade to 200-amp service. Your HVAC contractor and your electrician need to talk to each other before anything is installed.

**EV chargers.** A Level 2 home charger typically needs a dedicated 240-volt circuit. Whether your current panel can support it depends on how much spare capacity you have. For the full picture on this, see [Installing an EV Charger at Home in Sioux Falls](/guides/ev-charger-installation-sioux-falls).

**Hot tubs.** A standard hot tub requires a dedicated 240-volt circuit, often 50 to 60 amps. That is a significant slice of a 100-amp panel.

**Finished basements.** Adding lighting, outlets, a bathroom, a home theater, or a workshop to an unfinished basement adds circuits. If the panel is already tight, you may hit a wall quickly.

**Electric dryers or ranges.** Switching from gas to electric for either one means adding a 240-volt circuit and real load you weren't carrying before.

None of these projects is unusual for Sioux Falls homeowners. All of them require an honest look at current panel capacity before the work starts.

## Load Management as an Alternative (or a Delay)

A full 200-amp service upgrade is not always the only answer. Depending on your situation, an electrician may suggest load management approaches that delay or reduce the scope of an upgrade.

Options worth asking about include:

- **Smart load management devices** that limit how much an EV charger or other high-draw device can pull when other loads are running
- **Subpanels** that distribute load without replacing the main service
- **Removing unused or redundant circuits** to free up space

These are not workarounds to skip a necessary upgrade. They are legitimate tools, and a good electrician will tell you whether they actually apply to your situation or whether a panel upgrade is genuinely the right path. Ask for the reasoning, not just the recommendation.

## Permits and What to Expect From the Process

A panel upgrade in Sioux Falls requires an electrical permit. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, an electrical permit is separate from a building permit and must be pulled either by a licensed electrical contractor or by a homeowner who lives in and personally owns the home and is doing the work themselves.

If you hire a contractor, they pull the permit. You can verify that a contractor is currently licensed by calling 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list online. You can also look up permit records on the city's permit site, which updates every Sunday.

After the work is done, an inspection is required. Do not skip this step. The inspection protects you, confirms the work meets code, and creates a record that matters if you ever sell the home or file an insurance claim.

For a deeper look at how the permit process works, [Electrical Permits in Sioux Falls, What Homeowners Should Know](/guides/sioux-falls-electrical-permit-guide) covers it in detail.

## Questions to Ask Before You Agree to Anything

Before any work is quoted or scheduled, ask the electrician these questions:

- What is my current panel's rated capacity, and what is my actual available capacity?
- Do I need a full service upgrade, a subpanel, or something else?
- Will you pull the permit, and can I see it before work starts?
- What does the inspection process look like, and who schedules it?
- What is the full scope of work in writing, including any conditions that could change the price?

That last question is especially important. Verbal quotes have a way of growing by the time the invoice arrives. Get everything in writing before work begins.
