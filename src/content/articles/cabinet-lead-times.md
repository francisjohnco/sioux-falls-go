Cabinets are usually the longest lead-time item in a kitchen remodel, and they set the schedule for almost everything else. Order too late and your countertop templating, electrical rough-in, and flooring all sit waiting. Order without accurate measurements and you are reordering, which can add weeks. This article walks through the full process: measuring, placing the order, receiving delivery, and fitting cabinets into the right sequence. For the full scope of a kitchen project from planning through punch list, start with the [Remodeling a Kitchen in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/kitchen-remodel-guide-sioux-falls).

## Understanding Cabinet Lead Time Before You Order

The phrase "cabinet lead time" refers to the time between your confirmed order and the day cabinets arrive at a supplier or your home. That window varies a lot depending on what you are buying.

Stock cabinets (pre-built, limited sizes and finishes) typically ship faster than semi-custom or fully custom lines. Semi-custom gives you more size options and finishes, but the cabinet lead time stretches longer. Fully custom cabinets take the longest of all.

Ask your supplier or designer for the current lead time in writing before you finalize anything. Lead times shift with factory backlogs, and what was true last spring may not be true today. Some Sioux Falls homeowners have found themselves living without a kitchen far longer than expected because a lead time estimate turned out to be optimistic.

Things that affect lead time:
- Cabinet line (stock, semi-custom, custom)
- Finish and door style (popular options sometimes backorder)
- Current factory and shipping backlogs
- Whether any items need to be reordered after delivery

Build in extra time. If your contractor says cabinets need to be on-site by a certain date, count backward from that date using the lead time, then order at least a week or two before that calculated deadline.

## Measuring the Right Way Before You Place a Kitchen Cabinets Order

Measurement errors are one of the most common reasons a kitchen cabinets order has to be revised or reordered. Even a small discrepancy between what was measured and what was ordered can mean a cabinet does not fit or a run ends short.

Measure every wall that will have cabinets, floor to ceiling and corner to corner. Note the locations of:
- Windows and doors (measure the rough opening and the finished frame)
- Outlets, switches, and any junction boxes
- Plumbing supply and drain locations
- Gas lines if you have them
- Any soffits or bulkheads above the upper cabinet line

Measure twice, in different spots on the same wall. Walls in older Sioux Falls homes, especially in neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints, are rarely perfectly square or plumb. A wall that reads one dimension at floor level may read differently at ceiling height. Your cabinet designer needs to know about those variations before the order goes in, not after.

If you are working with a designer, ask how they handle out-of-square walls in the cabinet plan. A good designer will plan every inch of the kitchen with those variations built in, and they will flag any gaps that need to be filled with filler strips.

## What to Confirm Before the Order Is Placed

Once measurements are done and a layout is set, do not rush the order. Walk through the full plan carefully with your designer or supplier before you sign off.

Go through the order and confirm:
- Every cabinet's width, height, and depth
- Door swing direction on every base and wall cabinet
- Drawer versus door configurations
- Hinge and hardware boring (pre-drilled or not, and for which hardware)
- Finish and door style match what you selected in person, not just online
- Filler strip widths and locations
- Molding and trim pieces included

Also confirm what is not included. Some lines quote cabinets only. Soft-close hardware, crown molding, light rail, and interior accessories like pull-out shelves may be add-ons. Missing pieces cause delays just as much as measurement errors.

Get the full order confirmation in writing, review it line by line, and ask the supplier to flag anything that ships separately or on a different timeline.

## Receiving Delivery and Checking for Damage

Cabinets are packaged for palletized freight, not for a homeowner's front door. Even well-packed cabinets can arrive with damage, and how you handle the delivery determines whether the supplier covers it.

When cabinets arrive:
- Do not sign the delivery receipt until you have done a full inspection, or at minimum noted "subject to inspection" on the paperwork
- Count every box against the packing list
- Look at every box for visible crushing, puncture, or water damage before the driver leaves
- Open boxes and inspect cabinet boxes, door samples, and any exposed wood before the driver leaves if you can

If you find damage, photograph it immediately with time-stamped photos. Note the damage on the delivery paperwork. Contact your supplier the same day. Most suppliers and manufacturers have a window for damage claims, and missing that window means you are paying out of pocket for replacement pieces, which then adds more cabinet lead time to your project.

Store cabinets in a conditioned space if installation is not happening immediately. Sioux Falls winters and humid summers can both affect unfinished wood or cabinet boxes left in an unheated garage.

## Sequencing Cabinets Into the Rest of the Remodel

Where cabinets fall in the project timeline affects every other trade. Getting the sequence wrong is one of the most common causes of a remodel running long.

A typical sequence looks like this:

1. Demolition of old cabinets and soffits
2. Rough-in work: any plumbing, electrical, or mechanical changes (these need licensed contractors and separate permits, per the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs)
3. Drywall patching and priming
4. Cabinet installation
5. Countertop templating (after cabinets are in and level)
6. Countertop fabrication and installation
7. Backsplash
8. Finish electrical and plumbing (outlets, switches, faucet, disposal)
9. Flooring (if going around cabinets rather than under them)
10. Finish work, hardware, and touch-up

Cabinets must be in and level before the countertop company comes to template. That is non-negotiable. Templating off rough framing or old cabinets almost always produces a countertop that does not fit. For more on how flooring fits into this sequence, see [Flooring Under Kitchen Cabinets or Around Them?](/guides/kitchen-flooring).

One thing that catches homeowners off guard: cabinets and countertops do not require a permit in Sioux Falls, according to the City of Sioux Falls April 2025 home improvement reminders. But if any part of your remodel touches plumbing, electrical, or mechanical systems, those pieces do require permits and licensed contractors. Skipping that step does not speed the project up. It creates problems at inspection or when you sell. For a full breakdown, see [Kitchen Remodel Permits in Sioux Falls](/guides/kitchen-permits-sioux-falls).

The most important thing you can do is lock in your cabinet order early, confirm every detail in writing, and build the rest of the schedule around the confirmed delivery date, not the estimated one. That one habit keeps more Sioux Falls kitchen remodels on track than almost anything else.
