Pulling permits feels like extra paperwork on an already complicated project, but skipping the ones you need can stall a home sale or cost you a fine down the road. The City of Sioux Falls draws a clear line: cosmetic work stays on one side, anything touching building systems lands on the other. Knowing where your project falls before you sign a contract saves real headaches. For the full picture of planning a kitchen overhaul, start with [Remodeling a Kitchen in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/kitchen-remodel-guide-sioux-falls).

## What Does and Does Not Need a Permit in Sioux Falls

According to the City of Sioux Falls April 2025 home improvement reminders, these items do not require a permit:

- Cabinets and countertops
- Flooring (tile, LVP, hardwood, laminate)
- Painting
- Finish hardware and fixtures attached to existing boxes

That covers a wide range of popular updates, including a full cabinet refresh or a countertop swap.

But the same City guidance is clear that projects involving building, electrical, mechanical, or plumbing systems do require permits. In a kitchen, that almost always means anything involving a new outlet, a relocated sink, a range hood vented through the wall, or structural changes to open up a wall.

If you are unsure whether your specific scope crosses the line, the City of Sioux Falls Building Services office can tell you before you spend a dollar on labor.

## The Three Permit Types That Come Up in Kitchen Remodels

The City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs separate permit types. A building permit covers structural work such as removing a wall, adding a window, or bumping out the kitchen footprint.

Electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits are separate. According to the City's FAQs, those permits require licensed contractors to do the work, unless you are a homeowner pulling a homeowner permit for your own single-family home.

Here is a quick breakdown of what typically triggers each:

**Building permit:**
- Moving or removing a load-bearing or non-load-bearing wall
- Adding or enlarging a window or exterior door
- Any structural addition to the kitchen footprint

**Electrical permit:**
- Adding circuits for a new range, dishwasher, or refrigerator location
- Installing new outlets or moving existing ones
- Under-cabinet lighting wired into the panel (see [Kitchen Lighting That Actually Helps](/guides/kitchen-lighting) for more on how lighting ties into electrical permits)

**Plumbing permit:**
- Moving the sink to a new location
- Adding a pot filler
- Relocating drain lines

**Mechanical permit:**
- Venting a range hood through the exterior wall or roof

## Licensed Trades and Homeowner Permits

The City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs are specific on this point: building permits do not cover electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work unless licensed contractors perform that work.

If you hire a contractor for those trades, the licensed contractor pulls the permit, not you. That is worth confirming before work starts. Ask your contractor directly: "Will you pull the permit for your trade, and can I see the permit number before work begins?"

If you plan to do electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work yourself in a home you own and live in, the City does offer homeowner permits for single-family residences. You are still subject to inspection; the permit just acknowledges you as the responsible party instead of a licensed trade.

A few things to know about the homeowner permit route:

- It applies only to your primary residence, not a rental or investment property
- Inspections still happen, and failed inspections mean rescheduling
- Some lenders and home insurance policies have questions about owner-performed trade work, so check yours first

## Why Permits Matter When You Sell

Homeowners sometimes skip permits to save time or avoid fees, but that decision shows up on the other end. When you sell, buyers and their agents commonly pull permit history for the property.

Unpermitted electrical or plumbing work can require retroactive inspection, re-opening walls, or discounting the sale price to account for the unknown.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, remodels are among the projects that need a building permit when structural work is involved. Getting that stamp on the front end is simply cheaper than resolving it at closing.

It also protects you during ownership. If a dishwasher drain line fails and there is water damage, an insurance claim tied to unpermitted plumbing work can get complicated fast.

## How to Confirm Permit Status Before Work Starts

The most common homeowner mistake is assuming the contractor handled permits without checking. Here is a simple sequence before work begins:

1. Ask your contractor which permits the project requires.
2. Ask who pulls each permit and get the permit number before that trade starts work.
3. Contact City of Sioux Falls Building Services if you want to verify the permit is active.
4. Keep a copy of the permit on-site during the project (contractors are generally required to post it, but having your own copy matters).
