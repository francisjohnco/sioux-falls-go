Replacing a concrete driveway is one of the bigger home expenses a Sioux Falls homeowner will face, and the final number can vary by thousands of dollars depending on details that aren't obvious until a contractor walks your property. Before you start collecting bids, it helps to understand what actually moves the price. For the full picture on materials, contractors, and what good work looks like, start with the [Concrete Driveways and Patios in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/concrete-guide-sioux-falls). This article focuses specifically on cost factors so you can read a bid intelligently and ask the right questions.

## Size, Shape, and What "Square Footage" Actually Means

The most basic driver of concrete driveway cost is how much concrete gets poured, but square footage alone doesn't tell the whole story.

A long, straight driveway is cheaper per square foot to form and pour than one with curves, angles, or decorative borders. Every change in direction adds forming time, which is labor.

Width matters too. A single-car driveway and a double-wide are not just different in area, they may require different base prep equipment and more reinforcement to hold up at the edges.

Ask your contractor to break down the bid by area so you can see exactly what square footage is priced and at what rate.

## Removal and Disposal of the Old Slab

Tearing out an existing driveway adds real cost, and it's a line item worth understanding before you sign anything.

Old concrete has to be broken up, loaded, hauled away, and disposed of. If the slab is thick, heavily reinforced, or close to a structure like a garage or retaining wall, the demo work takes longer and costs more.

Some homeowners assume demo is included as a standard line item. It sometimes is, sometimes isn't. Ask explicitly: does this bid include full removal and disposal of the existing concrete?

Also ask what happens to the debris. Responsible contractors haul it to a licensed facility. If a bid seems unusually low, it's worth asking this question.

## Base Preparation and Sioux Falls Soil

This is the cost factor that most homeowners overlook, and it's the one that determines whether your driveway lasts 10 years or 30.

Sioux Falls sits on expansive clay soil. Clay holds moisture, swells when wet, shrinks when dry, and shifts with freeze and thaw cycles. The city's frost line runs 42 inches deep, meaning the ground moves significantly every winter. A driveway poured directly onto unprepared clay is likely to crack, heave, or settle within a few years.

Proper base prep typically means excavating the clay, bringing in compacted gravel fill, and grading it to drain. That takes time, equipment, and material. Bids that skip or minimize base work are cheaper upfront and far more expensive later.

One Sioux Falls homeowner noted in a public review that after road construction disturbed their driveway, they specifically wanted the grading done right so water drained away and erosion stopped. That's the kind of detail base prep addresses. For more on why drainage matters so much here, see [Drainage and Grading, the Secret to Concrete That Lasts](/guides/concrete-drainage-and-grading).

Ask every contractor: how much excavation, what fill material, and how will it be compacted?

## Thickness and Reinforcement

A residential driveway is typically poured at 4 inches thick for cars and 5 to 6 inches for areas that see trucks or heavy loads. Thicker concrete costs more in material and sometimes in forming, but it lasts significantly longer under real use.

Reinforcement adds cost too. Options include:

- Rebar (steel bars tied in a grid)
- Wire mesh (less labor, less strength)
- Fiber reinforcement added to the mix (reduces minor cracking, not a substitute for rebar)

What's right for your driveway depends on the soil conditions, the thickness, and how the driveway is used. What matters most is that the bid specifies what reinforcement is included. Vague bids that say "reinforced as needed" are a red flag. Ask for the specific type and spacing in writing.

## The Approach, Permits, and Access

The approach is the section of driveway that crosses the city right-of-way between the street and your property line. It's often overlooked in driveway cost conversations, but it matters here.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, the city's Zoning Department handles driveway permits, including work that involves the approach. If your project touches the approach, a permit may be required. Replacing or widening an approach without one can create problems when you sell the house or if the city does street work nearby.

Ask the contractor directly: does this project require a permit, who pulls it, and is that cost included in the bid? For a full breakdown of when permits apply, see [Do You Need a Permit for a Driveway in Sioux Falls?](/guides/driveway-permit-sioux-falls).

Also, before any digging begins, the city requires you to call 811 (South Dakota One Call) so underground utilities are marked. A good contractor will handle this, but confirm it's part of their process.

Access to your property affects cost too. A driveway with limited truck access, a steep grade, or tight clearance near a garage or fence takes longer to work around. Mention any access constraints when you get bids so contractors price the job accurately.

## Finishes and Decorative Options

The basic finish for a residential driveway is a broom finish, which creates a slightly textured surface for traction. It's the most common and the most affordable.

Other options include:

- Exposed aggregate (pebbles brought to the surface for a textured, speckled look)
- Stamped concrete (patterns pressed into the surface before it sets)
- Colored concrete (pigment added to the mix or applied as a surface stain)

Each step up adds cost. Stamped and colored work in particular requires more skill and more time, so it's priced higher per square foot. These finishes also require sealing and more careful de-icer use in winter to stay looking good. For more on how finishes hold up here, the [Concrete Patios, Options and What to Ask](/guides/concrete-patio-ideas-sioux-falls) article covers durability and maintenance in detail, and most of those considerations apply to driveways too.

One note from local homeowners: a hand-finished broom surface done well is valued for durability. A decorative finish done poorly is an expensive problem. If you go decorative, ask the contractor for examples of similar work and ask how long they've been doing stamped or exposed aggregate pours.

## Timing, Scheduling, and What to Ask Before You Sign

Sioux Falls contractors get busy fast once the ground thaws. Homeowners have reported waiting about four weeks from an accepted bid to a start date, and projects sometimes run longer than the original timeline. That's not unusual, but it means planning matters.

Before you sign a bid, ask:

- What is the estimated start date and what could push it?
- What is included in demo, base prep, reinforcement, and cleanup?
- Is permit handling included, and who is responsible for pulling it?
- What is the warranty, and what does it cover?
- What finish is specified, and what maintenance does it need?

Get every answer in writing. A verbal promise about rebar or base depth is worth nothing if there's a dispute later.
