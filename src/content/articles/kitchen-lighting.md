Good kitchen lighting is one of those things nobody notices when it is done right, but everyone notices when it is wrong. You chop vegetables in a shadow, you squint at the stove, or you turn on the overhead light and still can't see into the cabinet. Getting lighting right during a remodel is easier and cheaper than fixing it after the fact. If you are early in the planning process, [Remodeling a Kitchen in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/kitchen-remodel-guide-sioux-falls) lays out the full picture before you start making decisions like this one.

## The Three Layers Every Kitchen Needs

Kitchen lighting works in layers, and skipping one of them is what creates the problems homeowners complain about.

**Ambient lighting** is the general overhead light that fills the room. It is usually recessed cans, a flush-mount fixture, or a semi-flush pendant. This is your starting point, not your whole plan.

**Task lighting** goes directly over the surfaces where you work. The counter, the sink, the stove. Without it, your own body blocks the ambient light and you are always working in your own shadow.

**Accent lighting** is optional but useful. Inside glass-front cabinets, toe kicks, or above upper cabinets. It adds depth and helps the kitchen feel finished rather than flat.

Most kitchens that feel dim or frustrating are missing the task layer entirely, or have one overhead fixture doing all three jobs at once.

## Under-Cabinet Lighting Is the Most Useful Upgrade

Under cabinet lighting sits at the bottom of the upper cabinets and shines directly onto the counter. It is the single most practical lighting upgrade in most kitchens, and it is easy to overlook during a remodel because it is not glamorous.

Options range from plug-in LED strips (no electrician needed) to hardwired puck lights or linear LED bars that disappear behind the cabinet rail. Hardwired looks cleaner because there are no visible cords, but it requires an electrical permit in Sioux Falls if new wiring is involved.

According to the City of Sioux Falls, projects involving electrical systems require a permit. If your contractor is running new circuits or wiring for under-cabinet lighting, that work needs a permit and must be done by a licensed electrical contractor or under a homeowner permit if you own and live in the home and are doing the work yourself.

Ask your electrician or contractor upfront:

- Is new wiring required, or can existing circuits handle it?
- Will a permit be pulled?
- Who pulls it and who is responsible if it is missed?

If the answer to that last question is vague, that is worth pressing on before work starts.

## Recessed Lighting: Placement Matters More Than Count

Homeowners often ask how many recessed lights they need. The honest answer is that placement matters more than quantity.

Lights placed too close to the cabinets cast harsh shadows on the counter. Lights placed too far toward the center of the room light the floor well but miss the work surfaces entirely. A good rule of thumb is to position recessed cans so they aim at the front edge of the counter, not the middle of the room.

Spacing depends on ceiling height, the trim size you choose, and how much overlap you want. This is where a kitchen designer or a good electrician earns their money. Homeowners who have been through a remodel often say they wished someone had planned every inch of the kitchen at the start, including lighting, before a single fixture was purchased.

Adding recessed lights to an existing ceiling almost always involves new wiring, which means an electrical permit under Sioux Falls rules. Plan for it in the budget and the schedule.

## Pendants and Decorative Fixtures

Pendants over an island or peninsula look great in every kitchen photo, and they can be genuinely useful over a breakfast bar or prep area. But they are task lighting only for the surface directly below them. They do not replace ambient or under-cabinet light.

A few things to get right with pendants:

- Height matters. Too low and they block sightlines across the kitchen. Too high and they lose their visual effect. Ask your designer or contractor for the standard range for your ceiling height.
- Wattage and bulb type affect color temperature. Warm white (around 2700K to 3000K) tends to feel comfortable in a kitchen. Cooler light (4000K and above) can feel clinical unless that is what you want.
- Pendant placement should line up with the island itself, not the room. Off-center pendants over a centered island are a common mistake that is hard to fix after the fact.

If new wiring is needed to place pendants where you want them, that is an electrical permit situation. See [Kitchen Remodel Permits in Sioux Falls](/guides/kitchen-permits-sioux-falls) for a clear breakdown of what triggers a permit and what does not.

## Natural Light and What to Do When You Don't Have Much

Sioux Falls kitchens in older neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints sometimes have small windows above the sink and not much else. Dark kitchens feel smaller and push people toward too much artificial light that still doesn't feel right.

If your remodel budget includes structural work, adding a window or enlarging an existing one makes a real difference. That is a building permit project according to the City of Sioux Falls. If structural changes are not in the budget, focus on maximizing what you have:

- Light-colored cabinet finishes reflect more light than dark ones.
- Under-cabinet lighting at the right color temperature can mimic natural light at the counter level.
- Glossy or semi-gloss backsplash tile reflects more than matte surfaces.
- Keeping upper cabinets away from windows (or replacing them with open shelving) lets natural light travel further into the room.

If you are also rethinking the cabinet layout to open up natural light, [Kitchen Layouts That Work Day to Day](/guides/kitchen-layout-basics) covers how the arrangement of cabinets and work zones affects how a kitchen actually functions, including where natural light lands.

## What to Get in Writing Before Work Starts

Lighting plans are easy to agree on verbally and easy to get wrong in execution. Before work starts, get the following confirmed in writing:

- The exact location of every fixture (recessed cans, pendants, under-cabinet lights)
- Whether new circuits or wiring are required and who is pulling the permit
- The fixture allowances in the bid, so you know what is covered and what costs extra if you upgrade
- Who is responsible for inspections

Homeowners who have been through remodels consistently say the estimates and timelines that stayed on target were the ones where everything was spelled out at the start. Lighting is small enough that it often gets glossed over in a big remodel bid. Push for specifics.
