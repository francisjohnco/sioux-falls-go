Ice dams look like a roofing problem, but they almost always start with what is happening inside your attic. Heat escapes through the ceiling, warms the roof deck, melts snow from underneath, and the water refreezes at the cold edge of the roof. Understanding that cycle is what lets you actually fix it, rather than just chipping at ice every winter. For a full picture of how insulation fits into your whole house, start with [Insulating a Sioux Falls Home, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/insulation-guide-sioux-falls).

## Why a Warm Attic Is the Real Problem

Your furnace heats your living space. Some of that heat finds its way into the attic through gaps around light fixtures, top plates, plumbing chases, and anywhere wiring or ductwork punches through the ceiling.

Once that warm air is in the attic, it heats the roof deck unevenly.

The area over the warm living space gets warm enough to melt snow. The eaves, which hang over the exterior wall and have no heat below them, stay cold. Meltwater runs down and hits that cold zone and refreezes, building up into an ice dam.

This is not about how much snow fell. It is about how much heat is leaking into your attic.

## What Air Sealing Actually Does (and Why It Comes First)

Insulation slows heat from moving through materials. Air sealing stops heated air from moving through gaps entirely. Both matter, but air sealing is where you get the most control over the warm-attic problem.

Common leak points on the attic floor include:

- Gaps around recessed light cans
- Top plates where interior walls meet the ceiling
- Plumbing vent pipes and stacks
- Attic hatches and pull-down stairs
- Gaps around any ductwork

A contractor who does a thorough attic job should seal all of these before adding any blown-in insulation on top. If you are comparing quotes, ask each contractor whether air sealing is included and how they confirm it is done. See [How to Compare Insulation Quotes](/guides/insulation-quotes) for the specific questions to ask.

Sioux Falls homeowners have noted in reviews that the best attic crews explain what they found and show photos before and after. That is a reasonable thing to expect and ask for.

## Attic Ventilation: The Second Half of the Fix

Air sealing reduces how much heat gets into the attic. Attic ventilation removes whatever heat does get in, keeping the roof deck cold and even from ridge to eave.

Proper attic ventilation requires both intake and exhaust, working together.

- Soffit vents (at the eaves) pull in cold outside air
- Ridge vents or gable vents let warm air escape near the peak
- Baffles (also called rafter baffles or vent chutes) keep insulation from blocking the soffit vents

If insulation is packed against the soffits without baffles, the intake is choked off and the system does not work. This is a common mistake in older Sioux Falls homes where insulation was added without installing baffles first.

If your attic has adequate insulation depth but you are still getting ice dams, have a contractor check whether the soffit vents are actually open and unobstructed.

## What Ice Dams Do to Your Home

Ice dams back water up under the shingles. Roof shingles are designed to shed water moving downward, not water sitting still or pushing upward from a dam below.

Water that gets under the shingles can soak into the roof deck, work into the wall framing, and show up as wet spots or stains on upstairs ceilings. Some Sioux Falls homeowners have noticed this as wet patches in January, sometimes with ice visible on attic framing when they look inside.

That moisture can lead to rot, mold, and insulation that is wet and no longer doing its job. For a detailed look at the full range of damage ice dams cause and what to do about an active situation, read the [Ice Dams on Sioux Falls Roofs](/guides/ice-dams-sioux-falls-roof) guide.

Heating cables on the roof edge and gutter guards do not prevent ice dams. They can manage where water drains in the short term, but the dam still forms, and water can still back up under shingles. The fix is always in the attic.

## Why Gutters Are Not the Cure

A clean gutter does not stop an ice dam. The dam forms on the roof surface above the gutter, not inside it.

Clogged gutters can make drainage worse once ice starts melting, but clearing them does not address why the ice is there in the first place.

If a salesperson suggests that a gutter product will prevent ice dams, ask them to explain how it stops snow from melting on the warm part of your roof. It cannot. That answer belongs in the attic.

## The Right Order of Work

If you want to actually prevent ice dams with insulation, here is the sequence that works:

1. Air seal the attic floor thoroughly, especially around penetrations and the attic hatch
2. Install vent baffles in every rafter bay that has a soffit vent
3. Add insulation to the recommended depth over the sealed attic floor
4. Confirm soffit and ridge vents are unobstructed

Doing step three without step one or two is one of the most common mistakes, and it leaves homeowners wondering why they still get ice dams after paying for new insulation.

If your upstairs rooms are also cold regardless of the thermostat, that is usually the same air-leakage problem showing up differently. [Why One Room Is Always Cold](/guides/cold-rooms-causes) covers how to think through that alongside the ice dam issue.

A home energy assessment with a blower door test can tell you how leaky your house actually is before you spend money on materials. Ask your contractor whether they offer this, or ask what City of Sioux Falls utility programs are available in your area.
