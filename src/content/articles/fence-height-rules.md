Before you mark a fence line or order materials, knowing the height limits for each part of your yard can save you a costly rebuild. Height rules in Sioux Falls come from at least two places: City code and, often, your neighborhood's covenants. Getting one right and missing the other is a real and common mistake. Start with [Building a Fence in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/fence-guide-sioux-falls) if you want the full picture before diving into the specifics here.

## What the City of Sioux Falls Actually Says About Fence Height

The City's residential fence guide states that on both corner lots and interior lots, fences in the front yard can be up to four feet tall.

That four-foot rule is the one verified number available from the City's published guidance. The City's documents do not give a single published limit for rear or side yards in the same pamphlet, so do not assume any number you read elsewhere applies to your property.

For anything beyond that front-yard figure, your best move is to call the Zoning Office directly at 605-367-8254 before you buy a single board.

Questions worth asking when you call:

- What is the maximum height for a fence in my rear yard?
- Are there different rules for a side yard that faces a street?
- Does my lot's zoning classification change any of these limits?
- Is there a setback distance from the sidewalk or curb?

Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the date. That note protects you if there is ever a dispute.

## Front Yard Fence Height in Sioux Falls: Why Four Feet Matters

Four feet is shorter than most people picture when they think "fence." A four-foot fence is roughly chest height on an average adult, which means it provides a visual boundary without blocking sightlines the way a privacy panel would.

That height limit is not arbitrary. Front yards affect how drivers and pedestrians see traffic, driveways, and intersections. The City's concern for sightlines is especially important on corner lots, where two streets meet and visibility in multiple directions matters.

If your goal is to keep a dog in the front yard, four feet may not be enough depending on the breed. A determined jumper can clear it. See [Choosing a Fence for Dogs](/guides/fence-for-dogs) for a full breakdown of height, gaps, and what actually keeps different dogs contained.

If your goal is privacy, a four-foot front yard fence is not going to deliver it. The City's height limits mean that true privacy fencing is generally a backyard solution in Sioux Falls.

## Corner Lots and Sight-Line Rules

Corner lots face rules that interior lots do not. When your property sits at an intersection, fences near that corner can obstruct a driver's view of cross traffic, cyclists, or pedestrians.

The City's residential fence guide specifically calls out corner lots alongside interior lots in its height guidance, which suggests the City treats them similarly for the four-foot front-yard limit. But corner lots may also trigger additional sight-triangle restrictions depending on your zoning district.

A sight triangle is a clear zone near an intersection where nothing (fence, shrub, sign) should exceed a certain height. If you are on a corner, ask the Zoning Office specifically whether a sight-triangle rule applies to your property before you finalize any fence plan.

Mistakes corner-lot owners make:

- Assuming the front-yard limit only applies to the yard facing the main street, not the side yard that faces the cross street
- Installing a fence and discovering after the fact that a sight-triangle setback applies
- Skipping the survey and later finding the fence is on city right-of-way, not private property

## Covenants: The Layer the City Does Not Tell You About

City code is the floor, not the ceiling. Many Sioux Falls neighborhoods, including areas with active homeowners associations and older platted additions, have recorded covenants that set stricter rules than the City does.

A covenant might cap rear yard fences lower than what the City allows, require a specific style (no chain link, for example), or ban certain materials entirely. Some require HOA board approval before you break ground, and skipping that step can mean being required to remove the fence at your own expense.

According to publicly shared homeowner experiences from Sioux Falls, HOA disputes over fence height and style are among the most common sources of frustration, right alongside disagreements with neighbors over property lines.

Where to find your covenants:

- Your title documents or closing paperwork
- The Minnehaha County or Lincoln County Register of Deeds (depending on where your home sits)
- Your HOA, if one exists for your neighborhood

For a full walkthrough of the approval process and what to watch for, [Fences, Covenants and HOA Approval](/guides/fence-and-hoa) covers exactly this ground.

## Permits, Inspections and Getting It Done Right

No matter what height you choose, the City of Sioux Falls requires a permit to build, move, replace, or repair a fence. The City's fence permit pamphlet lists a $20 permit fee and a required final inspection after construction is complete.

Permits expire six months after issue, so plan your timeline accordingly. Extensions are available if you need them.

To schedule the final inspection after your fence is up, call the Zoning Office at 605-367-8254. The inspection confirms that your fence meets the terms of the permit you pulled.

A few things to confirm before the first post goes in:

- Your property lines are located (the City recommends a licensed surveyor for this, not estimates or old surveys)
- Your permit is in hand, not just applied for
- You have called 811 to have underground utilities marked

On that last point, [Call 811 Before You Dig a Fence Line](/guides/call-811-before-digging) explains exactly how the locate process works and why waiting a few days before you dig is worth it.

If you are relying on a fence contractor to pull the permit, confirm in writing that they are doing it and that the final inspection is included in the scope of work. Some homeowners have discovered after the fact that the permit was never pulled.

## A Simple Checklist Before You Build

Pull these together before construction starts:

- Call 605-367-8254 to confirm height limits for your specific yard zones and zoning district
- Pull your deed and title documents to find any recorded covenants
- Contact your HOA, if applicable, and get written approval
- Have your property lines located by a licensed surveyor
- Pull your City permit and schedule the final inspection for after construction

Height rules feel like paperwork until you are asked to tear down a fence you just paid for. Getting the specifics confirmed in writing, from the right sources, before you build is the one step that eliminates that risk entirely.
