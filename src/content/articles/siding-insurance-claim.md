Sioux Falls takes a beating every spring and summer. Hail that dents cars and strips shingles hits your siding just as hard, and a lot of homeowners discover they have a legitimate siding insurance claim only after a neighbor or contractor points it out. If that happened to you recently, you are not alone, and you have more leverage than you might think. This article walks through the claim process step by step, from the first photos to the final check. For the broader picture on replacing siding afterward, see [New Siding in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/siding-guide-sioux-falls).

## Documenting Damage Before Anyone Else Touches It

The single most important thing you can do after a storm is document everything before anything gets cleaned up, covered, or repaired.

Walk the whole exterior in good daylight. Look for:

- Circular dents or cracks in vinyl panels
- Chipped or missing paint on fiber cement
- Spatter marks (the dark rings hail leaves on wood or paint)
- Bent or dented soffit and fascia
- Damaged window casing and trim

Take photos from close up and from a few steps back so the adjuster can see location and scale together. Photograph every wall, not just the one that faces the storm direction, because hail bounces.

Video works well for showing the pattern of damage across a whole elevation. Date-stamp everything if your phone does not do it automatically. If the storm left hailstones on the ground, photograph one next to a coin for size reference before they melt.

Write down the date and rough time of the storm. Cross-reference it with a weather service record later if the insurer questions when the damage happened. The National Weather Service keeps archived storm data you can reference.

## Filing the Claim and What Comes Next

Call your insurer as soon as you have your documentation together. Do not wait weeks. Most policies require you to report damage within a reasonable time, and "reasonable" is defined in your policy, so read that section.

When you call, ask:

- What is my deductible for wind and hail specifically? (Some policies have a separate percentage-based deductible for hail.)
- Is there a deadline to file or to complete repairs?
- Will you send your own adjuster, or will I need to hire a public adjuster?

The insurer will assign a claim number and schedule an adjuster visit. That visit is the key event in your whole claim process.

It is worth knowing that the siding claim process runs very similarly to a roof claim. If you want to understand how adjusters evaluate storm damage across the whole exterior, [How a Roof Insurance Claim Works in South Dakota](/guides/roof-insurance-claim-south-dakota) covers the adjuster visit, supplements, and settlements in useful detail.

## Having Your Contractor at the Adjuster Visit

This is the step homeowners most often skip, and it is one of the most useful things you can do.

A licensed local contractor who has seen the damage can walk the adjuster around, point out hits that are easy to miss on a quick walk-through, and ask about line items while everything is still visible. Adjusters are not always siding specialists. Having someone present who is familiar with local material costs and scope can prevent a low initial estimate.

Before you invite any contractor onto your property, verify they hold a current City of Sioux Falls license. The City requires a Residential Building Contractor's License or, for re-siding work with no structural changes, the Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license created in 2024. You can confirm a contractor's status on the City's publicly available licensed contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors. The City does not accept licenses from other states, so an out-of-state storm chaser cannot legally do this work in Sioux Falls.

After major hail events, door-to-door solicitors show up fast. Some pressure homeowners to sign over their insurance claim assignment before the work even starts. That document gives the contractor, not you, control of your claim money. Do not sign anything like that. If you are unsure whether a visitor is legitimate, check the City's licensed list first. The article [Spotting Storm Chasers After a Hail Storm](/guides/storm-chasers-siding) goes into the specific warning signs in more detail.

## Matching Disputes and What to Ask

This is where a lot of Sioux Falls siding claims get complicated. When only one or two walls are visibly damaged, an insurer may want to pay only for those walls. But if your siding color has faded over several years, or if the profile has been discontinued, new panels on two walls will not match the old ones on the other two.

A matching dispute is a real and common problem, not a homeowner trying to get more than they deserve.

Ask your insurer directly:

- Does my policy include a matching provision?
- If the damaged material can't be matched, what does the policy say about replacing adjacent undamaged sections?
- Is this a coverage question or an adjustment question?

South Dakota does not have a statewide statutory matching requirement for siding, so your answer comes from your specific policy language. Read the sections on "like kind and quality" and "matching." Some policies are explicit. Others leave room for negotiation.

For a deeper look at why matching is harder than it sounds, the article [Matching New Siding to Old After a Repair](/guides/matching-siding-colors) explains fading, discontinued profiles, and what a sample board comparison actually reveals.

## Depreciation, Actual Cash Value vs. Replacement Cost

When your adjuster sends the estimate, it will likely show two numbers: the replacement cost value (RCV) and the actual cash value (ACV). The difference between them is called depreciation.

If your policy pays ACV, you receive only the depreciated value up front, which may be significantly less than what new siding actually costs. If your policy pays RCV, you typically receive the ACV first, then a second payment (called a recoverable depreciation check or supplement) after the work is completed and you submit the final invoice.

Ask your insurer:

- Does my policy pay ACV or RCV for siding?
- If RCV, when and how do I request the recoverable depreciation?
- Is there a deadline to complete repairs and claim recoverable depreciation?

Do not assume the first check is the whole settlement. Many homeowners are surprised when a second check arrives after repairs are done. That is how RCV policies are designed to work.

Also watch for non-recoverable depreciation, which is depreciation your policy will not pay back regardless of repairs. It may appear on the estimate. If you see it and do not understand why, ask the adjuster to explain which line items carry it and why.

## Permits, Final Steps, and What to Keep

Re-siding a Sioux Falls home requires a building permit, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs. Your contractor should pull that permit before work starts. If they suggest skipping it, that is a red flag worth taking seriously.

Keep copies of everything:

- Your original claim submission and date
- The adjuster's written estimate (called a scope of loss)
- Any supplements your contractor submits
- The final contractor invoice
- The building permit and inspection records
- Your own photos from before and after

Insurance carriers can request documentation during or after a claim. Having your paperwork organized makes any dispute easier to resolve.

After the work is complete, get written confirmation from your insurer that the claim is closed and that no balance remains. Some homeowners skip this step and later discover an open item on their policy history.

A well-documented hail claim siding situation, handled with a licensed contractor and clear communication with your insurer, usually resolves without major disputes. The problems tend to come from rushed decisions, signing over claim rights too early, or skipping the adjuster walk-through. Take your time, check credentials, and ask questions in writing when it matters.
