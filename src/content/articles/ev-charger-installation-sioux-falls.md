More Sioux Falls homeowners are adding electric vehicles every year, and the first question most of them have is not about the car. It is about the garage. Getting a Level 2 charger home installation done right takes more planning than most people expect, and skipping steps can cost you later.

Before you schedule anyone, read the [Home Electrical Work in Sioux Falls, What Homeowners Should Know](/guides/home-electrical-guide-sioux-falls) guide. It covers the basics of how permits, licensing, and panels work here, so this article can go deeper on EV chargers specifically.

## What Level 2 EV Charger Installation Actually Means for Your Home

A standard wall outlet is a Level 1 charger. It delivers about 1.4 kilowatts and can add roughly 4 to 5 miles of range per hour. That is fine for a plug-in hybrid or a short daily commute, but it is slow for a full EV.

A Level 2 charger runs on 240 volts, the same voltage as a dryer or range. It delivers anywhere from 7 to 19 kilowatts depending on the unit and circuit size, adding 20 to 30 miles of range per hour or more. For most EV owners, that means a full charge overnight.

To install a Level 2 charger, your electrician will run a dedicated 240-volt circuit from your panel to the charger location, usually the garage. The most common setup is a 50-amp circuit with a 40-amp continuous load rating, but your EV's onboard charger and your panel capacity both factor in.

This is not a DIY project for most homeowners. It requires a permit in Sioux Falls, and the work needs to be done by a licensed electrical contractor or by a homeowner who lives in and owns a single-family home and pulls the permit personally, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs.

## Checking Your Panel Before Anything Else

The most common surprise during EV charger installation is a panel that does not have room.

A 50-amp circuit for a Level 2 charger is a significant load. If your panel is already running a heat pump, electric water heater, electric range, or electric dryer, it may be close to capacity. An older 100-amp panel in a Sioux Falls home built before the 1980s may not have the headroom at all.

Ask your electrician to do a load calculation before quoting the charger circuit alone. They should look at:

- Total panel amperage (100 amp, 150 amp, or 200 amp)
- Existing large loads and how much capacity remains
- Whether a subpanel or panel upgrade is needed first

If your panel is already stretched, adding an EV charger without upgrading it is a problem. The article [Do You Need an Electrical Panel Upgrade?](/guides/electrical-panel-upgrade-sioux-falls) walks through exactly how to tell.

Some electricians will also recommend a smart charger with load management, which throttles the EV charge rate when other large appliances are running. That can let you avoid a full panel upgrade in some situations. Ask whether that option makes sense for your load profile.

## Permits and Licensing for EV Charger Installation in Sioux Falls

Sioux Falls requires a separate electrical permit for this work. It does not get covered by a general building permit.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, a licensed electrical contractor must pull the permit, or the homeowner can pull it personally if they live in and own a single-family home and are doing the work themselves. If you are hiring an electrician, the permit should be in their name, not yours.

You can verify that your electrician holds a current city license by calling 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list online. You can also look up whether a permit was actually pulled for your address on the city's permit site, which updates every Sunday.

Do not let an electrician skip the permit. The inspection that follows protects you, confirms the work is safe, and matters if you ever sell the home or file an insurance claim.

## Garage Considerations for Sioux Falls Winters

Sioux Falls winters create two specific issues for EV chargers that homeowners in warmer climates never deal with.

First, temperature affects charging. EV batteries charge more slowly in extreme cold, and some Level 2 chargers have operating temperature minimums. If your garage is unheated, ask whether the charger you are buying is rated for sub-zero temperatures. Many are, but not all.

Second, the charger location matters more in a cold climate. If you are parking in the garage, the charge cable needs to reach the car's charging port without being stretched taut or lying on the icy floor where it can be run over. Your electrician should talk with you about mount height and location before running any conduit.

If your garage is detached, running a circuit underground is likely the only option. That means digging, which means calling 811 (South Dakota One Call) before any work starts so underground utilities are marked, as required by the City of Sioux Falls. Trench depth and conduit type are part of the permit scope, so make sure the quote covers everything from the panel to the final charger mount, not just the indoor portion.

## Getting a Written Quote and Knowing What to Ask

Verbal quotes for electrical work have a way of growing by the time the invoice arrives. That is one of the most consistent frustrations Sioux Falls homeowners have shared about hiring electricians. Get everything in writing before anyone starts.

A written quote for EV charger installation should include:

- The circuit size (amps) and wire gauge
- Whether a subpanel or panel upgrade is included or excluded
- The charger unit itself, if the electrician is supplying it, or whether you are supplying your own
- Indoor versus outdoor conduit runs and their lengths
- Trench work and backfill if the garage is detached
- The permit fee
- Any conditions that could change the price, written out explicitly

Ask specifically what happens if the electrician opens the wall or ceiling and finds something unexpected. Know the answer before work starts.

For more on protecting yourself through the quoting process, read [Why Every Electrical Quote Should Be in Writing](/guides/get-electrical-quote-in-writing). It covers exactly how to frame the conversation and what to watch for in the final invoice.

## What to Expect on Installation Day

A straightforward attached-garage installation with an existing 200-amp panel usually takes a few hours for a licensed electrician. A more complex job involving panel work, conduit through finished walls, or a detached garage with trenching will take longer.

The city inspection typically happens within a few days of the permit being pulled. The inspector will check the circuit, the connections at the panel, and the charger mount. You do not need to be present for every step, but you should ask your electrician when the inspection is scheduled so you know the job is closed out properly.

After installation, test the charger with your car before the electrician leaves. Confirm the charge rate matches what you expected, and ask for any documentation related to the charger warranty in case you need it later.
