## Is Sioux Falls water actually hard, or is that a myth?

It's real. Most local sources put Sioux Falls water in the 12.8 to 15 grains per gallon range, drawn largely from the Big Sioux Aquifer, which is limestone-rich. That's firmly in "hard water" territory and is the reason water softeners, extra scale in water heaters, and shorter appliance lifespans come up so often in local plumbing conversations.

## Do I need a permit for plumbing work in my house?

For most repairs, no. For new construction, repiping a significant section of a home, or work that changes your home's plumbing layout, the City of Sioux Falls Building Services office typically requires one. When in doubt, ask your plumber directly whether the job needs a permit — a reputable one will know and will pull it rather than skip it.

## Why do my pipes freeze even though I keep my house warm?

Interior temperature doesn't protect pipes that run through unheated spaces — garages, crawlspaces, or exterior walls, especially on the north side of a house. The fix is usually insulation or a heat trace cable on the specific vulnerable section, not turning up the thermostat.

## How deep do water lines need to be buried in Sioux Falls?

At least 42 inches, per the local building code amendment tied to how deep the ground actually freezes here. This is deeper than in many other regions and is worth knowing if you're planning any landscaping or excavation work near existing lines.

## My drain is slow but not fully clogged — is that worth calling someone about?

Often yes, especially right around spring thaw. The freeze-thaw cycle shifts soil around sewer lines, and a slow drain that appears out of nowhere in March can be an early sign of a line that's been stressed over winter, not just a routine clog.

## How fast can someone actually get to an emergency?

This varies by company and by season — winter is genuinely the busiest time for plumbing emergencies here, so response times can stretch during a hard freeze. It's worth asking any plumber you're considering what their real emergency response time looks like before you need it, not after.
