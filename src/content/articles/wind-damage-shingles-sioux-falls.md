Sioux Falls gets its share of serious wind events, and the July 25, 2026 storms made that clear in a hurry. According to the National Weather Service Sioux Falls, a 72 mph gust hit the airport and winds near Harrisburg reached 77 mph, leaving widespread tree, fence, and roof damage across the southern metro.

If you think your shingles took a hit, [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls) is the best place to start for the full picture. This article goes deeper on wind damage specifically: what it looks like, what to check without climbing anything, how to protect the roof while you wait, and what to do with your insurance company.

## How Wind Actually Damages Shingles

Wind does not have to tear a shingle completely off to cause real damage. It often starts at the edges or along the ridge, where wind gets underneath and flexes the shingle repeatedly.

That flexing creases the shingle at the nail line, breaking the bond between layers and leaving a hinge point where water can get in. You may never see this from the street, but every rain after that is a potential leak.

The three most common types of roof wind damage Sioux Falls homeowners find after a storm:

- **Lifted shingles.** The sealant strip on the underside loses its grip. The shingle lies back down and may look fine from below, but the bond is broken.
- **Creased or kinked shingles.** A sharp bend across the face, usually about an inch up from the lower edge. The crease is a crack waiting to open.
- **Missing shingles.** Fully removed by wind, leaving bare felt or decking exposed. These are obvious and urgent.

Older shingles are more vulnerable because the sealant dries out over time. The 72 mph gusts recorded on July 25, 2026 by the National Weather Service Sioux Falls are well above what standard 3-tab shingles are rated for, and even architectural shingles can fail at that speed if the sealant is already compromised.

## What to Check From the Ground Right Now

Do not get on the roof yourself. A shingle that just survived a 70 mph storm may be loose in ways that are not visible, and a fall from a one-story roof can cause serious injury.

Work from the ground and from inside. Here is what to look for:

**Outside:**
- Missing shingles, especially near ridges, hips, and edges
- Shingles that look curled up at the lower edge or slightly lifted
- Granules in the gutters or piled at downspout outlets (a large amount after one storm suggests shingles were scraped against each other)
- Bent, dented, or missing ridge cap shingles along the peak
- Flashing that has pulled away from a chimney, wall, or pipe boot
- Gutters or downspouts pulled loose from the fascia

**Inside your attic (if you can safely access it):**
- Daylight showing through the decking
- Wet insulation or water stains on rafters
- A musty smell that was not there before

Take photos of everything you see, with timestamps. The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to photograph damage before making any repairs, and to keep a log of every contact with their insurer starting from the day of the storm.

## Temporary Protection While You Wait

If you have a missing shingle or a visible opening, you do not have to wait for a contractor to do something about it. The South Dakota Division of Insurance specifically tells homeowners to make temporary repairs to prevent more damage and to keep all receipts for reimbursement.

A few practical steps:

- A heavy-duty polyethylene tarp, secured with wood battens screwed into the roof deck (not into shingles you want to save), covers a missing section and keeps rain out until a contractor can get there.
- Do not use duct tape or staple a tarp to finished shingles. Both cause more damage.
- Hardware stores in Sioux Falls carry roofing tarps and emergency sealant. Buy what you need and save the receipt.
- If you are not comfortable on a ladder, call a contractor just for the temporary cover. Some roofers will tarp quickly after a major storm event, though demand spikes fast after a widespread event like the July 25, 2026 storm.

The Division of Insurance is clear on one point: do not make permanent repairs before contacting your insurer. Temporary protection is expected and reimbursable. Permanent repairs done without an adjuster's sign-off can complicate your claim.

## Filing a Wind Damage Claim in South Dakota

Wind damaged shingles are a covered peril on most homeowner policies, but how much you receive depends on your specific policy, your deductible, and whether the insurer uses replacement cost value or actual cash value. Read your policy before you call.

A few things the South Dakota Division of Insurance says every homeowner should know when filing:

- File promptly. Policies can set notice deadlines, and waiting too long after a storm can be used as a reason to reduce or deny a claim.
- Insurers must respond to claim communications within 30 days.
- Ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer you receive.
- If you disagree with the insurer's findings, you have the right to ask for that itemized breakdown and to dispute it.

When the adjuster comes out, ask if your contractor can be present. A roofer who does insurance work regularly can point out damage an adjuster might miss, particularly lifted or creased shingles that do not read well in photos.

[How a Roof Insurance Claim Works in South Dakota](/guides/roof-insurance-claim-south-dakota) walks through the full process from first call to final check, including what to do if the payment comes up short.

After the July 2026 storms, expect adjusters to be busy across the southern metro. Scheduling may take longer than normal, which is another reason to get temporary protection in place first.

## Watch for Storm Chasers at Your Door

A widespread wind event draws out-of-town roofing crews fast. After the July 25, 2026 storms, the southern Sioux Falls metro saw the kind of damage that brings door-to-door solicitors within days.

The South Dakota Attorney General's consumer protection office warns homeowners specifically after storm events: do not be pressured into a quick agreement, ask for proof of insurance and bonding, get a written contract with all work and pricing spelled out, be cautious of cash-only terms, and do not pay in full before the job is done.

A legitimate contractor doing shingle work in Sioux Falls is required to hold a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license from the City of Sioux Falls, carry at least $300,000 in general liability insurance, and post a $20,000 compliance bond made to the city. You can verify that license by calling Building Services at 605-367-8670.

Door-to-door sales contracts in South Dakota typically carry a three-day right to cancel, with any deposit refunded within ten days of cancellation. If a contractor pressures you to sign before the three days are up, that is a serious red flag.

[Storm Chasers After Sioux Falls Hail, How to Spot Them](/guides/storm-chasers-sioux-falls-roofing-scams) covers the full pattern of what to watch for and how to check a contractor's credentials before you sign anything. The Attorney General's Consumer Protection line is 1-800-300-1986.

## Timing Your Repair Before Winter

Wind damage caught in July or August gives you a reasonable window. Sioux Falls winters arrive fast, and a loose or missing shingle going into October is a liability.

Lifted shingles that did not reseal before cold weather sets in will not seal at all until temperatures climb again in spring. That means every freeze-thaw cycle (and Sioux Falls gets plenty of them) works at the opening and can eventually push water under the surrounding shingles.

A few timing realities to keep in mind:

- Demand for roofers spikes immediately after a major storm event. Contractors book out quickly. Call early, even if you are still waiting on your adjuster.
- Ask any contractor for a written timeline, not just a verbal estimate.
- If a contractor cannot get to your job before the weather turns, ask specifically about temporary protection and whether they can install it now.
- If your roof is older and the wind damage is significant, it may be worth asking honestly whether repair or replacement makes more sense before winter. [Repair or Replace Your Roof? A Sioux Falls Homeowner's Guide](/guides/repair-or-replace-roof-sioux-falls) lays out the factors clearly.

The worst outcome is getting the run-around from several contractors while October arrives and a temporary tarp is all that stands between your attic and the first snowfall. Start the process now, document everything, and ask for written answers to your questions.
