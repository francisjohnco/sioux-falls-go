Replacing a garage door is one of those projects where the price range is wide enough to feel useless until you understand what actually moves the number. A single-car steel door with no frills sits at a very different price point than a double-car insulated wood-look composite with windows and a new opener bundled in, and both are legitimate choices depending on what you have and what your home needs. If you are still figuring out whether you need a full replacement or just a repair, start with the [Garage Door Repair in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/garage-door-repair-sioux-falls) before committing to anything. This article focuses strictly on new door cost: what you are paying for, what you can control, and how to read a quote line by line so nothing surprises you.

Sioux Falls winters are not gentle on garage doors. The City of Sioux Falls sits in a humid continental climate with long stretches of below-zero air sweeping in from Canada, and as a National Weather Service meteorologist noted (reported by SiouxFalls.Business in February 2021), the February 2021 cold snap was the longest of its kind since around 1996. That kind of cold hammers springs, cables, lubricant viscosity, and weatherstripping in ways that warmer climates simply never see. When you are shopping for a new door, local climate is not a background detail, it is a spec sheet item.

## Single Door vs. Double Door: the First Big Price Fork

The most immediate variable in any garage door quote is the opening size. A single door, typically 8 to 10 feet wide, costs noticeably less than a double door, which usually runs 16 feet wide and requires a heavier door, more robust hardware, and a stronger opener. Nationally, single-car doors (materials and basic installation) often fall somewhere in the range of roughly $700 to $1,500 for a standard steel door, while double doors can run $1,200 to $3,000 or more before you factor in insulation, style upgrades, or opener bundles. These are typical national ranges, not Sioux Falls averages. Get a written quote from at least two local companies to understand what you will actually pay here.

The double door also puts more demand on the spring and cable system. If your existing hardware is old or already under stress, the installer may recommend upgrading the spring setup at the same time, which adds to the total. That is not necessarily a bad recommendation, but it should appear as a separate line item on your quote so you can evaluate it on its own merits. For more on how springs factor into the picture, the article on [broken garage door springs in Sioux Falls](/guides/broken-garage-door-spring-sioux-falls) explains why paired replacement is common and what a fair service visit looks like.

## Material Choices: Steel, Wood-Look, and Composite

Most doors sold in the Sioux Falls market today are steel, and for good reason. Steel handles freeze-thaw cycles better than raw wood, it does not warp when snow sits against the bottom seal, and it is available at nearly every price point. A basic single-layer steel door is the most affordable option, but it offers no insulation and dents more easily. Mid-range steel doors come with a polystyrene or polyurethane foam layer sandwiched between steel skins, which improves both insulation and dent resistance.

Wood-look steel and composite doors are designed to give you the aesthetic of a carriage-house or craftsman door without the maintenance burden of real wood. These tend to run at the higher end of the price range, but they hold their finish better in prairie wind and wide temperature swings than painted real wood would. Genuine wood doors are available and do look beautiful, particularly on older homes in neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints where architectural detail matters, but they require regular painting or staining and are more vulnerable to moisture damage near the threshold where blowing snow accumulates.

Composite doors, made from recycled wood fibers and resin, split the difference. They resist warping and moisture better than real wood and can be finished to look like painted or stained wood grain. They are heavier than steel, which affects the spring and opener sizing, and they carry a price premium over standard steel. If you are considering the curb appeal angle, the article on [whether a new garage door helps sell a Sioux Falls home](/guides/new-garage-door-home-value-sioux-falls) goes into which styles tend to suit Sioux Falls neighborhoods.

## Insulation Levels: Why R-Value Matters More Here Than Most Places

Insulation is measured by R-value, and garage door manufacturers typically offer doors in the range of R-6 to R-18 or higher. A single-layer steel door has essentially no insulation value. A door with polystyrene (expanded foam board) might reach R-6 to R-9. Polyurethane foam injected between two steel skins delivers R-13 to R-18 and also adds structural rigidity.

For an attached garage in Sioux Falls, the case for a well-insulated door is stronger than in milder climates. When temperatures drop below zero for days at a time, an uninsulated door lets cold air flood the garage, which affects any living space above, the water supply lines running through that space, and the performance of the opener itself. Even if you do not heat your garage, an insulated door buffers the temperature swings enough to matter. The tradeoff is cost: a high-R polyurethane door can run $200 to $600 more than the base model in the same line, depending on size. The article on [insulated garage doors and whether they are worth it in Sioux Falls](/guides/insulated-garage-door-worth-it-sioux-falls) walks through who benefits most and what the honest limits of energy savings look like.

Bottom seals and weatherstripping deserve attention in this same conversation. Blowing snow finds every gap, and a new door with a worn or mismatched bottom seal is a problem from day one. Ask your installer what seal comes standard and whether it is compatible with your threshold and floor surface.

## Windows, Hardware, and Opener Bundles

Windows add natural light to the garage interior and contribute to curb appeal, but they also add cost and, depending on placement, can reduce the door's effective R-value in that panel. A row of windows across the top section of a double door can add $150 to $400 or more to the door price, depending on the glass type (clear, frosted, or decorative) and the number of inserts. If security is a concern, frosted glass gives light without visibility into the garage.

Decorative hardware, hinges, handles, and magnetic carriage-house hardware overlays, is largely cosmetic on modern sectional doors but can add $50 to $200 depending on the finish and quantity. It is worth asking whether the hardware is included or priced separately, because some quotes bundle it and some do not.

Opener bundles are worth evaluating carefully. Many installers offer a package price that includes the new door, installation, and a new opener. If your existing opener is more than 10 to 15 years old or lacks modern safety features like auto-reverse, bundling a new opener often makes financial sense because the installer is already there and the labor overlap reduces the total cost compared to two separate visits. If your opener is relatively new and working well, you may not need to replace it. Ask for the bundle price and the door-only price so you can compare. For a detailed breakdown of when openers should be repaired versus replaced, see the guide on [garage door opener repair or replace](/guides/garage-door-opener-repair-or-replace).

Smart openers with phone alerts, remote access, and package delivery integration are available at most price points now and are worth considering if you travel or want to monitor garage access. They add roughly $50 to $150 over a standard opener in most product lines.

## Permits, Licensing, and What to Ask Before Work Starts

This is where a lot of homeowners skip steps they should not. According to the City of Sioux Falls (February 2024 and April 2026), the city licenses residential contractors, and since 2024 also offers a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license that covers smaller jobs including door and window replacements on homes that require no structural changes. You can check any contractor on the city's licensed contractor list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors or by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672. The city's announcement does not specifically name garage doors, so ask the company directly which city license it holds before work begins.

On permits: the City of Sioux Falls requires a building permit for structural work and for building or placing a detached garage, according to city home improvement guidance from April 2026. Widening a garage door opening or changing the header above it is structural and would require a permit. For a same-size door replacement with no structural changes, ask your installer directly, or call Planning and Development Services at 605-367-8670 to confirm before the job starts. A reputable company will know the answer and will not be bothered by the question.

## How to Compare Quotes Line by Line

Getting two or three written quotes is not just about finding the lowest number. It is about understanding what each quote actually includes so you are comparing the same job. When you receive a quote, look for these items listed separately: the door itself (brand, model, R-value, and material), the opener if included (brand, horsepower, and features), labor for installation, removal and disposal of the old door, any hardware or weatherstripping, and the warranty on parts and labor.

Removal of the old door is sometimes included and sometimes not, and disposal fees can add $50 to $150 to the total. Some companies charge separately for haul-away. The warranty question matters too: a one-year labor warranty is common, but some companies offer two years. Ask whether the warranty covers the spring system, because springs have their own cycle-based lifespan and are sometimes excluded.

If a quote is significantly lower than the others, ask what is not included rather than assuming it is a better deal. And if you are being quoted over the phone without anyone measuring the opening, that is a number to treat with skepticism.
