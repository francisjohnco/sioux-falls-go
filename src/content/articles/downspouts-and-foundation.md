Your gutters collect water off the roof, but where that water goes next is what actually protects your foundation. A lot of Sioux Falls homeowners discover this the hard way, usually after a heavy summer rain leaves water pooling against the house or trickling into the basement. Getting the discharge point right matters as much as the gutters themselves.

If you're newer to this topic, start with the [Gutters for a Sioux Falls Home, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/gutter-guide-sioux-falls) for a full picture before diving into drainage specifics here.

## Where Downspout Water Actually Needs to Go

The goal is simple: get water far enough away from the house that it moves away from the foundation rather than toward it.

A downspout that dumps water right at the base of the house is almost as bad as no gutter at all. The soil around most Sioux Falls homes contains clay, which absorbs moisture slowly and holds it longer than sandy or loamy soil. When water sits against a foundation repeatedly, it has time to find cracks, expand them through freeze-thaw cycles, and eventually work its way inside.

The general rule contractors and grading guides use is that water should end up at least six feet from the foundation. Some situations call for more, especially on homes where the grade is flat or slopes back toward the house.

The ground itself needs to slope away from the foundation too. A common guideline is about one inch of drop per foot for the first six to ten feet out from the house. If your yard is flat or tilts inward at the base, downspout extensions alone won't fully solve the problem.

## Downspout Extensions: Simple and Often Overlooked

A downspout extension is exactly what it sounds like: a piece of pipe or flexible corrugated tube that attaches to the bottom of your downspout and carries water farther across the yard before releasing it.

Extensions are inexpensive and something most homeowners can add without a contractor. The tradeoff is that rigid extensions stay put and work reliably, while flexible corrugated extensions are easier to maneuver around landscaping but can kink, trap debris, or get run over by a lawn mower.

A few things to check on any extension:

- It should angle away from the house, not just straight down
- The outlet end should sit on a splash block or drain onto turf, not bare soil or mulch that washes away
- It should not terminate right next to a window well, neighbor's property line, or low spot that fills up

One mistake Sioux Falls homeowners make heading into winter: leaving a corrugated flexible extension attached when freeze-thaw cycles begin. Water trapped inside can freeze and block the entire discharge path. For the full picture on getting your downspouts ready before cold weather hits, the article on [getting gutters ready for winter](/guides/winter-gutter-prep) covers exactly that.

## Underground Drainage Lines

When the yard doesn't give you enough room for a surface extension, or when you want a cleaner look, an underground drainage line is worth considering. These are pipes buried below grade that carry water from the downspout to a point farther out in the yard, usually ending in a pop-up emitter that opens under water pressure and closes when dry.

This is a bigger project than clipping on a surface extension. The pipe needs to be buried deep enough to avoid being cut by routine yard work, and in Sioux Falls the frost line sits at 42 inches according to local building code, so any line you want to stay thawed and functional year-round needs to account for that depth. A line that isn't properly sloped or is poorly jointed can back up and trap water right where you were trying to avoid it.

Ask any contractor doing this work:

- What slope does the pipe have from the downspout to the emitter?
- Where exactly will the emitter discharge?
- How will the line be cleaned if it clogs?
- Is a permit required for any grading or excavation involved?

According to the City of Sioux Falls, gutters and fascia work does not require a permit, but significant yard grading or drainage work could involve different requirements depending on scope. When in doubt, call City of Sioux Falls Building Services before work starts.

## Grading and What It Has to Do With Your Gutters

Even the best gutter system underperforms when the yard doesn't drain well. Grading is the shape and slope of the soil around your home, and it's the last line of defense once the downspout does its job.

Sioux Falls sits on expansive clay soil in many neighborhoods. Clay heaves with moisture and frost, which means the grade around an older home can shift over time. What started as a healthy outward slope in the 1970s might now have a slight inward tilt that sends water right back toward the foundation.

Signs your grading may be working against you:

- Grass or mulch washed away near the foundation after rain
- Wet or damp soil right against the house days after it rains
- Low spots in the yard that hold standing water for more than a few hours
- Water marks or mineral deposits on the lower sections of your siding

Regrading is typically done by adding and compacting soil close to the foundation to restore the outward slope. It is not a dramatic fix, but it makes every other part of the drainage system work better.

## Signs Your Drainage Is Already Failing

Sometimes water is finding its way toward the foundation or inside without any obvious puddle outside. Watch for these signals:

- Efflorescence (white chalky deposits) on basement walls
- Musty smell in the basement that gets worse after rain
- Paint bubbling or peeling on the lower interior walls
- Cracks in the foundation wall, especially horizontal ones
- Window wells filling with water during storms

If water is already getting inside, the gutter and drainage fixes above matter, but you may also be dealing with a moisture or structural problem that goes beyond gutters. The guide to [Water Damage in Your Sioux Falls Home, What to Do](/guides/water-damage-guide-sioux-falls) walks through what to assess and who to call when water has already found its way in.

Sioux Falls homeowners also mention overflowing gutters in heavy summer downpours as a recurring frustration. If the gutters themselves are the source of water falling too close to the house, the article on [why your gutters overflow and how to fix it](/guides/overflowing-gutters) covers the most common causes and what to check first.

A dry basement in Sioux Falls is not just about sump pumps and waterproofing products. It starts with where every inch of roof water ends up after a rain. Getting the downspouts, extensions, grading, and underground lines working together is what makes the difference between a foundation that holds up for decades and one that slowly wears down from the outside in.
