Home watch means trusting someone with access to your home while you're away, sometimes for months at a time — which makes verification and clear communication expectations more important here than for almost any other home service.

## What to verify first

- **Bonding and insurance**, given the level of access and trust involved.
- **Whether they're affiliated with or certified by the National Home Watch Association**, which sets defined standards for the industry, though excellent uncertified providers exist too.
- **How they document each visit** — photos and written notes are a stronger signal than a simple "all good" text message.

## Questions worth asking

1. **"What exactly do you check on each visit?"** A real home watch visit covers more than a quick look — HVAC function, signs of leaks, exterior condition, mail/package accumulation, and more.
2. **"How do you handle an emergency you discover during a visit?"** Get specifics on their process and how quickly they'll reach you.
3. **"Can you coordinate with other service providers if needed?"** — for example, letting in a repair technician while you're away.
4. **"What's your visit frequency during winter versus summer?"** Given the added winter risk of frozen pipes and snow load, frequency should reasonably increase.

## Red flags

- No bonding or insurance
- Vague or inconsistent documentation of visits
- No clear emergency response process
- Reluctance to provide references from other clients, especially other snowbird or seasonal-absence homeowners

## Why this matters more than it might seem

A missed frozen pipe or an unnoticed roof leak can cause thousands of dollars in damage over weeks of absence. The difference between a genuinely thorough home watch provider and a casual "we'll swing by" arrangement often only becomes obvious after something goes wrong — which is exactly why the verification questions above are worth taking seriously before you leave, not after.
