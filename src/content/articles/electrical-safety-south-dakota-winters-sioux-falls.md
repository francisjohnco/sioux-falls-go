Electrical service calls genuinely spike in Sioux Falls once temperatures drop into the single digits and below, and it's worth understanding why before assuming the worst — because in most cases, the cause is load, not a failing electrical system.

## Why breakers trip more in winter

Block heaters, space heaters, and heat tape all draw significant current, and in a hard freeze, homeowners often run several of these at once overnight. A single breaker trip during a genuine cold snap is frequently just that circuit doing exactly what it's supposed to do — protecting against overload. It's not automatically a sign of a deeper problem.

## When a tripping breaker is actually a red flag

If the same breaker trips repeatedly, or trips even when the load hasn't obviously changed, that's different from a one-time overload event. Repeated tripping can indicate a wiring issue, a failing breaker, or a connection problem — and it's worth having an electrician look at it within the same week, not waiting until it becomes a bigger issue mid-winter.

## Space heater safety, specifically

Space heaters are one of the most common causes of both electrical fires and overloaded circuits in Sioux Falls homes during winter. A few things that actually matter:

- Never run a space heater on an extension cord as a permanent solution — extension cords aren't rated for the sustained draw.
- Don't run more than one high-draw space heater on the same circuit.
- Older homes with original wiring are more vulnerable to this specific failure mode than newer construction with modern circuit capacity.

## Older neighborhoods carry more real risk here

Homes in Sioux Falls's older established neighborhoods — built well before modern electrical codes existed — were wired for a household that used a fraction of the electricity a modern home draws. Adding space heaters, block heaters, and modern appliances onto original wiring is exactly the scenario that causes both overloaded circuits and, in worse cases, real fire risk. If your home is older and you're relying on space heaters through the winter, that's a legitimate reason to have an electrician assess your panel and circuits before the coldest months, not after something goes wrong.

## The bottom line

One tripped breaker during a genuine cold snap: reset it and move on. Repeated trips, warm outlets, or a burning smell: that's not a wait-and-see situation — call an electrician the same day.
