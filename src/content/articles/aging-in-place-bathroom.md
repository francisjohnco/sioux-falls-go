A bathroom that felt perfectly fine at 55 can become genuinely difficult at 70, often before you expect it. The good news is that most of the changes that make a bathroom safer and easier to use every day can be built into a remodel without making the room feel like a hospital wing. If you are already thinking about a remodel, [Remodeling a Bathroom in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/bathroom-remodel-guide-sioux-falls) is the right place to start before you dig into this article.

The couples who push through an aging in place bathroom remodel most successfully are the ones who plan for where they want to be in 15 to 20 years, not just where they are today. That mindset shapes every decision, from the floor tile you pick to whether the doorway gets a little wider.

## The Shower Is Usually the First Thing to Get Right

For most older couples, the shower is the single highest-priority item in an accessible bathroom remodel. Climbing over a tub wall, or even stepping up into a traditional shower curb, becomes a real hazard over time.

A low-threshold or zero-threshold (curbless) shower eliminates that step entirely. Water is controlled through a slight floor pitch toward the drain and sometimes a small linear drain along one edge, rather than a curb you have to step over.

These showers can be built with custom tile or with prefabricated systems. If you want to understand that trade-off in more detail, the [Tub to Shower Conversion, One Day Install or Custom Tile?](/guides/tub-to-shower-conversion) guide walks through both options honestly.

A few things worth discussing with your remodeler:

- How the floor will be sloped and where the drain will sit
- Whether the waterproofing system beneath the tile is a membrane or a prefab base, and how it is tested
- What the transition looks like at the bathroom floor so there is no trip hazard there either

A built-in bench or fold-down teak seat inside the shower is worth adding at the same time. It is far easier and less expensive to frame one in during construction than to add it later.

## Block the Walls Now, Even If You Do Not Need Grab Bars Yet

This is the single most cost-effective aging in place move you can make, and most homeowners skip it.

Standard drywall cannot support grab bar loads. Grab bars need to be anchored into blocking, solid backing, or studs. If you install blocking now, any grab bar can be added later at any height, in any position, without opening the walls again.

The blocking is just solid wood or plywood installed inside the wall cavity before the board and tile go on. It costs very little to add during a remodel. It costs a lot to retrofit later.

Good locations to block:

- Both sides of the shower entry
- The inside walls of the shower at standing height and sitting height
- The wall beside the toilet
- Near the vanity if the person uses it for balance

Talk to your contractor about blocking the entire shower surround and the toilet wall as standard practice, not an afterthought.

## Fixtures That Make Daily Use Easier

A handheld showerhead on a slide bar is one of the most practical upgrades in an accessible bathroom. It lets someone shower sitting down, rinse specific areas without twisting, and hand off to whoever needs it without adjusting a fixed head.

Sioux Falls water runs hard, typically around 10 to 15 grains per gallon depending on which treatment plant serves your home, according to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report. That means showerheads accumulate mineral scale faster than in softer-water cities. A showerhead with rubber nozzles is easier to clean, and it is worth asking your plumber about whether a softener makes sense for your home. The [Hard Water and Your New Bathroom in Sioux Falls](/guides/hard-water-bathroom-sioux-falls) guide covers this in more depth.

Comfort height toilets sit about two to four inches taller than a standard toilet, closer to chair height. That difference is significant for anyone with hip or knee issues. If you are replacing a toilet anyway, it is an easy swap.

Other fixture details worth discussing with your remodeler:

- Lever-style faucet handles instead of knobs (easier to grip with limited hand strength)
- Pressure-balancing or thermostatic shower valves to prevent scalding
- A single-control shower valve placed where it can be reached before stepping fully under the water

## Lighting and Floors Matter More Than People Expect

Older eyes need more light to see the same detail, and the contrast between wet tile and dry tile is easy to misjudge in a dim bathroom.

Good aging in place lighting means:

- A bright overhead fixture that does not cast shadows on the floor
- Night lighting at the baseboard or toe kick, so the room is navigable at 3 a.m. without flipping on a bright overhead
- Good light at the mirror that hits the face, not the back of the head

Slip-resistant tile has a coefficient of friction rating (COF). For a wet shower floor, look for a COF of 0.60 or higher. Smaller tiles with more grout lines also add grip. Talk to your tile installer about which products meet that standard for a wet application.

Sioux Falls goes through significant freeze-thaw cycles each winter, and the frost line runs 42 inches deep here. That matters mostly for exterior construction, but it is a reminder that our homes work hard. Inside, focus on porcelain tile rated for wet use rather than natural stone that is more porous and harder to grip when wet.

## Wider Doorways and What It Actually Takes to Change One

A standard interior door is 24 to 28 inches wide. A wheelchair requires a minimum of 32 inches of clear opening, and 36 is more comfortable. Even if neither of you uses a wheelchair now, a wider doorway is easier to navigate with a walker, a cane, or just carrying laundry.

Widening a doorway is a structural project. It may or may not involve a load-bearing wall, and that determination matters for how the opening is framed.

According to the City of Sioux Falls, projects that involve structural work require a building permit. A Residential Building Contractor's License is required of any contractor doing that work within city limits. If you are not sure whether your contractor is licensed, the City publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list you can check directly.

A pocket door or barn-style door is worth considering at the same time. Swing-out doors use floor space that can be hard to manage with a walker or wheelchair. A pocket door disappears into the wall entirely.

This is also a good moment to review the vanity layout and make sure there is enough clear floor space to turn comfortably. The [Choosing a Vanity and Storage for a Small Sioux Falls Bathroom](/guides/small-bathroom-vanity-storage) guide covers clearance and layout in detail.

## Planning for Later Without Making It Feel Clinical

The goal is a bathroom that works beautifully now and continues to work as your needs change. That does not mean grab bars everywhere and a shower chair bolted to the wall.

Design choices that are both functional and attractive:

- A curbless shower with large-format tile looks modern and clean, not medical
- A built-in bench can be tiled to match the walls
- Grab bars now come in finishes that match towel bars and faucets
- Blocking inside the walls is invisible

An occupational therapist (OT) is the right person to consult if you or your partner have specific mobility, balance, or vision concerns. A doctor can tell you what is coming medically; an OT can translate that into specific measurements and placements. Many hospitals and rehab networks in the Sioux Falls area can connect you with one.

It is worth getting that input before your contractor finalizes the layout, not after the tile is on the walls.
