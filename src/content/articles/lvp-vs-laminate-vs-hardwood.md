Choosing between LVP, laminate, and hardwood feels straightforward until you are standing in a showroom holding three sample boards that all look nearly identical. The right answer depends on where the floor is going, what your household puts it through, and how Sioux Falls winters affect your home's humidity levels. If you are starting fresh and want the full picture before diving into specifics, [New Floors in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/flooring-guide-sioux-falls) is worth reading first.

This article focuses on the real differences between the three materials so you can narrow your choice before you ever talk to a contractor.

## What Each Material Actually Is

LVP stands for luxury vinyl plank. It is a synthetic product, usually four to eight layers, with a printed wood-look layer under a clear wear layer. The core is 100 percent plastic, which means it does not absorb water.

Laminate is a wood-fiber core (HDF) with a photographic top layer sealed under a hard melamine coating. It looks similar to LVP and is often confused with it, but the core swells when it gets wet.

Hardwood is exactly what it sounds like: a solid or engineered plank of real wood. Engineered hardwood has a real-wood veneer over a plywood or HDF core. Solid hardwood goes all the way through.

Knowing what is inside each product matters because that core determines where it can go and what happens when something goes wrong.

## Water Resistance and What That Means Here

Sioux Falls homeowners often finish basements, and that changes the conversation immediately. LVP wins on water resistance without any competition. Because the core is plastic, a flooded utility room or a slow appliance leak does not mean a ruined floor if you catch it reasonably fast.

Laminate looks waterproof on the surface, but water that gets into seams reaches the HDF core and causes swelling. Some newer laminate products market themselves as water-resistant, and that is worth asking about specifically, but no laminate matches LVP's tolerance for standing water.

Solid hardwood and basements simply do not mix in most Sioux Falls homes. The concrete slab below grade holds moisture, and wood moves with humidity. For a deeper look at that, [Best Flooring for a Sioux Falls Basement](/guides/basement-flooring-options) covers the specifics of concrete prep and subfloor requirements.

Engineered hardwood sits in the middle. It handles humidity better than solid, but it still has real wood in it, and most manufacturers do not warranty it below grade.

## How Sioux Falls Winters Affect All Three

Sioux Falls has a humid continental climate with significant humidity swings between January and July. Indoor humidity in a tightly sealed home in January can drop well below 30 percent without a humidifier running. That matters because wood, and to a lesser extent laminate, moves with those changes.

Solid hardwood gaps and cups as humidity drops and rises. Engineered hardwood is more stable but still moves. LVP expands and contracts with temperature more than humidity, and large open rooms without enough expansion gaps can cause planks to buckle against walls.

Laminate is somewhere in between. It shrinks and swells with humidity less aggressively than solid hardwood but is not immune.

Practical takeaway for Sioux Falls homes:
- Run a humidifier in winter if you have hardwood. Target 35 to 50 percent relative humidity.
- Leave proper expansion gaps on LVP, especially in rooms that see direct sunlight or sit above a crawl space.
- Acclimate all three materials before installation. LVP needs 24 to 48 hours in the room. Hardwood can need several days.

[Why Floors Gap and Cup in Sioux Falls Winters](/guides/winter-flooring-gaps-humidity) explains what is actually happening and when a gap is normal versus a problem.

## Pets, Kids, and Daily Wear

For households with dogs and young children, the wear layer on LVP is usually the starting point of the conversation. Wear layers are measured in mils (thousandths of an inch). A 12-mil wear layer handles most residential use. Twenty mils and above holds up better under large dogs with claws.

Laminate has a hard surface that resists scratches reasonably well, often better than a soft-species hardwood like pine. But once laminate is scratched or chipped, there is no refinishing it. You replace the plank or the floor.

Hardwood can be refinished. That is its single biggest long-term advantage. A solid hardwood floor can be sanded and recoated multiple times over decades. Engineered hardwood can be refinished at least once, sometimes more, depending on the veneer thickness.

For traction, all three are slippery when wet compared to carpet. If traction matters for young children or older family members, look for a textured surface finish rather than a high gloss.

More detail on this topic is in [Flooring for Pets and Kids That Holds Up](/guides/flooring-for-pets-and-kids).

## The Look, Matching, and Resale Value

LVP vs hardwood is not just a performance question. It is also an aesthetic and financial one. Real hardwood has variation, grain depth, and a texture that most LVP products still do not fully replicate. In an older McKennan Park or All Saints neighborhood home where original hardwood is part of the character, replacing it with LVP may not feel right to the next buyer.

That said, LVP and laminate technology has improved significantly. Many buyers cannot tell the difference from a standing position. The question is what the homes around yours have, and what your buyers will expect.

On resale, general guidance from real estate professionals tends to favor hardwood in higher price-point homes, but that varies by neighborhood and buyer pool. No invented numbers here: ask a local real estate agent what they are seeing in your specific area of Sioux Falls before making a decision based on resale alone.

One practical risk to know about: ordering the wrong color is more common than you would think. Homeowners who take samples home often change their minds when they see the floor under their own lighting and against their trim and cabinets. Order a returnable sample first. Confirm the color code in writing before anything is ordered, because correcting a wrong-color order after the fact can be difficult.

## Comfort Underfoot and Sound

Hardwood, and especially engineered hardwood, feels solid and warm underfoot in a way that LVP and laminate do not always match. LVP has some give depending on the core type, and rigid-core LVP is firmer than WPC (wood-plastic composite) LVP, which has more cushion.

Laminate on a hard subfloor can sound hollow and clicky. An underlayment pad reduces that significantly, and some laminate products come with underlayment pre-attached.

If you are replacing carpet in a room and stepping onto hard flooring for the first time, the comfort difference will be noticeable regardless of which hard floor you pick. An area rug solves a lot of that for living and bedroom spaces.
