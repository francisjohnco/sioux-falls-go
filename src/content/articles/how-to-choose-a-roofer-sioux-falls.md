Finding a roofer in Sioux Falls is not hard. Finding one you can actually trust with your biggest asset, especially after a hailstorm when every contractor in a three-state radius shows up at once, takes a little more work.

This article is a vetting guide built from what local homeowners say matters most in their own words: the right credentials, a real local address, honest answers, and follow-through after the job is done. For a broader look at repair and replacement decisions, start with the [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls).

## Credentials to Verify Before You Call Anyone Back

The City of Sioux Falls requires a building permit for shingle work on homes, and any contractor doing that work must hold either a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license or a broader Residential Building Contractor license. According to the City of Sioux Falls, that license requires a $20,000 compliance bond made to the city and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance.

That bond matters to you personally. If a contractor abandons the job or causes damage, the bond is a financial backstop. Without it, your recourse is a lawsuit against someone who may be impossible to find.

You can call Building Services licensing at 605-367-8670 to confirm any contractor is currently licensed. Take two minutes and do it.

The South Dakota Attorney General also advises homeowners to ask for a current South Dakota sales or contractor's excise tax license. If a roofer does business in this state, they should have one. The Attorney General's consumer protection line is 1-800-300-1986 if you have doubts.

**Before you schedule an estimate, confirm:**

- City of Sioux Falls Residential Roofing or Residential Building Contractor license (call 605-367-8670)
- $20,000 city compliance bond
- General liability insurance of at least $300,000
- South Dakota sales or contractor's excise tax license
- A local business address, not just a phone number or a website

## Questions to Ask Every Roofer

Ask these out loud, not just on a form. The answers, and the hesitation, tell you a lot.

**On credentials and business:**

- "Can you give me your city license number so I can verify it?"
- "What is your local business address?"
- "How long have you been operating in Sioux Falls specifically?"
- "Who will be on my roof, your own crew or a subcontractor?"

If a roofer cannot answer the license question immediately, that is a red flag. If they use a subcontractor, ask whether that sub carries their own insurance.

**On the job itself:**

- "Who is my day-to-day contact once work starts?"
- "How will you protect my landscaping and flower beds?"
- "What does cleanup look like, same day or the next morning?"
- "Will there be a magnet sweep for nails?"
- "What happens if you find damaged decking after tear-off?"

That last question is important. Decking damage is common and not always visible until the old shingles come off. A good contractor will show you the damage, explain the cost, and get your approval before proceeding.

**On the contract and warranty:**

- "Can I see a sample contract before I commit to anything?"
- "Where is the workmanship warranty in writing, and how long does it run?"
- "What is the manufacturer's warranty on the shingles, and will you register them in my name?"
- "What payment schedule do you use?"

For more on what warranties actually cover and what can void them, see [Roof Warranties Explained, Manufacturer vs Workmanship](/guides/roof-warranty-explained).

## Answers That Should Worry You

Some answers are not red flags on their own. But in combination, or paired with pressure, they signal a contractor you should walk away from.

**Be cautious if a roofer:**

- Cannot give you a license number on the spot
- Asks for full payment upfront, or cash only
- Gives you a quote verbally and resists putting it in writing
- Pressures you to sign the same day, especially at your door after a storm
- Says they will "handle everything" with your insurer but will not explain how
- Has no local office or gives you only a cell number
- Cannot tell you who will actually be managing your job site

The South Dakota Attorney General specifically warns homeowners after hailstorms not to be pressured into quick agreements and to be cautious of cash-only terms. Door-to-door sales contracts in South Dakota carry a three-day right to cancel, with any deposit refunded within ten days.

If you feel rushed, slow down. A contractor who loses patience when you ask reasonable questions is not someone you want on your roof.

## Working with Your Insurance Adjuster

A lot of Sioux Falls homeowners want a roofer who will deal with the adjuster for them. That is reasonable, and a good local contractor will know how to do it. But there are boundaries worth understanding.

A contractor can walk the roof with your adjuster, point out damage, and explain what the repair scope should include. What they cannot legally do is negotiate your claim on your behalf as a licensed public adjuster, or get paid a cut of your settlement.

**Ask your roofer:**

- "Have you worked with adjusters from my insurance company before?"
- "Will you be present at the adjuster inspection?"
- "If the adjuster's scope misses something, can you submit a supplement?"

The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement and to keep a log of every contact with the insurer. Insurers must respond to claim communications within 30 days.

If your first check comes in lower than expected, that does not necessarily mean the claim is over. Some roofers will begin work and wait for a supplemental payment. Ask upfront whether that is an option, and get the payment terms in writing. ## The Contract, Timeline, and What Happens After

A written contract is not bureaucratic. It is the only thing protecting you if the job goes wrong.

**Your contract should spell out:**

- Full scope of work, materials, and brands
- Price and payment schedule (never 100 percent upfront)
- Start date and estimated completion
- Who is responsible for permits and inspections
- Landscaping protection and cleanup expectations
- What happens if hidden damage is found
- Workmanship warranty terms, in writing
- How and when the manufacturer warranty gets registered in your name

Timeline communication is one of the things Sioux Falls homeowners mention most in reviews, both when it goes well and when it does not. Ask directly: "How will you communicate with me about scheduling changes?" A roofer who cannot answer that question clearly is likely to go quiet when things get complicated.

After the job, do a final walkthrough with the contractor before they leave. Check the gutters, the landscaping, and the ground around your home for leftover nails. Ask for copies of the permit, the inspection record, the warranty documents, and any manufacturer registration paperwork. Keep those in a folder. If you sell your house, a buyer will want them.
