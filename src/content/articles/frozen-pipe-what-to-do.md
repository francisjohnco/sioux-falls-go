Sioux Falls winters are not gentle. With average snowfall around 38 inches a year and temperatures that routinely drop below zero, frozen pipes are one of the most common calls local plumbers get from December through February. The good news is that if you catch a frozen pipe before it splits, you can often handle it yourself without calling anyone. For a broader look at how South Dakota winters affect your whole plumbing system, see [Plumbing and South Dakota Winter: What Every Sioux Falls Homeowner Should Know](/guides/plumbing-south-dakota-winters-sioux-falls-homeowners), and the [Plumbing in a Sioux Falls Home, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/plumbing-guide-sioux-falls) covers the full picture of what local homes face year-round.

The order of these steps matters. Do not skip ahead.

## Step 1: Find Your Main Water Shutoff Before You Do Anything Else

The first thing to do is not thaw the pipe. It is find the shutoff valve so you can reach it in seconds if the pipe bursts when it thaws.

In most Sioux Falls homes, the main shutoff is in the basement near where the water line enters the foundation wall. Because the city requires a 42-inch frost line depth for buried pipes, your service line comes up through the floor or low on the wall, and the shutoff is usually within a few feet of that point.

If you have never located your shutoff, do it now, before the pipe thaws. Turn it once to confirm it actually closes. Old gate valves can seize up and fail to close fully. If yours does not close, call a plumber before you attempt to thaw anything.

Also identify which fixtures are downstream of the frozen section. That tells you where water will flow if the pipe has already cracked.

## Step 2: Open the Faucet on the Affected Line

Before you apply any heat, open the faucet that the frozen pipe feeds. Leave it open.

As the ice melts, water and steam need somewhere to go. A closed faucet traps pressure, and pressure is what turns a frozen pipe into a burst pipe. Even a slow drip through the open faucet means the thawing is working.

If multiple faucets on that line are not running, open all of them.

## Step 3: Safe Ways to Thaw a Frozen Pipe (and What Never to Use)

Work from the faucet end of the pipe toward the frozen section, not from the middle outward. This lets melting water and steam escape toward the open faucet instead of building pressure in a trapped section.

**Safe thawing methods:**

- Electric heating pad wrapped around the pipe
- Hair dryer on a low or medium setting, kept moving
- Portable electric space heater aimed at the wall or cabinet where the pipe runs
- Warm (not boiling) water poured over towels wrapped around the pipe

**Never use these:**

- Open flame of any kind, including a propane torch or lighter. This is a leading cause of house fires during pipe-thawing attempts.
- Boiling water poured directly onto the pipe. The sudden temperature change can crack the pipe or surrounding fittings.
- Any heat source left unattended.

If the pipe is inside a wall and you cannot reach it directly, warming the room on both sides of that wall is your safest option. A space heater in a cold basement or utility room, left on with the door closed, can raise the ambient temperature enough to thaw a pipe without you touching it at all.

Work slowly. A pipe that froze over several hours may take an hour or more to clear.

## Step 4: If the Pipe Has Already Split

Sometimes you open the cabinet under the sink and find water already running. That means the pipe burst while frozen, and the ice was holding the crack shut until it thawed.

The moment water starts flowing from a split pipe, close the main shutoff. Then open a faucet at a low point in the house (a basement utility sink works well) to drain the pressure out of the lines.

A split pipe is a plumber call, not a weekend project, because the damage is rarely limited to the visible crack. Water that ran inside a wall, under subfloor, or into insulation before you found it can cause mold and structural damage that takes weeks to surface. The guide on [Burst Pipe in the Wall, What Happens Next](/guides/burst-pipe-water-damage) walks through what to expect from that point, including how water damage gets assessed and what the repair sequence usually looks like.

Do not use the water on that line until a licensed plumber has repaired and pressure-tested it.

## When to Call a Plumber for a Frozen Pipe in Sioux Falls

You can try to thaw the pipe yourself in most situations, but call a licensed plumber if:

- You cannot locate the frozen section
- The frozen section is inside a wall, ceiling, or floor with no access
- The pipe has visibly cracked or water is already leaking
- The faucet stays dry after 30 to 45 minutes of careful thawing
- The main shutoff does not close fully
- You smell anything unusual, which could indicate a frozen pipe near a gas line

The City of Sioux Falls requires a city-issued plumbing contractor license for any plumber working in city limits, and does not accept out-of-state licenses. You can verify any contractor at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors before they touch your pipes. This matters especially for emergency calls, when it is tempting to hire anyone who answers the phone fast.

Sioux Falls homeowners have noted in reviews how much it helps when a tech explains the problem and lays out the options before starting work. If a plumber is pushing you toward a larger repair without showing you why, ask to see the problem yourself or get a second opinion.

## How to Prevent the Next Frozen Pipe

Once this one is thawed and repaired, the goal is making sure it does not happen again. The pipes that freeze in Sioux Falls homes are almost always in predictable spots.

**High-risk locations in local homes:**

- Exterior walls in older homes in neighborhoods like McKennan Park or All Saints, where pipe placement does not always account for modern insulation standards
- Crawl spaces and unheated garages
- Cabinets on exterior walls (under kitchen or bathroom sinks)
- Hose bibs and the pipes feeding them

**Prevention steps that actually work:**

- Add insulation around pipes in unheated spaces. Foam pipe sleeves cost a few dollars and install in minutes.
- Leave cabinet doors open under exterior-wall sinks on the coldest nights so heated air reaches the pipes.
- Let a cold-side faucet drip at night when temperatures drop below about 15 degrees Fahrenheit. Moving water is harder to freeze.
- Disconnect and drain garden hoses before the first hard freeze. A hose left connected can hold water pressure back into the wall.
- Know where your shutoff is. Every adult in the house should know, not just the person who handles repairs.

If you are not sure whether your pipes are adequately insulated or whether the placement of a line inside an exterior wall is a code issue in your home, that is a reasonable thing to ask a plumber to look at during a non-emergency visit.
