A broken garage door spring is one of those home problems that announces itself. Most people describe hearing a loud bang from the garage, like a gunshot or something falling off a shelf, and then finding a door that refuses to budge. If that just happened to you, you're in the right place. This article walks through exactly what broke, why you should leave the door alone until a technician arrives, how to get your car out if you need to, and what to expect from a legitimate repair visit. For the full picture on garage door problems and how to handle them, see the [Garage Door Repair in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/garage-door-repair-sioux-falls).

## How to Tell a Garage Door Spring Broke

The most common sign is the sound. Springs store an enormous amount of tension, and when one snaps, the release sounds like a firecracker or a heavy object crashing to the floor. If you heard that and then found your garage door sitting at the bottom, that is almost certainly what happened.

Walk up to the door and look at the horizontal metal rod that runs above it, called the torsion bar. On most Sioux Falls homes with attached garages, you will see one or two tightly wound coils of spring mounted on that bar. If a spring broke, you will see a visible gap in the coil, usually somewhere in the middle. The two halves will be slightly separated, and the break is often visible even from several feet away.

Extension spring systems, which run along the horizontal tracks on each side of the door rather than above it, can also break. With those, you may see a spring hanging loose or coiled on the floor of the garage. If you are not sure which type you have, the article on [torsion vs extension garage door springs](/guides/torsion-vs-extension-garage-door-springs) explains both clearly.

Sioux Falls winters are especially hard on springs. According to SiouxFalls.Business reporting from February 2021, a National Weather Service meteorologist described the February 2021 cold snap as the longest stretch of extreme cold since about 1996. When temperatures drop that far, metal contracts, lubricants thicken, and springs that were already near the end of their service life tend to fail. Many local technicians see a noticeable spike in spring calls after prolonged cold stretches, so if yours broke during or just after a cold snap, you are not alone.

## Why You Should Not Open the Door or Try to Fix It Yourself

This is the most important section in this article. A garage door typically weighs between 150 and 300 pounds. The springs are what counterbalance that weight, allowing a relatively small opener motor (or your arm) to lift it. When a spring breaks, that counterbalance is gone. The door is now essentially dead weight.

If you press the wall button or remote, the opener motor will strain against that full weight. At best, the motor burns out. At worst, the door lurches, cables snap under the uneven load, the door comes down fast and hard, and anything or anyone in its path gets hurt. This is not a theoretical risk. Garage door injuries are a real and documented category of home accident, and they spike in DIY repair attempts.

Replacing a spring requires compressing or winding a new spring to a specific tension using winding bars and knowing exactly how many turns to apply based on the door's weight and spring specifications. Getting that wrong can send the bar flying with serious force. This is why spring replacement belongs on the list of repairs that professionals handle, full stop. For more on that line between safe DIY and genuinely dangerous territory, see [Garage Door Repairs You Should Never Do Yourself](/guides/garage-door-repairs-never-diy).

Do not attempt to prop the door open manually and slide under it. Do not disconnect the opener and try to lift it by hand. Leave the door exactly where it is until a qualified technician arrives.

## How to Get Your Car Out Safely If You Need To

This is the question most people have after they accept that the door needs professional repair. If your car is stuck inside and you need it, here is how to do this without hurting yourself.

First, never try to open the door more than a few inches to see if it moves. If the spring is broken and the door starts moving, you lose control of it quickly. Instead, locate the red emergency release cord that hangs from the trolley above the door. Pulling this cord disconnects the door from the opener carriage, so you can operate the door manually. With a broken spring, the door will be very heavy. You will need a second person to help you lift it and to hold it in the open position while you back the car out. Do not let go of the door and do not stand under it unassisted.

Once the car is out, lower the door carefully and do not try to use the door again until the spring is replaced. If you have an attached garage, consider locking the door from the inside through your house until the repair is done, since a manually released garage door with a broken spring can often be lifted from the outside with minimal effort, which is a real security concern.

If your car is not stuck and you can wait, just leave the door closed and do not touch it. That is genuinely the safest option.

## Why Technicians Often Replace Both Springs at Once

If you have a two-spring torsion system (two coils on the torsion bar), a technician will almost always recommend replacing both springs even if only one broke. This is a reasonable recommendation, not an upsell, and here is why.

Both springs are installed at the same time, and both go through the same number of open and close cycles under similar conditions. If one has worn to the point of failure, the other is usually not far behind. Replacing only the broken one means you are likely calling the same technician back within months for the same job on the other side, paying another service call fee and another labor charge. Replacing both at once costs more upfront, but it is typically less expensive than two separate visits.

There is also a balance issue. Springs are sized by weight capacity, and a new spring paired with a worn spring creates uneven tension on the door. That imbalance puts stress on cables, rollers, and the opener motor. If the technician is recommending paired replacement, ask them to explain the spring specs and why they chose the specific springs for your door weight. A professional should be able to answer that clearly.

## What a Fair Replacement Visit Looks Like in Sioux Falls

When you call for garage door spring replacement in Sioux Falls, here is what a legitimate visit should include.

The technician should give you a written or clearly communicated price before any work begins. That price should cover parts and labor. Ask specifically whether the quote includes both springs if you have a two-spring system, and ask whether the cable inspection and any lubrication is included or billed separately. National ranges for spring replacement vary, and prices in Sioux Falls can differ based on spring type, door weight, and the company you call, so get a written quote and compare it rather than relying on any single number. For more on what affects cost, see [Garage Door Repair Cost in Sioux Falls, Typical Prices](/guides/garage-door-repair-cost-sioux-falls).

On licensing: the City of Sioux Falls licenses residential contractors, and according to the City of Sioux Falls in February 2024 and April 2026, it now also offers a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license for certain smaller jobs on homes and townhomes. The city announcement does not name garage doors specifically, so ask the company which city license it holds. You can verify any contractor on the city's licensed contractor list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors or call Building Services at 605-367-8672. This takes two minutes and tells you whether the company is operating legitimately.

Several Sioux Falls garage door companies offer evening, weekend, or emergency service, while others keep standard weekday hours. Ask about after-hours fees before you book, since emergency rates can be meaningfully higher than standard rates. If the door is closed and secured and you can wait until morning, you may save money by scheduling a standard appointment. If your car is stuck and you need it, or if the door is stuck open and your garage contents are exposed, that is a real emergency and worth the premium.

Finally, a reputable company will test the door balance and opener force settings after the spring is replaced, not just hand you back the remote and leave. The balance test (disconnecting the opener and seeing whether the door stays in place halfway up) confirms the new spring is wound correctly. If a technician skips that step, ask them to do it.
