## Basic HVAC Questions Every Sioux Falls Homeowner Has

**How often should I change my furnace filter?**

Most 1-inch filters need changing every 1 to 3 months. Homes with pets, recent remodeling dust, or multiple people on the upper end of that range.

Sioux Falls winters mean the furnace runs hard from roughly November through March, so a filter that looked fine in September can be clogged by January. Check it monthly and change it when you can no longer see light through it.

For filter ratings and airflow tradeoffs, the [Furnace Filters, Which One and How Often](/guides/furnace-filter-guide) guide walks through every detail.

---

**Why does my house feel so dry in winter?**

Sioux Falls winters are cold and long, and running forced air heat for months drops indoor humidity significantly. Dry air causes static shocks, cracked woodwork, and scratchy throats.

A whole-home humidifier mounted on the furnace is a common fix here, and many HVAC companies install them as an add-on when doing other work. The [Is a Whole-Home Humidifier Worth It in Sioux Falls?](/guides/whole-home-humidifier-sioux-falls) guide covers whether it makes sense for your home and what maintenance looks like.

---

**Do I need a permit for HVAC work in Sioux Falls?**

Yes, in most cases. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, a general building permit does not automatically cover heating and air conditioning work. A separate mechanical permit is required.

Licensed mechanical contractors pull that permit themselves. If you live in and own a single-family home and are doing the work personally, you can pull the permit yourself. You can verify that any contractor you hire holds a current city mechanical license by calling 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list online. Permit records update every Sunday on the city's permit site.

---

**How do I know if a contractor is actually licensed in Sioux Falls?**

Call the city directly at 605-367-8672 or look up the contractor on the City of Sioux Falls Currently Licensed Contractors list. This takes about two minutes and is worth doing before anyone starts work.

Do not rely on a company's website alone. Licenses can lapse. The city list is the authoritative source.

## Furnace FAQ

**My furnace is blowing cold air. What should I check first?**

Start with the basics before calling anyone:

- Check the thermostat setting (heat mode, not cool or fan-only)
- Check or replace the air filter (a clogged filter triggers safety shutoffs)
- Check the furnace switch on the wall near the unit, it looks like a light switch and gets turned off accidentally
- Check the breaker

If the furnace has a blinking light on the front panel, count the flashes. The pattern is a code, and the legend is usually printed inside the furnace door.

[Furnace Not Working? What to Check Before You Call](/guides/furnace-not-working-sioux-falls) walks through every safe check in order, including when to stop and call a technician.

---

**Is it worth repairing my furnace or should I replace it?**

The honest answer depends on the age of the furnace, what the repair costs, and what has already been spent on it. A furnace over 15 to 18 years old with a major repair quote deserves a hard look.

A rough rule some technicians use: if the repair costs more than half the price of a new furnace and the unit is over 15 years old, replacement often makes more sense. But run the numbers for your specific situation.

One important note from homeowners here: the same repair has sometimes been quoted at very different prices by different companies. If a quote surprises you, getting a second opinion is reasonable and normal. See [When to Get a Second Opinion on an HVAC Repair](/guides/hvac-second-opinion) for how to do that without wasting time.

---

**What does a furnace tune-up actually include?**

A real tune-up goes beyond swapping a filter. It should include:

- Inspecting the heat exchanger for cracks
- Checking the burners and flame sensor
- Testing safety controls and limit switches
- Measuring the flue draft
- Inspecting the blower motor and belts

A cracked heat exchanger can let carbon monoxide into the house, which is why the heat exchanger inspection matters. A technician who skips it or rushes through in 20 minutes is not doing a full tune-up.

---

**When should I schedule a furnace checkup?**

Fall, before you need the heat. September and October are the right window in Sioux Falls. Appointment availability gets tight fast once cold weather hits.

Do not wait for a problem to schedule. A tune-up that catches a small issue in October is far cheaper and less stressful than an emergency call in January.

---

**My furnace is making a banging noise when it starts. Is that serious?**

A loud bang or boom at startup usually means delayed ignition, where gas builds up briefly before lighting. This can stress the heat exchanger over time.

It can also mean dirty burners or a gas pressure issue. Either way, it is worth having a technician look at it this season rather than ignoring it.

## AC and Heat Pump Questions

**My AC is running but not cooling the house. What should I check?**

Before calling, check these:

- The air filter (replace it if it has been more than a month or two)
- The thermostat setting and batteries
- The outdoor unit (make sure nothing is blocking airflow around it)
- The breaker for the outdoor condenser

A frozen coil is another common cause. If you see ice on the indoor unit or refrigerant lines, turn the system to fan-only for a few hours to let it thaw, then try again.

If none of that helps, it is time to call. Refrigerant issues and electrical problems need a technician.

---

**Will a heat pump actually keep up during a Sioux Falls winter?**

Older heat pumps struggled below about 25 degrees Fahrenheit, which matters a lot here because Sioux Falls regularly sees temperatures well below that. Modern cold-climate heat pumps are rated to perform much lower, but performance still drops as temperatures fall.

Most heat pump installs in this climate use a dual-fuel setup, pairing the heat pump with a gas furnace backup. The furnace kicks in when it is most efficient to do so. Ask any contractor quoting you a heat pump specifically how the system handles subzero nights.

---

**Can my electrical panel handle a heat pump?**

Not always. Heat pumps require more electrical capacity than a standard AC, and older homes in Sioux Falls sometimes have panels that are already close to their limit. Some homeowners have discovered during the quote process that a panel upgrade is needed, which adds cost.

Ask any contractor doing a heat pump estimate to assess your panel capacity in writing before you agree to anything. Do not assume your current panel is sufficient.

## Getting Work Done

**How do I compare HVAC quotes honestly?**

Get at least two or three written quotes for any major job. Make sure each quote includes:

- The specific equipment model numbers (not just brand names)
- Labor included
- Permit fees
- What is and is not covered

A lower quote that excludes permit fees or uses lower-efficiency equipment is not necessarily a better deal. Line them up side by side.

---

**What should I expect during and after a new furnace or AC installation?**

A full replacement usually takes most of a day. The technician should pull a mechanical permit, protect your floors during the job, do a proper startup and test, and walk you through the new system before leaving.

You should receive paperwork including the permit, warranty registration information, and the manufacturer's documentation. Keep all of it. If a permit was pulled, an inspection will follow, and the city's permit site will show the status.
