Hiring a bathroom remodeler is one of the more personal decisions a homeowner makes. Someone is coming into your house, tearing things apart, and sometimes bringing other workers you've never met. The questions in this article help you sort out who you're actually dealing with before you sign anything. For a full picture of the planning process, start with [Remodeling a Bathroom in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/bathroom-remodel-guide-sioux-falls).

Meeting a remodeler at a home show is a perfectly fine way to start a conversation. It is not a reason to skip comparing bids or asking every question below.

## Licensing and Permits for Bathroom Remodeling in Sioux Falls

The City of Sioux Falls requires a Residential Building Contractor's License for any contractor working on residential one and two family homes and townhomes within city limits. That includes bathroom remodels.

Plumbing work has its own license requirement. According to the City, a plumbing contractor license is required for anyone doing plumbing work inside city limits, and the City does not accept licenses from other states. That matters if a remodeler brings in a plumber from out of the area.

The City publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list you can check yourself. Ask every contractor for their license number and verify it before you sign.

Permits are separate from licensing. The City of Sioux Falls notes that a building permit does not automatically cover electrical, mechanical, or plumbing work. Those trades each require their own permits, pulled by licensed contractors doing that work. If a remodeler tells you permits aren't needed for your project, ask specifically which trades are involved and confirm with City Building Services. Homes outside city limits in Minnehaha County go through the county's Planning and Zoning office instead.

For a deeper look at what triggers a permit in a bathroom project, read [Bathroom Remodel Permits in Sioux Falls, What Needs One](/guides/bathroom-remodel-permits-sioux-falls).

Questions to ask:

- What is your Residential Building Contractor's License number?
- Are you licensed for plumbing, electrical, and mechanical, or are you subcontracting those trades?
- Who pulls the permits, you or me?
- Will all permits be open and inspected before you close the walls?

## Who Will Actually Be in Your Home

One thing homeowners sometimes don't think to ask until it's too late is who, beyond the remodeler they interviewed, will show up on the job.

Subcontractors are common and not a problem in themselves. Tile setters, electricians, and plumbers are often specialists who work on many projects at once. What matters is whether your remodeler has a real working relationship with those people or is calling in someone they've never used before.

A Sioux Falls homeowner reviewing a local remodeling project noted that a painter was brought in midway through the job who the crew had no prior experience with. That kind of situation can affect quality, accountability, and the schedule.

Questions to ask:

- Which parts of the work will you do yourself, and which will subcontractors handle?
- Have you worked with these subcontractors before?
- Are your subs licensed and insured?
- Will I know in advance before anyone new comes into my home?

## Schedule, Cleanup, and Daily Life

The fear of losing a bathroom for weeks keeps a lot of Sioux Falls homeowners from starting a remodel at all. Getting clear answers on schedule upfront won't eliminate surprises, but it will help you plan.

Ask for a written project schedule that shows the major phases. Ask what could cause it to slip and how the contractor handles delays. If you have only one bathroom, that timeline becomes critical. The article on [How Long a Bathroom Remodel Takes and Living Without One](/guides/bathroom-remodel-timeline) walks through the stages and what slows each one.

Cleanup is worth asking about directly. Bathroom dust, grout residue, and debris travel through a house fast. Some crews lay down floor protection and clean daily. Others don't. Find out before they start, not after.

Questions to ask:

- What does the project schedule look like for my specific job?
- How do you protect the rest of the house from dust and debris?
- What happens at the end of each workday, is the space secured and reasonably clean?
- How many other jobs will you be running at the same time?

## Hidden Damage and Change Orders

A bathroom remodel is one of the jobs most likely to uncover something unexpected behind the walls. A repair that starts as cracked grout has turned into rotted subfloor and leaks in several places for more than a few Sioux Falls homeowners. This is not unusual, and it doesn't have to be a disaster if the contractor handles it correctly.

What you want is a clear, written process for how change orders work before work starts. Ask what happens the moment something unexpected is found. A good contractor stops, shows you what they found, explains the options, and gets your approval in writing before the extra work begins. A contractor who keeps going and surprises you with a bigger bill at the end is a problem.

Ask what their markup is on hidden damage repair. Ask if they can show you photos of what they found. And ask how quickly they can get additional materials if, say, more subfloor needs to come out than expected.

Questions to ask:

- How do you handle hidden damage when it comes up?
- Do I see and approve any change order in writing before the work is done?
- Can you show me photos or video of what you found before proceeding?
- What is your markup on materials for unexpected repairs?

## Payment Schedule, Warranty, and the Final Walkthrough

Never pay the full project cost upfront. A reasonable payment schedule is tied to project milestones, not to dates on a calendar. A large initial deposit, a mid-project payment when rough work is done and inspected, and a final payment only after a completed walkthrough is a structure worth asking for.

Ask what the warranty covers and get it in writing. Ask specifically whether the warranty covers labor, materials, or both, and for how long. Ask what happens if a tile cracks six months after installation or a fixture leaks.

The final walkthrough is where you catch everything before you hand over the last check. Run every fixture. Flush the toilet. Run the shower and the tub. Turn on every fan and confirm it actually vents to the outside, not just into the attic or a wall cavity. Check the ceiling or the basement below the bathroom for any sign of drips. Remove the showerhead and check inside for leftover tape or debris, a small thing that homeowners have found after the fact.

A written punch list you both sign off on protects you and gives the contractor a clear finish line.

Questions to ask:

- What does the payment schedule look like, and what does each payment correspond to?
- What does your warranty cover, for how long, and is it in writing?
- Will you walk through the completed job with me before I make the final payment?
- Who do I call if something is wrong after you're done?

Final walkthrough checklist before signing off:

- Run every faucet, the shower, and the tub
- Flush the toilet and check around the base
- Turn on the exhaust fan and confirm it vents outside
- Check the ceiling or basement below for drips
- Remove and inspect the showerhead for tape or debris left inside
- Open and close every door and drawer
- Look at grout and caulk lines for gaps or voids
- Confirm all permits have been closed out with the City
