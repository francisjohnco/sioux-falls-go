Electricians are state-licensed in South Dakota, which makes verification the easiest and most important first step — more so than with almost any other home service, since electrical work done wrong is a genuine safety risk, not just a quality-of-work issue.

## Start with verification, not price

Ask for a license number before you ask about pricing. A licensed electrician should provide this without hesitation. Also confirm liability insurance — this protects your home if something goes wrong during the work, not just the electrician doing the job.

## Confirm they pull permits

The City of Sioux Falls requires permits for panel upgrades, new circuit installation, and most rewiring work. An electrician who routinely skips this to move faster is cutting a corner that becomes your problem later — unpermitted electrical work can complicate a home sale and, more importantly, may not have been inspected for safety.

## Questions worth asking directly

1. **"Will you pull a permit for this job, and is that included in the quote?"** If the answer is vague, that's worth noting.
2. **"Have you worked on homes from [your home's era] before?"** Older Sioux Falls neighborhoods often have wiring that behaves differently than modern homes — real experience with that era matters.
3. **"Is this quote based on what you can see, or could it change once the panel is open?"** Especially relevant for panel work, where hidden issues are common.
4. **"What's your warranty on labor?"** A standard warranty period is normal; if there isn't one, ask why.

## Red flags

- No license number offered, or reluctance to provide one
- A bid dramatically lower than every other quote with no explanation
- Skipping permits as a matter of course, framed as "saving you money"
- No verifiable business address or online presence beyond a phone number

## Why this matters more for electrical work specifically

A bad plumbing repair usually just means a leak. Bad electrical work can mean a fire risk that isn't visible until it's a real problem. That's the reason licensing verification and permit compliance matter more here than they might for a lower-stakes home service — it's worth the extra five minutes to check.
