You get three flooring quotes and they are nowhere close to each other. One is hundreds of dollars higher than another, and you are not sure if someone is padding the number or someone else is leaving things out. Both can be true at the same time.

Before you decide, it helps to know exactly what goes into a flooring quote and why the numbers legitimately differ. The [New Floors in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/flooring-guide-sioux-falls) covers the full process from material selection through installation. This article focuses on the quote itself: what the line items mean, why square footage numbers vary, and how to read two quotes side by side so you are comparing the same thing.

## Why Measured Square Footage Varies Between Quotes

This is the most common source of confusion. Sioux Falls homeowners have reported getting quotes where one company measured roughly 100 more square feet than another on the same house. That is not necessarily a mistake.

Flooring installers add a waste factor on top of your actual floor area. The standard range is 10 to 15 percent for a simple rectangular room with a straight-lay pattern. It goes higher for diagonal installs, herringbone, natural hardwood with more character cuts, or rooms with a lot of angles.

Stairs are measured differently by every installer. Some quote each step individually, some group them. Some include the riser (the vertical face), some do not, and some charge a separate per-step labor rate on top of the material.

Here is what legitimately makes measured square footage differ:

- Waste factor percentage (10 percent vs. 15 percent adds up fast in a whole-house job)
- Whether closets are included in full or estimated
- How stair landings are calculated
- Whether each bathroom gets rounded up to a minimum order quantity
- Diagonal or pattern cuts that require more waste

Ask every installer to show you their measured square footage before the waste factor, and what percentage they added. Then the numbers become comparable.

## What Line Items Should Appear in a Flooring Quote

A quote that just says "LVP installed, whole house, $X" is not a quote you can evaluate. You need to see the job broken into parts before you can tell whether two bids are actually close.

A properly itemized flooring quote should show:

- Material cost per square foot and total square footage ordered
- Labor cost (sometimes per square foot, sometimes per room or per day)
- Subfloor prep, leveling, or repair if needed
- Old floor removal and disposal
- Furniture moving, if the installer includes it
- Transition strips (the metal or wood pieces where flooring meets a different surface or a doorway)
- Stair installation listed separately, usually per step or per flight
- Underlayment, if not included in the material price

Transitions are a line item that frequently disappears from low quotes and reappears as a surprise charge on the final invoice. A whole-house job in a typical Sioux Falls home can involve a dozen or more transitions between rooms, hallways, and bathrooms. Ask for that count upfront.

## Removal, Disposal, and Furniture Moving

Removing old carpet in a newly purchased house is one of the most common flooring jobs in Sioux Falls, and it is also one of the most variable line items in quotes. Some installers include demo and haul-away. Others charge per room. Others expect you to have the floor bare before they arrive.

Furniture moving is handled inconsistently. Some crews move everything and move it back. Others move furniture to one side, do half the room, then move it again. Some will not move electronics, aquariums, or beds, and expect those cleared before the crew arrives.

None of these approaches is wrong, but if one quote includes furniture moving and another does not, the lower quote may require a full day of your own labor before the crew even shows up.

Ask directly:

- Do you remove and haul the old flooring, and is that in this quote?
- What do you need cleared before you arrive?
- Do you move and replace furniture, or is that my responsibility?

## Stairs, Transitions, and the Small Jobs That Add Up

Stairs deserve their own attention because they are consistently the most labor-intensive part of a flooring job and the most inconsistently quoted part. Installing LVP or hardwood on stairs involves cutting each tread and riser to fit, nosing pieces that overhang the step edge, and dealing with any existing carpet staples or adhesive.

A quote that covers your entire main floor but seems suspiciously low may simply have left the stairs out of the labor total. Get a stair count on paper.

Transitions are similar. Every doorway where your new floor meets a different floor or a different height needs a transition strip. That includes:

- Doorways between rooms with the same flooring
- Doorways between new flooring and tile (bathrooms, laundry)
- The threshold at exterior doors
- The base of stairs where the flooring ends

The strips themselves are not expensive individually, but the labor to cut and fit each one adds time. If a quote lists "transitions included" with no count, ask how many are assumed in the price.

## How to Actually Compare Two Flooring Quotes

Once you have itemized quotes, put them in a simple side-by-side list. You are looking for three things: what is included, what is excluded, and whether the same material is being priced.

Material brand and product line matter more than people realize. Two quotes may both say "LVP" but one is a 6-millimeter product with a 12-mil wear layer and one is an 8-millimeter product with a 20-mil wear layer. Those are not comparable. Ask for the full product name or SKU so you can look them up.

A quick comparison checklist:

- Same product or comparable products?
- Same square footage before waste?
- Waste factor percentage stated?
- Removal and disposal included in both?
- Furniture moving included in both?
- Transitions counted and priced in both?
- Stairs listed separately in both?
- Subfloor prep included, or conditional on what they find?

That last item, subfloor prep, is worth a conversation before any contract is signed. Sioux Falls homes with expansive clay soil under the slab can have uneven concrete that needs grinding or leveling compound before flooring goes down. If one installer includes a contingency for that and another does not, they are quoting different scopes of work.
