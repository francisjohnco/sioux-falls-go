A flooded basement feels urgent because it is urgent. Every hour standing water sits against your framing, drywall, and belongings pushes you closer to a mold problem that is far harder and more expensive to fix than the water itself.

This checklist walks you through the first several hours, from the moment you discover the water to the point where a professional crew takes over. For the full picture of what comes next, including professional drying and rebuilding, see the main guide: [Water Damage in Your Sioux Falls Home, What to Do](/guides/water-damage-guide-sioux-falls).

## Step 1: Do Not Go In Until You Know It Is Safe

This is not a small caution. It is the step that matters most.

Water and electricity in a basement can kill you before you realize there is a problem. If your electrical panel, water heater, furnace, or any outlets are in the basement, do not step in until you have cut power at the main breaker, which is usually on the main floor or in a detached garage. If you are not sure where it is or if the panel itself is already wet, call your utility or a licensed electrician before you enter.

Gas is a separate risk. If you smell anything like rotten eggs, leave the house immediately and call MidAmerican Energy or your gas provider from outside. Do not flip any switches on the way out.

Once power is confirmed off and there is no gas smell, you can enter carefully. Wear rubber-soled boots if you have them. Watch for uneven or slippery flooring.

## Step 2: Stop the Source of the Water

Standing water you cannot stop will keep rising. Figure out where it is coming from before anything else.

Common sources in Sioux Falls basements:

- Sump pump failure, especially after spring snowmelt or heavy rain
- A burst or leaking pipe inside the wall or at a fixture
- Water heater or appliance failure
- Groundwater seeping in through the foundation or floor drain backing up

If it is a plumbing source, shut off the main water valve immediately. In most Sioux Falls homes the main shutoff is near the water meter, often on the front foundation wall closest to the street. Know where yours is before you need it.

If it is sump pump failure, the pump may need to be replaced or temporarily supplemented with a submersible utility pump you can rent. If you are not sure what caused the flooding, [Sump Pump Failure, Prevention and Backup Options](/guides/sump-pump-failure-sioux-falls) explains what to look for and what backup systems can prevent a repeat.

## Step 3: Document Everything Before You Touch It

Insurance adjusters work from evidence. Your phone is your most important tool right now.

Take photos and short videos of:

- The water level and how far it has spread
- The source of the water if visible (a broken pipe, the sump pit, the water heater)
- Every affected wall, floor area, and piece of furniture or stored property
- The electrical panel and mechanical equipment if they were exposed

Do this before you move anything. Once you start clearing items or running a wet vac, the original condition is gone.

The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to photograph all damage and keep a written log of every contact with their insurer, including dates, names, and what was said. Keep all of that in one place, whether it is a folder on your phone or a notes app.

## Step 4: Protect What You Can Move Quickly

After documenting, move salvageable belongings out of the water.

Prioritize in this order:

- Documents, photos, and anything irreplaceable
- Electronics and appliances not already submerged
- Furniture on feet that can be moved to dry ground
- Area rugs (wet rugs on a wood subfloor accelerate damage to the floor itself)

Do not throw anything away yet. Your insurer may need to see damaged items in person, or at minimum in photos. The South Dakota Division of Insurance guidance is clear: do not make permanent repairs or dispose of damaged property before contacting the insurer.

Temporary protective actions, like pulling a wet rug off the floor or placing wood blocks under furniture legs, are fine and expected. Keep any receipts for materials you buy.

## Step 5: Call Your Insurance Company

Call as soon as the scene is safe and documented. Do not wait until the next business day if it is a weekend.

A few things to know before you call:

- Homeowner policies generally cover sudden water damage from inside the home (a burst pipe, an appliance failure) but do not cover groundwater or flooding from outside. That requires a separate flood policy. Ask your agent directly which situation you have and what is covered.
- Policies can have notice deadlines. The South Dakota Division of Insurance says to file promptly and to read your policy carefully for any timeframes.
- Ask whether your policy covers water backup from a drain or sump, which is a separate endorsement many Sioux Falls homeowners carry but do not realize they have.
- Insurers are required to respond to claim communications within 30 days under South Dakota rules.

For a full breakdown of how to navigate the claim process, see [Water Damage Insurance Claims, What Homeowners Should Know](/guides/water-damage-insurance-claim).

## Step 6: Start Drying as Fast as Possible

Mold can begin to develop within 24 to 48 hours in the right conditions. A Sioux Falls basement in summer is exactly those conditions: warm, humid, and dark.

If a professional restoration crew cannot arrive immediately, open windows if the outside air is drier than the inside air, run any dehumidifiers you have, and get a box fan moving air across wet surfaces. These steps slow the problem; they do not replace professional drying equipment.

Professional drying uses industrial air movers and commercial dehumidifiers that pull far more moisture from structural materials than consumer equipment can. Sioux Falls clay soils mean many homes here have concrete block or poured concrete walls that hold moisture longer than you would expect from the surface drying out.

Do not assume a floor or wall is dry because it looks dry. Moisture inside framing, insulation, and subfloor materials is what leads to hidden mold weeks later. A restoration company will use moisture meters to track actual readings, not appearances. If you want to understand what that process looks like from start to finish, [How Professional Drying Works After Water Damage](/guides/drying-process-explained) explains the equipment and the timeline in plain terms.
