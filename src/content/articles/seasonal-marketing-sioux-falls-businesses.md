Sioux Falls has real seasons, and so does almost every business here. Lawn care disappears under snow. Furnace calls spike in January. Patios fill up in June, and shops live for December.

The businesses that grow steadily do not market the same way every month. They match their effort to their season, and they use the slow months to get ready for the busy ones.

## The Big Idea, Build in the Slow Season

Some marketing works right away. Ads and promotions can bring calls within days.

Other marketing builds slowly and then keeps working: your Google Business Profile, reviews, website content, and your reputation. These take months to grow, which is why waiting for the busy season to start is too late.

So the rhythm looks like this:

- **Slow season:** build the long-term assets, gather reviews, and fix what is weak.
- **Lead-up:** turn on reminders, offers, and ads before demand arrives.
- **Busy season:** stay visible, respond fast, and ask every happy customer for a review.
- **After the rush:** follow up, ask for referrals, and plan the next cycle.

## Home Services and Trades

Lawn care, landscaping, roofing, painting, concrete, and exterior work tend to peak from spring through fall. HVAC peaks at both extremes, while plumbing and drain calls often rise when pipes are tested by cold.

**Winter (December to February).** Update your Google Business Profile with photos from last season. Ask fall customers for reviews. Write or update service pages for what you will sell in spring. This is the best time to fix your website.

**Late winter into spring (February to April).** Send reminders to past customers before your competitors do. Start ads a few weeks before demand, not after. Post spring offers and booking dates on your profile.

**Peak season.** Answer calls and messages fast, because speed wins jobs. Ask for a review after every job, while the result is fresh.

**Fall.** Follow up with everyone you served. Offer end-of-season services and book early spring work. Then start the cycle again.

## Restaurants, Cafes, and Bars

Demand is steadier, with spikes you can plan around: holidays, graduation season, summer patio months, and big events downtown.

- **Plan around the calendar.** Holiday gift cards in November and December, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, graduation parties, and summer evenings.
- **Keep your Google profile current.** Holiday hours, menus, and photos change constantly, and wrong hours cost you customers.
- **Lean on reviews.** Diners read them constantly, so ask happy guests and reply to every review.
- **Use slow months for loyalty.** Email or text your regulars with a reason to come in on a quiet Tuesday.

## Retail and Shops

The fourth quarter matters most for many shops, with smaller peaks around back-to-school, Valentine's Day, and local events.

- **Start holiday visibility early.** Update your profile, photos, and website in October, before shoppers start searching.
- **Feature what is in stock**, with real photos, so people searching for gifts find you.
- **Build your list year round.** Customers who join your email or text list in summer are the ones you reach first in December.

## Professional Services

Accountants, lawyers, insurance, real estate, and health practices have their own seasons, like tax season, open enrollment, and spring moving season.

The same rule applies. Build content and reviews in the quiet months, then remind past clients and start promotions before your peak.

## Use the Calendar Around You

Sioux Falls gives local businesses plenty of moments to show up: festivals, holiday events downtown, youth sports seasons, school calendars, and community fundraisers.

Being visible at these moments, through sponsorships, timely posts, or offers, keeps your name in front of people when they are out in the community. Read [Do Community Sponsorships Pay Off for Sioux Falls Businesses?](/guides/community-sponsorships-sioux-falls-business) before you commit to one.

## The Mistake to Avoid

The most common mistake is cutting everything in the slow season. Spending on ads for a service nobody needs in January makes no sense. But stopping the long-term work hands the spring to competitors who kept going.

Cut what only works in the busy season. Keep building the things that compound.

## Put It on One Page

Take a sheet of paper and mark your three busiest months and your three slowest. Next to the slow months, write what you will build. Next to the month before your rush, write when reminders and ads start.
