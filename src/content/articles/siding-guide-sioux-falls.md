Replacing siding is one of the biggest exterior projects a Sioux Falls homeowner can take on, and it rarely happens on a calm, planned schedule. Most of the time it starts with a hailstorm, a rotten board found during a paint job, or an insurance adjuster's visit. This guide walks through every stage, from that first worried walk around the house to the final cleanup, so you know what to expect, what to ask, and what to watch out for.

## After a Hailstorm, Start Here

Sioux Falls sits in one of the more hail-active corridors in the country, and a single storm can send contractors knocking on doors within hours.

Before you talk to anyone, walk your property yourself.

Look at every side of the house, not just the one facing the storm. Hail damage on siding shows up differently depending on the material:

- Vinyl: cracks, splits, or small circular fractures
- Fiber cement: chipping, exposed raw edges, dents in paint
- Engineered wood: crushed surface, areas where the top layer lifted
- Aluminum: dents and dings that are easy to see once you know where to look

Take photos dated and timestamped before anything is touched.

Then check the gutters, window wells, windowsills, and any painted wood trim, because those surfaces show impact marks that confirm a storm was severe enough to damage siding.

After you have documented everything yourself, read through [Hail Damaged Siding, What to Check First](/guides/hail-damaged-siding) for a room-by-room walkthrough and guidance on when to call your insurer.

## Repair or Replace? How to Think Through the Decision

Not every damaged or tired-looking wall needs full replacement, and not every repair job is the right call either.

A few panels cracked by hail, a small area of rot at a corner trim, or one wall that took more sun damage than the rest, those can sometimes be fixed without touching the whole house.

But there are situations where repair is the wrong move:

- The siding is more than 20 to 25 years old and showing widespread wear
- Moisture has gotten behind the panels and is showing up inside walls
- You cannot match the color or profile of existing panels (more on that below)
- An insurance claim will cover replacement, making repair a costly partial fix

The key question is whether you are spending money on a problem or on a solution. [Siding Repair or Replacement? How to Decide](/guides/siding-repair-or-replace) goes deeper on each of these scenarios, including what a contractor should be checking behind the surface before recommending one or the other.

## Choosing a Material That Fits Sioux Falls Weather

South Dakota's climate is not gentle on the outside of a house. Sioux Falls gets plenty of snow every winter, with freeze-thaw cycles that stress any material repeatedly through winter and spring. Summers bring heat, UV exposure, and those hailstorms.

The three materials most commonly used for siding replacement here are vinyl, fiber cement, and engineered wood, and each handles the local climate differently.

**Vinyl** is by far the most common choice in Sioux Falls. It does not rot, it holds color reasonably well, and it is easier to replace individual panels when hail hits. Cold weather is the main vulnerability: vinyl becomes more brittle below freezing, which matters both for storm damage and for installation timing.

**Fiber cement** is heavier, holds paint very well, and resists impact better than vinyl. It costs more and requires a skilled crew for installation and painting. It also needs to be kept off the ground and properly flashed because it can absorb moisture at cut edges.

**Engineered wood** looks closest to real wood and can add to resale appeal, but it requires careful installation and maintenance to prevent moisture infiltration.

For a detailed comparison of how each material performs against hail, cold, wind, and sun, see [Vinyl, Fiber Cement or Engineered Wood Siding?](/guides/vinyl-vs-fiber-cement-siding).

## What Goes Under the Siding Matters as Much as What Goes Over It

A lot of homeowners focus entirely on the panel they can see, and contractors who want to cut costs know that. What goes behind the siding, the house wrap, flashing, and any insulation board, is where long-term performance is decided.

House wrap acts as a water-resistive barrier that keeps any moisture that gets behind the panels from reaching the sheathing. If a previous installation skipped it, used the wrong product, or lapped it incorrectly, you may already have moisture damage that only shows up when panels come off.

Flashing at every window, door, and penetration is equally important. Gaps there are how water finds its way into wall cavities, where it sits and causes rot and mold that is expensive to fix.

If your contractor does not mention house wrap and flashing as line items in their scope of work, ask directly what they plan to install and how. This is not an optional layer.

[What Goes Under Your Siding and Why It Matters](/guides/what-goes-under-siding) covers each layer in plain language so you know what to look for in a proposal and what questions to ask on the job site.

## Permits and Licensed Contractors in Sioux Falls

Re-siding a home in Sioux Falls requires a building permit, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs. This is not a gray area or something that depends on how much work you are doing: replacing the siding on a residential home requires a permit.

The contractor doing the work must hold a City of Sioux Falls Residential Building Contractor's License, or, for projects limited to re-siding with no structural changes, the City's Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license, which was created in 2024 specifically to cover re-shingling, re-siding, and window or door replacement. The City does not accept licenses from other states.

You can confirm any contractor is currently licensed before signing anything by checking the City's publicly available list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.

This matters especially after hail, because out-of-state storm chasers who arrive after major weather events are not eligible to pull permits here and cannot legally do the work. If a contractor cannot show up on the City's licensed list, that is a clear reason to walk away.

For everything you need to know about the permit process and how to verify a contractor, read [Siding Permits and Licensed Contractors in Sioux Falls](/guides/siding-permits-licensed-contractors).

## Working Through an Insurance Claim

If a storm caused the damage, your homeowner's insurance may cover some or all of the replacement cost. The process is more involved than most homeowners expect.

A few things to know before you call your insurer:

- Document damage thoroughly before anything is touched or repaired
- Your contractor can and should be present when the adjuster walks the property
- Adjusters can miss damage, particularly on less visible surfaces, and your contractor can flag items on your behalf
- Depreciation, deductibles, and matching disputes are common friction points

The matching question comes up frequently in Sioux Falls when only part of the house was damaged. If your insurer will only pay to replace the affected walls, but the existing color is discontinued or has faded significantly, getting a reasonable match may be difficult or impossible. How insurers handle matching varies by policy and adjuster.

Work through each stage of a claim carefully and get everything in writing. [Siding and Your Insurance Claim After a Storm](/guides/siding-insurance-claim) covers documentation, the adjuster visit, and how to handle disputes.

## Getting and Comparing Quotes

Once you know roughly what scope of work you need, get at least three written quotes. A written quote is not the same as a rough number on the phone.

A complete quote should include:

- Total square footage of siding being replaced, including trim, soffit, and fascia if applicable
- Removal and disposal of existing siding
- House wrap and flashing as separate line items
- Material, profile, and color specified
- Warranty terms for both material and labor
- Payment schedule and project timeline

Bids that look dramatically cheaper often leave out one or more of these items. You cannot compare quotes that are not scoped the same way.

Do not pay a large deposit upfront. A reasonable deposit to order materials is normal; paying most of the job before work starts is not.

[How to Compare Siding Quotes](/guides/comparing-siding-quotes) gives you a checklist to put every proposal side by side so the differences are clear.

## Matching New Siding to What Stays

If only part of your house is being replaced, color and profile matching becomes a real challenge. Vinyl colors fade over years of Sioux Falls sun exposure, and many profiles and colors are discontinued by manufacturers.

Even a new panel in the same listed color may look noticeably different next to panels that have been on the house for eight or ten years.

Options contractors use:

- Sample boards held against existing panels in natural light
- Replacing entire walls rather than individual panels to minimize visible seams
- Negotiating with insurers for expanded scope when matching is not feasible

Bring this up early in conversations with any contractor. If they dismiss the concern, that is a warning sign. [Matching New Siding to Old After a Repair](/guides/matching-siding-colors) explains how fading works, what your options are, and how insurers typically view matching disputes.

## Timing, Storm Chasers, and Taking Care of What You Install

Siding can be installed in any season in Sioux Falls, but cold weather affects vinyl specifically, making it more likely to crack during installation if crews are not adjusting their practices. If you are scheduling work in winter, ask your contractor directly how they handle cold-weather installation.

After installation, the work is not entirely done. Sioux Falls freeze-thaw cycles and spring moisture mean siding needs regular attention: checking caulk around windows and doors each fall, washing off mildew before it stains, and looking for any panels that have shifted or cracked after a severe storm.

One more thing worth repeating: be cautious of anyone who knocks on your door within days of a hailstorm, cannot show you a City of Sioux Falls license, or pressures you to sign anything before you have had time to think. These are the contractors local homeowners consistently warn others about in their reviews, and the pressure tactics are a reason to end the conversation.

Take the time to check the licensed list, get competing quotes, and read your insurance policy before committing to anything.
