Flooring install day has a way of sneaking up on homeowners. You picked the material, got the quote, and scheduled the crew, and then suddenly it is the week before and you are not sure what you are supposed to do before they show up.

This checklist walks you through what happens before, during, and after the install so nothing catches you off guard. If you are still deciding on material or figuring out the full scope of your project, start with the [New Floors in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/flooring-guide-sioux-falls) first.

## Preparing Your Home Before the Crew Arrives

The single biggest thing you can do is clear the rooms. Most installers will move heavy furniture for an added fee, but smaller items, lamps, breakables, wall art, and anything on the floor, are almost always your responsibility.

Ask your installer specifically what they will and will not move before the job starts. Get the answer in writing if furniture moving is included in the quote, because that detail disappears fast if there is a dispute later.

A few other things to handle the day before:

- Remove all rugs, floor mats, and door mats
- Clear out closets if closet floors are being done
- Take down any floor-length curtains or drapes
- Move pets to a room that is not being worked on, or arrange for them to be out of the house
- Make sure the crew has a clear path from your front door (or garage) to the work area

If you have a newer home in a neighborhood like All Saints or the southeast growth corridor, also confirm where the crew plans to stage materials. Tight driveways and HOA rules on street parking can complicate morning logistics more than people expect.

## Acclimation: Why the Floor May Arrive Before the Crew Does

Many flooring materials need to acclimate to your home's temperature and humidity before installation. Hardwood typically needs 3 to 7 days sitting inside the house. LVP products vary by manufacturer, so ask specifically for your product.

This matters more in Sioux Falls than in a lot of other markets. Our winters regularly pull indoor humidity down into the 20 to 30 percent range, which causes wood and wood-based products to shrink. If the material goes in cold and dry and then adjusts to your living conditions, you end up with gaps. The article [Why Floors Gap and Cup in Sioux Falls Winters](/guides/winter-flooring-gaps-humidity) covers what happens when acclimation gets skipped and what to watch for afterward.

Ask your installer:

- How many days does this product need to acclimate?
- Will you stage it in the room where it will be installed?
- What temperature should I keep the house at during acclimation and install?

A reasonable general target for most installs is between 65 and 75 degrees Fahrenheit, but confirm the range for your specific product.

## Dust, Fumes, and VOCs: Plan for Real Disruption

Every flooring install creates some disruption. The type depends on the material.

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) and laminate generate dust from cutting, mostly along baseboards. It is fine dust and it travels. Close doors to adjacent rooms and cover any HVAC returns in the work area if you are sensitive to dust or if you have people in the home with respiratory issues.

Adhesives and certain finish coatings produce volatile organic compounds (VOCs). For most glue-down installs and anything with a site-applied finish, plan to stay out of the work area for at least a few hours. Hardwood refinishing with oil-based finishes is the most significant case, where off-gassing can require the room to air out for one to two days. Water-based finishes off-gas faster, often clearing in a few hours, but still require ventilation. If you are having hardwood refinished rather than replaced, [Refinishing Hardwood Floors, Is It Worth It?](/guides/hardwood-refinishing-sioux-falls) covers what to ask about timing and finish types.

Practical steps regardless of material:

- Open windows on the day of install if outdoor temps allow
- Run exhaust fans
- Keep children and pets out of the space until the smell clears
- Ask your installer what their specific product's VOC clearance time is

Do not let an installer brush past this question. It is a reasonable thing to ask and a good installer will have a straight answer.

## Protecting Walls and Trim During Installation

This is one of the most common complaints homeowners share after installs: scuffs, nicks, and gouges along walls and door frames that were not there before the crew arrived.

Before work starts, walk the space with your crew lead and point out anything already marked or damaged. Take photos with your phone. This protects both you and the installer.

Some things to know:

- Baseboards are often removed during install and reinstalled afterward. Ask whether this is included in your quote or billed separately.
- When baseboards come off, they can take paint with them on older walls. That is normal but good to expect.
- Door frames and jambs sometimes need to be undercut so the new floor slides underneath cleanly. A good installer will do this, but it is worth confirming.
- Transition strips between rooms or between different flooring types are usually installed at the end. Ask whether they are included or whether you need to purchase them separately.

If installers are working in a finished basement, the tight stairwells and low clearances typical in Sioux Falls homes from the 1960s to 1990s make wall damage more likely. Pad corners yourself with cardboard or foam if you are concerned.

## Timelines: What a Day (or Two) Actually Looks Like

Homeowners sometimes plan for two days and the crew finishes in one. Sometimes it goes the other direction. Sioux Falls homeowners have noted in public reviews that finishing a whole basement, stairs, and multiple bathrooms in a single day was not what they expected.

A realistic way to think about it:

- A single medium-size room (say, 200 to 300 square feet) of LVP click-lock over a prepared subfloor often takes half a day to a full day
- Stairs add significant time per step because each tread and riser is cut and fitted individually
- Removal of old flooring, if included, adds time before installation even starts
- Concrete subfloor prep (grinding high spots, filling low spots) can add hours or push the job to a second day

Ask your installer before the job starts what the planned schedule looks like and what could push it into an additional day. That way you can plan childcare, work from home arrangements, or pet care accordingly.

## The Final Walkthrough: What to Check Before the Crew Leaves

Do not skip this step. Walk every inch of the installed floor before you sign off or make a final payment.

Look for:

- Gaps along walls that are wider than the baseboard will cover
- Transition strips that feel loose or are not fully seated
- Planks that click or move when you step on them (a sign of improper locking or a subfloor low spot underneath)
- Scratches or chips on the surface from installation tools
- Any damage to walls, trim, or door frames that was not there before

If something is wrong, note it on the spot and ask the crew lead what the correction process is. Get any promises about return visits in writing, even just a text or email confirmation.

After the job, keep your paperwork: the product SKU, the dye lot number if applicable, and the installer's warranty terms. If you ever need to replace a plank or settle a warranty question, that information is what makes it possible. The article [Flooring Warranties and After-Install Service](/guides/flooring-warranty-and-service) explains exactly what those documents cover and what to watch for in the fine print.
