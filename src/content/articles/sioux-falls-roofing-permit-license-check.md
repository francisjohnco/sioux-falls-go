Getting a new roof or major repair in Sioux Falls involves more than picking a contractor and writing a check. The city requires both a building permit and a licensed contractor for shingle work on homes, and skipping either one can create real problems when you sell, file a claim, or discover defective work later. This article covers exactly what is required, how to verify it, and what happens if something goes wrong. For a broader look at the full replacement or repair process, start with the [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls).

## What the City of Sioux Falls Actually Requires

According to the City of Sioux Falls, shingle work on homes requires a building permit. That is not optional, and it applies even when a storm chaser quotes you a fast turnaround before you have time to think.

The contractor doing the work must also hold a current city license, specifically the Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license or the broader Residential Building Contractor license. These are not state licenses, they are city-issued.

That city license comes with real requirements attached:

- A $20,000 compliance bond made to the City of Sioux Falls
- At least $300,000 in general liability insurance

The bond and insurance are what protect you if the contractor damages your property, disappears mid-job, or does work that fails inspection. Without them, your only real option is a lawsuit.

If you want to confirm whether a contractor holds a current city license, you can call Building Services licensing directly at 605-367-8670. That call takes five minutes and can save you a serious headache.

## What the Permit Actually Does for You

A lot of homeowners hear "permit" and think it just means paperwork and fees. But a Sioux Falls roofing permit does something more important than that: it triggers an inspection.

That inspection gives you an independent check that the work meets building code. For a roof, that matters because a failed installation can void your shingle manufacturer's warranty, cause ice dam problems in Sioux Falls winters, and become an issue the next time you file an insurance claim.

The permit also creates a public record. When you sell your home, buyers and their agents can verify that work was done with a permit and passed inspection. Unpermitted work is a red flag in a sale and can delay or kill a deal.

Permits are the contractor's responsibility to pull, not yours. If a contractor tells you that you need to handle the permit yourself, or that "this job doesn't need one," that is worth questioning directly. For shingle work in the city, the permit requirement is clear.

## How to Verify a Contractor Before Signing Anything

Checking a contractor's credentials takes less time than most people expect. Here is what to do before you agree to anything:

- Call 605-367-8670 (City of Sioux Falls Building Services licensing) and ask if the contractor holds a current Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license or Residential Building Contractor license.
- Ask the contractor directly for their city license number. A legitimate contractor will give it to you without hesitation.
- Ask to see a current certificate of insurance showing at least $300,000 in general liability coverage. The certificate should name the City of Sioux Falls as a certificate holder, or at minimum show a current policy date.
- Ask the South Dakota Attorney General's office what they recommend: the Attorney General's consumer protection office advises homeowners to also ask for a current South Dakota sales or contractor's excise tax license. You can reach consumer protection at 1-800-300-1986.

Do not skip the verification step just because someone knocked on your door after a storm and seems friendly. The South Dakota Attorney General specifically warns homeowners after hailstorms not to be pressured into quick agreements and to ask for proof of bond and insurance before signing anything.

## What Happens With Unpermitted Work

If a contractor completes roofing work without pulling a permit, you as the homeowner can be held responsible for bringing it into compliance later. That can mean paying for an inspection after the fact, potentially tearing off completed work so an inspector can verify what is underneath, and paying any fines assessed.

Unpermitted work also creates problems with your homeowner's insurance. If you file a future claim and the insurer discovers prior work was done without a permit, they may dispute the claim or reduce the payout on the basis that the work was not code-compliant.

For homes being sold, unpermitted work typically shows up in a title search or buyer's inspection. It can require the seller to either remediate it or negotiate a price reduction. Neither is a good position to be in.

The short version: the permit protects you far more than it inconveniences you. A contractor who pushes back on pulling one is signaling something worth taking seriously.

## Homes Outside Sioux Falls City Limits

If your home is in Harrisburg, Tea, Brandon, or another city near Sioux Falls, the requirements above are specific to the City of Sioux Falls. Each municipality has its own permitting and licensing rules.

If you are in an unincorporated area of Minnehaha or Lincoln County, the county itself may have building requirements, or in some cases none at all. That does not mean you should skip verification; it means you need to contact your specific jurisdiction to find out what applies.

Regardless of where you live, the basic verification steps still make sense: confirm the contractor carries liability insurance, has a bond, and can show you a contract in writing before any money changes hands. The South Dakota Attorney General's tips apply statewide, not just inside Sioux Falls.

## Questions to Ask Your Contractor Before Work Starts

Here is a short list of questions worth asking any roofer before you agree to the job:

- Do you hold a current City of Sioux Falls Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license? What is the license number?
- Will you pull the building permit for this job?
- Can I see your current certificate of general liability insurance?
- Do you carry a $20,000 compliance bond with the city?
- Do you have a current South Dakota contractor's excise tax license?
- Will you provide a written contract that lists the full scope of work and total price before we start?

If a contractor hesitates or gets defensive on any of these, that is useful information. A contractor who is properly licensed and bonded has nothing to hide on these questions.
