Small bathrooms are the rule in many Sioux Falls homes, especially the bungalows and older two-stories in neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints. The challenge is not just finding a vanity that fits, but choosing one that holds up to daily steam, hard water, and a family's worth of stuff, without making the room feel like a closet.

If you are still early in planning, start with [Remodeling a Bathroom in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/bathroom-remodel-guide-sioux-falls) before you shop for anything. This article focuses on the specific decisions around the vanity, storage, and mirror lighting that most homeowners underestimate until they are standing in the showroom.

## Sizing a Bathroom Vanity for Older Sioux Falls Homes

Older homes do not follow today's standard dimensions. A bathroom that looked fine on a listing photo can turn out to be 5 feet wide with a door that swings into the vanity space.

Before you measure for a new vanity, measure the room.

- Door swing clearance: a bathroom door needs at least 24 inches of clear floor space in its swing path. Pocket doors solve this in tight rooms, but adding one means a permit and wall work.
- Walkway clearance: the National Kitchen and Bath Association recommends at least 21 inches of clear floor space in front of a vanity. More is better if two people share the bathroom.
- Toilet clearance: local plumbing codes require a minimum of 15 inches from the toilet centerline to any side obstruction. Ask your plumber to confirm the exact requirement for your project.

A common mistake is buying a 36-inch or 48-inch vanity to replace a 30-inch original, then discovering the door no longer clears or the toilet is boxed in. Write down every measurement before you go to the store, including the distance from the vanity wall to the toilet, to the door, and to any step-in shower or tub.

If your bathroom has a window behind or beside where the vanity will sit, factor in the apron height. Many standard vanities are 32 to 35 inches tall, which can block an older double-hung window entirely.

## Cabinet and Countertop Materials That Hold Up to Steam

A bathroom vanity lives in the steamiest room in the house. In Sioux Falls, that also means hard water. According to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report, total hardness runs about 15 grains per gallon for water treated at the City's plant, and about 10 grains per gallon for Lewis and Clark water. Which source your home gets can vary by neighborhood.

That hardness leaves scale on every surface the water touches, including cabinet finishes, countertops, and sink basins.

For cabinet boxes, look for:

- Plywood construction (not particleboard) in the box itself, which holds up better to humidity over time
- Fully enclosed backs, so steam and humidity do not get behind the cabinet into the wall
- Soft-close hinges, which are sealed better than older exposed hinges and do not corrode as fast

For countertops, the main trade-off in a small bathroom is between durability and cleanability.

- Quartz is non-porous and resists staining and scale buildup better than natural stone. It does not need sealing.
- Cultured marble is a common, affordable option in Sioux Falls remodels. It is molded in one piece with the sink, which eliminates the seam where most countertop mold starts.
- Natural granite is porous and needs periodic sealing, which matters more here than in a low-humidity room.
- Laminate has improved and costs less, but the seams and edges can swell if water gets in repeatedly.

Whatever material you choose, the edge profile matters. A flat or eased edge traps less scale than an ornate ogee profile that has crevices you cannot reach with a cloth.

For more on how hard water affects every surface in a remodeled bathroom, see [Hard Water and Your New Bathroom in Sioux Falls](/guides/hard-water-bathroom-sioux-falls). It covers what to expect on glass, faucets, grout, and what to ask a plumber about a softener.

## Getting Real Storage Into a Small Bathroom

A single vanity cabinet is almost never enough storage, especially in a bathroom shared by two or more people. The good news is that small bathrooms usually have more usable wall and ceiling space than homeowners realize.

A local homeowner guide at [siouxfallsrevamp.com/sioux-falls-bathroom-vanity-guide/](https://siouxfallsrevamp.com/sioux-falls-bathroom-vanity-guide/) goes deeper on measuring and matching vanity styles to small bathroom layouts, and it is worth reading before you finalize anything.

**Medicine cabinets** are the most underused storage option in small bathrooms. A recessed medicine cabinet sits inside the wall, so it adds storage without sticking out into the room. In an older Sioux Falls home, the stud spacing is usually 16 inches on center, which fits a standard 14-inch-wide recessed cabinet without cutting studs. A wider cabinet means a header and more carpentry work.

Before you plan a recessed cabinet, ask your contractor what is in that wall. Plumbing and electrical are common on the vanity wall, and moving them adds cost and permits.

**Niches** are a similar idea applied to the shower or tub surround. A niche cut between studs gives you a shelf for soap and shampoo without a caddy hanging from the showerhead. Niches in a tile shower need to be waterproofed as carefully as the rest of the walls. This is not a shortcut area.

**Freestanding and wall-mounted shelving** above the toilet is another option that uses otherwise dead space. A wall-mounted unit needs to hit studs or use proper hollow-wall anchors rated for the weight.

If you are going from a single-sink vanity to a double, make sure the room can actually support it. Two sinks in a 60-inch vanity look appealing but often leave almost no counter space and require moving the drain and supply lines, which is a plumbing permit.

## Vanity Lighting at the Mirror

Lighting at the mirror is a vanity decision as much as an electrical one, and it is one most homeowners leave until too late. The fixture placement affects which vanity you can use, where the medicine cabinet goes, and how the whole wall reads when you walk in.

The most common mistake in small bathrooms is a single overhead fixture above the mirror. That placement casts shadows under the chin and around the eyes, which is exactly the wrong light for a bathroom mirror.

Side-mounted fixtures, one on each side of the mirror at roughly face height (around 60 to 65 inches from the floor to the center of the fixture), give even, shadow-free light. If the bathroom is narrow and side fixtures are not possible, a wide fixture directly above the mirror works better than a small centered one.

Vanity lighting is an electrical permit in Sioux Falls. According to the City of Sioux Falls, a homeowner may do their own wiring only if they own and live in the single-family home, and homeowner electrical permits are not issued to friends or family members. If a licensed electrician does the work, the City's Currently Licensed Contractors list is a free way to verify their license before anyone starts.

Plan the lighting before you buy the mirror or medicine cabinet. A recessed medicine cabinet and a side-lit vanity can share the same wall, but the stud bays and electrical rough-in need to be coordinated early.

## Putting It Together Before You Buy Anything

The decisions about a bathroom vanity and storage connect to almost every other part of a remodel. The vanity rough-in affects plumbing. The medicine cabinet affects electrical. The countertop affects how much daily cleaning you are signing up for.

Before you finalize anything, write down:

- The exact dimensions of your bathroom, including door swing and toilet clearance
- Whether your walls are exterior walls (which may have insulation that limits recessed options)
- Which water source your home uses and whether you already deal with hard water scale
- Whether any family members will need wider clearances or grab bar blocking now or in the future (the [Aging in Place Bathroom Updates That Make Sense](/guides/aging-in-place-bathroom) guide covers this in detail)
- Who will handle the electrical and plumbing rough-in, and whether they are on the City's licensed contractor list

A contractor who has done a lot of small bathroom work in older Sioux Falls homes will catch most of these conflicts before demo day. Ask during the estimate, not after the vanity has been delivered.
