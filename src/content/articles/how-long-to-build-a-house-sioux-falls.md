Building a custom home in Sioux Falls takes longer than most buyers expect when they first sit down with a builder. From the day you sign a contract to the day you get keys, a realistic custom home timeline runs somewhere between 10 and 18 months, depending on the size of the home, your builder's current workload, and how quickly decisions get made. The ranges below are typical starting points, not guarantees, so ask your builder to put a phase-by-phase schedule in writing before you commit. For the full picture of what the process involves, start with the [How to Build a Custom Home in Sioux Falls (2026 Guide)](/guides/build-custom-home-sioux-falls).

---

## Design, Plans, and Bids (2 to 4 Months)

This phase starts before a single permit is filed, and it is almost always longer than buyers anticipate.

You are selecting an architect or designer, working through floor plans, making structural decisions (basement or slab, for example), and getting bids from your builder. Each of those steps takes real calendar time, not just hours.

Architectural drawings thorough enough to submit for a permit can take six to twelve weeks on their own, depending on the designer's workload and how many revisions you request.

Once you have drawings, your builder prices the job. If you are getting competitive bids from multiple contractors, add another two to four weeks for that process.

A few decisions at this stage have outsize effects on the rest of the schedule:

- Foundation type (full basement, walkout, or slab on grade) affects excavation timing and cost
- Lot conditions, including drainage and soil, can require engineering before plans are finalized
- Whether your lot is inside Sioux Falls city limits or in a suburb like Brandon, Harrisburg, or Tea determines which permitting office you deal with (more on that below)

Skipping or rushing design to get to construction faster almost always costs more time later, in the form of change orders and re-work.

## Permits and Pre-Construction (3 to 8 Weeks)

Once your builder submits plans to the City of Sioux Falls Building Services, located on the first floor of City Center at 231 N. Dakota Avenue, the permit review takes time.

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, applicants provide an approximate cost of the work, and site, foundation, and floor plans may be required. Building permit fees start at $40 and rise with the construction value on the city's 2026 fee schedule, and electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits each have their own separate fee schedule.

Once issued, work must begin and an inspection be requested within 180 days, so builders typically move promptly after receiving the permit.

If your home is outside city limits, the City of Sioux Falls Building Services does not issue your permit. Homes in the county go through Minnehaha County Planning or Lincoln County, and homes in incorporated suburbs like Brandon, Harrisburg, Tea, or Crooks go through those cities. Each jurisdiction has its own review timeline, so confirm the expected turnaround before you plan your start date.

Before any digging begins, the City of Sioux Falls notes that you must call 811, South Dakota One Call, so underground utilities can be marked.

## Excavation and Foundation in Sioux Falls (3 to 6 Weeks)

Foundation work is where Sioux Falls-specific conditions matter most.

The city's building code requires footings at a 42-inch frost depth. That means excavation goes down deep, and the concrete must be placed and cured properly before framing can start.

Sioux Falls sits on expansive clay soils, and freeze-thaw cycles through a typical winter can shift and heave ground that was not properly prepared. Builders and engineers factor this into footing design, drainage planning, and grading, and you should ask your builder explicitly how they handle it.

Two local conditions can stretch this phase beyond six weeks:

- **Wet springs:** The Big Sioux River valley and low-lying lots in many neighborhoods can stay saturated well into May, making excavation impractical or damaging to the site.
- **Frozen ground:** Excavating frozen clay is expensive and can damage soil structure. Builders who start in late fall often plan to have excavation done before the ground freezes, or they wait until spring.

The [Can You Build a House in a Sioux Falls Winter?](/guides/building-a-house-in-winter-sioux-falls) article goes into detail on what can and cannot happen once temperatures drop, which is worth reading before you lock in a start date.

## Framing and Rough-Ins (2 to 4 Months)

Once the foundation is in and inspected, framing moves quickly under good conditions. A typical single-family home in Sioux Falls can be framed in two to six weeks depending on size and complexity.

After framing, rough-in trades come in: plumbing, electrical, HVAC, and sometimes low-voltage work. These trades work in a sequence, and scheduling them is one of the more logistically demanding parts of the build.

Delays here typically come from:

- Trade scheduling conflicts, which were more common when Sioux Falls had 821 single-family homes and townhomes under construction (SiouxFalls.Business reported in April 2026 that number had dropped to 604 as of April 1, 2026, so trade availability has eased somewhat)
- Inspections that require corrections before the next phase can proceed
- Special-order materials with long lead times, particularly windows, engineered lumber, and steel beams

Rough-in inspections by the city must pass before insulation and drywall can go in. Your builder manages that sequencing, but it is worth asking how they handle scheduling if an inspection fails and requires a correction.

## Insulation, Drywall, and Interior Finishes (2 to 4 Months)

This phase feels like it should be fast, and it is in a production home. In a custom home, it is often where the schedule slips the most.

The reason is selections. Cabinets, countertops, flooring, tile, fixtures, and trim all have to be ordered before they are needed. Many of those items have lead times of four to twelve weeks from specialty suppliers. If a buyer has not made selections before the job reaches that point, the builder is waiting.

Most experienced builders give clients a selection schedule at the start of the project, with deadlines for each category. Missing those deadlines is the single most common reason a custom home finishes late. Ask your builder for that schedule in writing before construction starts.

This phase also includes exterior work: siding, windows, roofing, and exterior trim. In Sioux Falls, roofing and exterior work are weather-dependent, and a wet or cold fall can push exterior completion into the next season.

---

## Final Inspections and Move-In (2 to 6 Weeks)

After interior finishes are complete, the builder schedules a final inspection with the city. The city inspects electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and the overall structure, and issues a certificate of occupancy when everything passes.

Punch list work, the small corrections identified during the builder's own walkthrough and your pre-closing inspection, typically runs two to four weeks depending on the list length and trade availability.

If you have a construction loan, your lender will likely require a draw inspection at this stage as well. Construction loans release funds in draws as work is completed, and a final draw typically requires the certificate of occupancy. For a full explanation of how that process works, see [How Construction Loans Work When You Build in Sioux Falls](/guides/construction-loans-sioux-falls).

One final note: lender draw delays are a real source of schedule friction that buyers often do not anticipate. If a draw takes two weeks to process and a subcontractor is waiting to be paid before returning, that two weeks shows up in your move-in date. Talk to your lender before construction starts about their draw timeline and what documentation they require at each stage.

## What to Ask Your Builder Before You Sign

No two builders run the same schedule, and published timelines are starting points. Before you sign a contract, ask for:

- A written phase-by-phase schedule with milestone dates
- Their current backlog and how many homes they are building simultaneously
- How they handle trade scheduling and what happens when a trade is delayed
- Their selection deadline schedule, in writing
- Their process when a city inspection requires a correction

You can verify that your builder holds a current Sioux Falls license by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs. The city's permit data site, which updates every Sunday, also lets you look up permits by address.
