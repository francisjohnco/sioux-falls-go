Getting a fence permit in Sioux Falls is simpler than most homeowners expect, but skipping it can create real problems at sale time or with your neighbors. According to the City of Sioux Falls, a permit is required any time you build, move, replace, or repair a fence, not just for new construction. If you are just beginning to plan your project, [Building a Fence in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/fence-guide-sioux-falls) covers the full process from material choice to final inspection.

## Why a Permit Is Required for Every Fence in Sioux Falls

A lot of homeowners assume a permit only matters for big projects, like additions or decks. The City of Sioux Falls does not make that distinction for fences.

The permit requirement exists so the City can confirm your fence meets height rules, setbacks, and sight-line requirements before concrete is poured. That matters especially on corner lots, where fence height in the front yard affects driver visibility.

Skipping the permit also creates a paper trail problem. When you sell your home, a permit record shows the fence was inspected and approved. Without one, a buyer's inspector or lender may flag the fence as unpermitted work.

## The Permit Fee, Inspection and Expiration

According to the City of Sioux Falls fence permit pamphlet, the permit fee is $20. That is one of the most affordable permit fees you will encounter for any home improvement project.

After construction is complete, a final inspection is required. You schedule that by calling the Zoning Office directly at 605-367-8254. Do not skip this step, it is part of what the permit covers and what closes the record officially.

Permits expire six months after the date of issue, according to the City. If your project runs long due to contractor scheduling or weather, extensions are available. Contact the Zoning Office before the permit lapses rather than after.

Here is what the permit process looks like in order:

- Apply for the fence permit (see where to apply below)
- Confirm your property lines before digging (more on this below)
- Build the fence
- Call 605-367-8254 to schedule the final inspection
- Keep a copy of the approved permit for your records

## Where to Apply for a Fence Permit in Sioux Falls

You apply through City of Sioux Falls Building Services. The Zoning Office handles questions about fence-specific rules, height limits, and setbacks.

Before you apply, have a basic site plan ready. This does not need to be an architectural drawing, but it should show your lot, where the fence will run, and approximate distances from the property line.

Always confirm current requirements directly with the Zoning Office at 605-367-8254 before you submit. Requirements and processes do get updated, and what a neighbor did two years ago may not match current procedure.

## Height Rules and Property Lines You Need to Know First

According to the City of Sioux Falls residential fence guide, fences on corner and interior lots can be up to four feet tall in the front yard. The City recommends using a licensed surveyor to locate your property lines before you build.

This matters more than many homeowners realize. Fence disputes with neighbors often start because one party assumed where the property line was rather than confirming it. A survey before you dig is far cheaper than moving a fence after it is in. See [Property Lines, Surveys and Your Neighbor](/guides/property-line-fence) for a full walkthrough of how to find pins, hire a surveyor, and document any agreement with your neighbor in writing.

For rear and side yard height limits beyond the front yard rules listed above, confirm with the Zoning Office, since those details can vary by lot type and are not fully covered in the pamphlet.

## HOA and Covenant Rules That Go Beyond the City Permit

A City permit does not mean your HOA has approved your fence. In many Sioux Falls neighborhoods, including areas with active homeowners associations, covenants restrict fence height, style, material, or color beyond what the City requires.

HOA approval and a City permit are two separate processes. You need both if your neighborhood has covenants. Getting the City permit first does not protect you from an HOA violation.

Check your covenants before you apply for anything. If you do not have a copy, your HOA management company or the Minnehaha County Register of Deeds can help you locate them. [Fences, Covenants and HOA Approval](/guides/fence-and-hoa) walks through how to find your covenants, what the approval steps usually look like, and what happens if you skip that step.

Some homeowners have been surprised to learn their neighborhood restricts wood privacy fences or requires a specific picket style. Finding that out before you purchase materials saves real money.

## What to Ask Your Fence Contractor About the Permit

If you are hiring a contractor, ask directly who pulls the permit. Some companies handle it as part of the job, and some leave it to the homeowner. Neither approach is wrong, but you need to know before work begins.

Questions worth asking:

- Will you pull the fence permit, or is that my responsibility?
- Do you schedule the final inspection, or do I call the Zoning Office myself?
- What happens if the permit expires before the project is done?
- Is the permit fee included in your quote, or billed separately?

A good contractor will have clear answers to all of these. If the permit process seems like news to them, that is worth noting. [Questions to Ask a Fence Company](/guides/choosing-fence-company) has a fuller list of what to cover before you sign anything.

The $20 permit fee is a small part of any fence project, but the inspection and the record it creates protect you for as long as you own the home. Confirm everything with the Zoning Office at 605-367-8254, since they are the authoritative source for current requirements.
