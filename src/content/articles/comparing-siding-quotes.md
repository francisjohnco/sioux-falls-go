Getting more than one siding estimate is smart. Making sense of them is harder than it sounds, because two bids for the same house can differ by thousands of dollars and still be quoting completely different scopes of work.

Before you start collecting quotes, read [New Siding in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/siding-guide-sioux-falls) so you understand your material options and what a full replacement actually involves. This article picks up where that one leaves off, focused entirely on how to read, compare, and question the numbers in front of you.

## What a Complete Siding Quote Should Include

A complete siding estimate in Sioux Falls should spell out every part of the job, not just the panels themselves.

If a quote is a single line item with a total price, ask the contractor to break it down. You need to see each of these categories listed separately:

- Siding material (type, style, thickness, color)
- Square footage being replaced
- Trim, soffit, and fascia
- Removal and disposal of existing siding
- House wrap and flashing
- Permits
- Warranty on both materials and labor

A quote that skips any of these categories is not an apples-to-apples comparison with one that includes them. The cheapest number on paper can easily become the most expensive job once the missing pieces get added back in.

## Square Footage: The Number That Anchors Everything

Square footage is the foundation of any siding estimate. If two quotes show different square footages for the same house, ask both contractors how they measured.

Legitimate contractors measure the actual wall surface, subtract windows and doors, and sometimes add a waste factor for cuts and trim. A low square footage number is not always a sign of a better deal. It may mean the contractor is not accounting for all the walls, or is planning to skip areas you expect to be done.

Ask each contractor to walk you through their measurement. If they used a satellite measuring tool, ask if they verified it on site. For a larger or more complex roofline, in-person measurement matters more.

Sioux Falls homeowners replacing storm-damaged siding often have to coordinate new siding on some walls while leaving others untouched, which makes matching and accurate measurement even more critical. If you are in that situation, the article on [Matching New Siding to Old After a Repair](/guides/matching-siding-colors) covers how contractors handle fading and discontinued colors.

## Trim, Soffit, Fascia, Removal, and Disposal

These four line items are where quotes diverge the most, and where homeowners are most often surprised.

**Trim and soffit:** Some contractors include the trim around windows, doors, corners, and the roofline. Others quote the flat wall panels only and treat everything else as an add-on. Ask directly: does this quote include all trim, soffit, and fascia?

**Removal and disposal:** Tearing off and hauling away old siding takes time and costs money for dumpsters or disposal fees. Some contractors include it in their base price. Others add it as a separate line or leave it out entirely. If you do not see it listed, ask.

**What to look for:**

- Is soffit and fascia replacement included or excluded?
- What happens if rot is found under the old siding? Is there a per-hour or per-board charge for repairs?
- Who is responsible for cleanup and dumpster removal?

Rotted sheathing or framing discovered during tear-off is common and almost always priced separately. Ask each contractor how they handle surprises and what their rate is so you can compare fairly.

## House Wrap, Flashing, and What Goes Between the Wall and the Siding

The layers between your wall and the siding panel matter more than most homeowners realize, especially in a climate with the freeze-thaw cycles Sioux Falls sees every spring.

A complete siding estimate should include:

- House wrap (or whether existing wrap will be reused)
- Flashing at all windows, doors, and penetrations
- Any foam insulation board if that is part of the scope

If a quote does not mention these items, it is worth asking whether the contractor plans to skip them or assume they are already adequate. Skipping or reusing deteriorated wrap and flashing is one of the most common ways a low bid costs more in the long run. ## Permits and Licenses: What Sioux Falls Requires

Re-siding a home inside Sioux Falls city limits requires a building permit. That is not optional.

According to the City of Sioux Falls, re-siding falls under the same permit requirement as re-roofing and exterior door replacement. The City also requires that any contractor working on a residential one or two family home or townhome hold a Residential Building Contractor's License, and the City does not accept licenses from other states. A Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license, created in 2024, specifically covers re-siding with no structural changes.

You can verify any contractor before you sign by checking the City's Currently Licensed Contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.

Ask each contractor who is pulling the permit. The permit should be pulled by the contractor, not left to you. If a bid does not mention permits at all, ask directly. A contractor who suggests skipping the permit is a contractor who is asking you to absorb all the risk.

## Warranty: What You Are Actually Getting

Every siding quote should include two separate warranties: one from the manufacturer and one from the contractor for their labor.

Manufacturer warranties vary by material and product line. Ask what warranty the specific product carries and whether it is a full replacement warranty or a prorated one. Ask what voids it, because improper installation by an unlicensed contractor can void a manufacturer warranty entirely.

Labor warranties vary by contractor. Ask:

- How long does the labor warranty last?
- What does it cover? (Water infiltration? Panel movement? Caulk failure?)
- Is it in writing in the contract?

A contractor who is confident in their work will put the labor warranty in writing without hesitation. If it is only offered verbally, ask for it in the contract before you sign.

## Why Two Bids for the Same House Can Look So Different

Sioux Falls homeowners often find that bids for the same job vary significantly, and not just because of pricing.

The most common reasons bids differ:

- **Scope differences:** One contractor included soffit replacement, the other did not.
- **Material differences:** One quoted a thicker panel or a higher-grade product.
- **Labor model:** Some contractors use their own crews, others subcontract. Ask which applies.
- **Permit assumption:** One contractor built the permit cost in, the other left it out.
- **Disposal:** One included dumpster and haul-away, the other assumed you would handle it.

After a hail storm, you may also encounter door-to-door contractors from out of state who offer quick, low quotes. The City's licensing requirement exists for exactly this reason. Before you accept any siding estimate in Sioux Falls from a contractor you do not know, check the licensed contractors list first. If a name is not on it, that is a serious flag.

## How to Actually Compare Quotes Side by Side

Once you have three written estimates, build a simple comparison.

For each quote, record:

- Total price
- Square footage included
- Materials specified (type, brand if given, thickness)
- Trim and soffit: included or excluded?
- Removal and disposal: included or excluded?
- House wrap and flashing: included or excluded?
- Permit: who pulls it, is cost included?
- Labor warranty: length and what it covers
- License number (verify at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors)

When you lay them out this way, the cheapest quote rarely stays the cheapest once you add back what it left out. The goal is to find the quote that covers everything and comes from a licensed contractor you can verify, not simply the lowest total.
