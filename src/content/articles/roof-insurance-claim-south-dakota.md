Filing a roof insurance claim in South Dakota feels straightforward until you are standing in your driveway after a hailstorm wondering what actually happens next. The process has real steps, real deadlines, and real places where things go wrong. Understanding it before you need it means fewer surprises when it matters most.

For the full picture on repairs and replacements, start with [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls). This article focuses entirely on the insurance process, from your first call to cashing the final check.

## Step One: Document, Notify, and Protect

The South Dakota Division of Insurance tells homeowners to photograph damage, file promptly, and make temporary repairs to prevent further damage while keeping every receipt. Do not make permanent repairs before contacting your insurer.

That order matters. If you replace shingles before the adjuster sees them, you may lose the ability to prove the original scope of the damage.

After photographing from the ground, call your insurer and open the claim. Ask them directly:

- What is my deadline to file?
- What is my deductible?
- When will an adjuster contact me?
- Will you provide a written list of what you need from me?

Write down the name of every person you speak to, the date, and what was said. The Division of Insurance recommends keeping exactly this kind of log throughout a claim. Their number is 605-773-3563 if you need guidance at any point.

## The Adjuster Inspection and Why Your Roofer Should Be There

The insurance company will send an adjuster to inspect the damage. This is one of the most important moments in the entire process, and many homeowners go through it alone without realizing they do not have to.

Having a licensed roofer present during the adjuster's inspection is not unusual or adversarial. A roofer who understands hail damage can point out bruising on shingles, granule loss in gutters, and damage to vents or flashing that an adjuster might miss or note differently. What gets written on that report directly affects what the insurer agrees to pay.

After hail events like the storms on July 25, 2026, when the National Weather Service Sioux Falls recorded gusts of 72 mph at the airport and 77 mph near Harrisburg, adjusters can be handling hundreds of claims at once. That is not an excuse for a rushed inspection, but it is a reason to have a second set of eyes on your roof.

## Reading the Estimate and Understanding Depreciation

After the inspection, your insurer will send a written estimate, often formatted through software called Xactimate. It will show line items for materials, labor, overhead, and profit. It may also show two separate numbers: the Actual Cash Value (ACV) and the Replacement Cost Value (RCV).

The first check you receive is usually the ACV. That is the replacement cost minus depreciation for the age and condition of your existing roof. The gap between that check and the full replacement cost can be significant, and it surprises a lot of homeowners.

For example, if your insurer values a full replacement at $14,000 but holds back $4,500 in depreciation, your first check is roughly $9,500 minus your deductible. You receive the withheld depreciation (called the "recoverable depreciation") after the work is complete and you submit the final invoice. Whether your policy allows recoverable depreciation at all depends on whether you have an RCV policy or an ACV-only policy. Check yours now, before you need to know.

## Supplements, Short Payments, and What to Push Back On

A supplement is an additional claim filed when the original estimate does not cover the full scope of work. They happen regularly, especially when:

- Decking damage is found after tear-off
- Code-required upgrades are needed (such as updated ventilation or drip edge)
- Material prices have risen since the estimate was written
- Items were missed or undervalued in the initial inspection

Your roofer files the supplement on your behalf, with documentation. The insurer reviews it. This can take weeks, and it is one reason why some roofers ask to wait for the final check before they consider the job financially closed.

If you believe your insurer has underpaid and supplements are not resolving it, the South Dakota Division of Insurance says you have the right to request an itemized explanation of any settlement. That written explanation gives you something specific to respond to or dispute.

Your policy may also include an appraisal clause, which lets both sides hire independent appraisers and use an agreed umpire to resolve the difference. This is not litigation. It is a standard policy process and worth asking about if the gap is large.

## When Insurance Refuses an Older Roof

This is one of the most common and painful situations Sioux Falls homeowners describe. You have lived in your house for 15 years, storms have come and gone, and now your insurer is saying the damage is due to "normal wear and age" rather than a specific storm event.

Here is what to understand. Insurers are allowed to factor age into their assessment, and many ACV policies apply heavy depreciation to older shingles. Some policies also include exclusions for roofs over a certain age. None of that means your claim is automatically valid or invalid. It means you need:

- A roofer who can document hail hits as a distinct, dateable event rather than general weathering
- Photos and weather data tied to specific storms (the National Weather Service keeps records)
- An itemized denial in writing, not just a verbal conversation

If the denial feels wrong, start with that written itemized explanation from the insurer. The Division of Insurance at 605-773-3563 can tell you whether the insurer followed proper claims handling procedures. Filing a complaint with that office is a formal step, and insurers know it carries weight.

## Protecting Yourself Through the Whole Process

A few things to watch for from first call to final payment:

- Never sign an Assignment of Benefits or any document that transfers your insurance rights to a contractor. South Dakota has restricted these in some contexts, but the pressure to sign them still exists after storms.
- Door-to-door contractors appearing after hailstorms may pressure you for a quick signature. Under South Dakota law, most door-to-door sales contracts carry a three-day right to cancel, with any deposit refunded within 10 days. The South Dakota Attorney General's consumer protection office specifically warns homeowners about post-hailstorm pressure tactics. Their number is 1-800-300-1986.
- Any contractor doing shingle work in Sioux Falls needs a city Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license, which requires a $20,000 compliance bond and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance, according to the City of Sioux Falls. You can verify a contractor's license by calling Building Services at 605-367-8670.

The [How to Choose a Roofer in Sioux Falls, Questions to Ask](/guides/how-to-choose-a-roofer-sioux-falls) guide has the full vetting checklist so you are not trying to research a contractor while also managing a claim.

The final step in any claim is submitting your completed invoice to release withheld depreciation. Keep every document, every photo, and every written communication until that check clears and you are satisfied with the work.
