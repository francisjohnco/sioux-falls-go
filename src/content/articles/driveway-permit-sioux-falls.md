Replacing or widening a driveway feels like a straightforward project until someone mentions permits and you're not sure whether that applies to you. The short answer is: it often does, and skipping the step can create real problems at resale or if the city flags the work. Before you sign anything, it helps to understand exactly what the City of Sioux Falls requires and what to ask the contractor who will be doing the work. For a broader look at the full scope of a driveway project, start with [Concrete Driveways and Patios in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/concrete-guide-sioux-falls).

## Who Handles Driveway Permits in Sioux Falls

According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, the Zoning Department handles driveway permits, not the Building Services department that most homeowners associate with construction permits.

This is a detail that trips people up. If you've pulled a building permit before for a deck or addition, you're used to dealing with one office. Driveway work routes differently.

The distinction matters because it means you need to contact the right department from the start. Calling the wrong one can cost you a week of back-and-forth before anyone points you in the right direction.

## What Typically Triggers a Permit

Not every patch of concrete in your driveway requires a permit, but several common projects do. Here are the situations where a Sioux Falls driveway permit is most likely required:

- Adding a new driveway where none existed before
- Widening an existing driveway
- Adding or modifying the approach (the section where your driveway meets the street)
- Replacing a driveway in a way that changes its footprint

The approach is worth calling out specifically. The approach connects private property to a public street, and any work that touches it involves the city's right-of-way. An approach permit is a common requirement even when the rest of the driveway work might not trigger one.

If you're only replacing concrete within your existing driveway footprint without changing dimensions, the permit situation may be different. The safest move is to contact the Zoning Department directly and describe exactly what you're planning before any work starts.

## Who Actually Pulls the Permit

This is one of the most practical questions to ask a contractor before you hire them. In many cases, the contractor pulls the permit on your behalf as part of the project. But not all contractors handle this automatically, and some may expect you to initiate it yourself.

Ask directly: "Will you pull the driveway and approach permit, or is that something I need to handle?" Get the answer in writing, in your bid or contract.

If a contractor tells you a permit isn't needed for a project that involves an approach or a change in footprint, that's worth verifying with the city yourself before work starts. It is not unusual for well-meaning contractors to be wrong about permit requirements in a jurisdiction they don't work in frequently.

Homeowners can look up permits through the city's permit site, which updates every Sunday, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs. That means after a permit is pulled, you can confirm it was actually filed.

## Before Any Digging Starts

Regardless of whether a permit is required for your specific project, the City of Sioux Falls is clear: call 811 (South Dakota One Call) before any digging so underground utilities are marked. This is not optional and it protects you from hitting a gas line, fiber cable, or water line buried in your yard.

Your contractor should initiate this call, but you can and should ask them to confirm it was done before excavation begins. The marking process typically takes a few business days, which is one reason projects sometimes start later than a homeowner expects after the bid is accepted.

Sioux Falls homeowners have noted in reviews of local contractors that waiting about four weeks from an accepted bid to the start of work is not unusual, and utility marking is one of the steps that is built into that lead time.

## What to Ask Your Contractor Before Signing

Going into a driveway project with the right questions protects you from surprises. Here is a short list to work through before you sign a bid:

- Does this project require a driveway or approach permit from the Zoning Department?
- Will you pull the permit, or do I need to contact the city directly?
- Have you called 811 to have utilities marked before digging?
- Will the permit be reflected in the contract or written bid?
