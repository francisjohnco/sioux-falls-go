Hiring someone to fix your basement is one of the bigger decisions a Sioux Falls homeowner makes. The work is often hidden once it's done, the sales pressure can feel intense, and the wrong fix can cost more than the original problem. Before you sign anything, the questions you ask in that first conversation tell you more than any sales pitch will.

For a full picture of how water gets into Sioux Falls basements and what actually stops it, start with [A Dry Basement in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/basement-waterproofing-guide-sioux-falls). This article is narrower: it's the checklist you bring to every contractor conversation.

## Does This Waterproofing Contractor Actually Diagnose the Cause?

This is the most important question on the list, and it's the one homeowners most often skip.

A good waterproofing contractor or foundation repair company will tell you where the water is coming from before recommending anything to sell you. Local homeowners consistently praise crews who explained the cause first. The frustration almost always goes the other direction, toward companies that showed up and immediately started quoting a full interior drainage system without looking closely at the wall, the grade outside, or where the water actually enters.

Ask the contractor directly: "What is causing the water, and how do you know?"

The answer should be specific. It might be a cove joint at the floor, a cracked block wall, hydrostatic pressure from clay soil, or surface water pooling against the foundation. If the answer is vague or skips straight to a product, that's a signal to slow down.

Also ask whether they think an engineer should look at anything first. If you have bowing walls, horizontal cracks, or stair-step cracks in block, a structural engineer's opinion belongs before any contractor's bid. See [Foundation Cracks, Which Ones Matter](/guides/foundation-cracks) for a plain-language breakdown of which crack types warrant that call.

## Is This Contractor Licensed to Work in Sioux Falls?

Licensing matters and it's easy to check.

According to the City of Sioux Falls, a Residential Building Contractor's License is required of all contractors working on residential one and two family homes and townhomes within city limits. The City does not accept licenses from other states, so an out-of-state company needs to hold a Sioux Falls license specifically.

The City publishes its Currently Licensed Contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors. You can look up any company before they ever come to your door.

Ask every contractor:

- "Do you hold a current Residential Building Contractor's License with the City of Sioux Falls?"
- "Will you pull any permits required for this work?"
- "Who will be on-site doing the work, your employees or subcontractors?"

That last question matters because licensing applies to whoever is physically doing the work. If the company subs out the labor, ask whether those crews are also licensed.

## What Does the Warranty Actually Cover?

Warranty language is where the gap between the sales conversation and the contract often shows up.

Ask for the warranty in writing before you agree to anything, and read it carefully. The questions to ask:

- Does the warranty cover the repair itself, the waterproofing system, or both?
- Is the warranty transferable if you sell the house? (This can matter at closing.)
- Who honors the warranty if this company goes out of business?
- What voids the warranty? (Some exclude damage from new cracks, landscaping changes, or failure to maintain a sump pump.)

A warranty that sounds strong in a sales meeting can have exceptions that make it nearly useless. National waterproofing franchises often have transferable warranties, which is worth asking about if you think you may sell within a few years. Independent local companies may offer stronger relationships but fewer guarantees about long-term coverage. Neither is automatically better. Read the document.

Also ask whether the fix they're proposing addresses the cause or manages the symptom. An interior drainage channel manages water that gets in. It does not stop water from entering. That is sometimes the right answer, but you should understand which one you're buying.

If you end up comparing multiple bids, [How to Compare Waterproofing Quotes](/guides/waterproofing-quotes) walks through what the scope of work should include and what oversell warning signs look like.

## Can You See Their References and Recent Local Work?

A contractor who has worked in Sioux Falls for years should be able to give you references without hesitation.

Ask for:

- Two or three references from jobs in Sioux Falls specifically, not just the region
- At least one reference from a job done more than two years ago (so you can ask how the fix has held up)
- Photos of completed work if the homeowner consented

When you call a reference, skip the generic "were you happy with them" question. Ask:

- "Did they tell you what was causing the problem before they recommended a fix?"
- "Did anything come back after the work was done?"
- "Did the crew stay on schedule and clean up after themselves?"
- "Would you call them again?"

Online reviews are useful but easy to game. A contractor who can give you a name and phone number for a real Minnehaha County homeowner is showing you something that matters.

## What Should You Know About Radon Before the Work Starts?

This one catches homeowners off guard, but it belongs in the contractor conversation.

According to the South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources, eastern South Dakota, including Sioux Falls and Minnehaha County, is EPA Radon Zone 1, the highest-risk designation. Dakota News Now reported in April 2023, citing state data from 2008 to 2017, that Minnehaha County had an estimated mean radon level of 6.5 pCi/L. The EPA recommends taking action at or above 4.0 pCi/L.

Why does this matter when hiring a waterproofing contractor?

Interior waterproofing work, especially interior drainage systems and sump pit installation, can alter the air pressure dynamics in a basement. That can change how radon moves through the space. If you haven't tested your basement for radon, do it before the work starts, and again after.

DANR has offered free radon test kits to South Dakota residents. Ask your waterproofing contractor whether their work affects radon, and ask whether they coordinate with radon mitigation contractors when needed.

For more on what radon levels mean for Sioux Falls homeowners and how to test, read [Radon Testing in a Sioux Falls Basement](/guides/radon-testing-sioux-falls).
