Losing your only bathroom for weeks is one of the main reasons Sioux Falls homeowners put off a remodel for years. The fear is real, but the timeline is also more predictable than most people expect once you understand what each stage involves and what tends to slow it down. This article walks through every phase of a bathroom remodel timeline, what affects the pace at each step, and how to plan your household around it. For the full picture of everything a remodel involves, start with [Remodeling a Bathroom in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/bathroom-remodel-guide-sioux-falls).

## Why "How Long Does a Bathroom Remodel Take" Doesn't Have One Answer

No honest contractor can hand you a fixed number of days without knowing the scope of your project, the condition of what's behind your walls, and how their schedule lines up with their subcontractors and suppliers.

A simple tub-to-shower swap with a prefabricated insert can genuinely be finished in a day. Some Sioux Falls homeowners have been surprised by exactly that. A full gut remodel with custom tile, moved plumbing, and new electrical is a different job entirely.

The honest answer is a range that depends on your specific project, and any contractor who gives you a firm day count before seeing the walls open is guessing.

What you can do is understand what each stage requires, ask your contractor what could extend it, and build your household plan around realistic possibilities rather than the best case.

## The Stages of a Bathroom Remodel and What Slows Each One

Think of a bathroom remodel as a sequence where each stage must be finished and sometimes inspected before the next one starts. Rushing one step creates problems in the next.

**Demo**

Demo feels fast because things come apart quickly. But what demo reveals can stop everything.

If your contractor finds soft subfloor, rotted framing, or evidence of an old leak, that becomes a change order conversation before anything else can proceed. Sioux Falls homes with older plumbing or tile installed directly over drywall (not cement board) are especially likely to have surprises under the surface.

This is not unusual. It is worth asking your contractor upfront how they handle hidden damage discoveries and what a change order process looks like before you sign anything.

**Rough Plumbing and Electrical**

If your project involves moving a drain, adding a circuit for a new fan or heated floor, or relocating a supply line, licensed subcontractors do this work before any walls close up.

According to the City of Sioux Falls, a plumbing contractor license is required for any plumbing work within city limits, and the City does not accept licenses from other states. The City also requires separate electrical permits for electrical work, and homeowners who want to do their own wiring may only do so in a home they own and live in.

If your general contractor brings in a plumber or electrician they haven't worked with before, that is worth asking about. Sioux Falls homeowners have noted in reviews that unfamiliar subcontractors can affect how smoothly a project runs.

**Inspections**

Rough inspections happen after plumbing and electrical are roughed in but before walls close. This is a required stop, not optional.

The City of Sioux Falls requires permits and inspections for projects that involve a home's building, electrical, mechanical, or plumbing systems. Homes outside city limits in Minnehaha County go through the county's Planning and Zoning office instead.

Inspection scheduling depends on the City's calendar, and a failed inspection means rework before re-inspection. This is one of the legitimate reasons a project can take longer than the original estimate, and it is not something a good contractor should try to skip.

**Waterproofing**

This step gets skipped or rushed more than any other, and it is the one that causes the most expensive problems later.

Shower walls and floors need proper waterproofing membrane before tile goes on. When this is done correctly it takes time to apply and cure. When it is not done correctly, water finds its way into the framing and subfloor, sometimes showing up as a leak in the basement ceiling below, sometimes not showing up for a year or more.

If you want to understand what inadequate waterproofing actually looks like after the fact, [What Remodelers Find Under Old Bathroom Tile](/guides/hidden-water-damage-bathroom) covers the warning signs and what proper waterproofing should involve.

**Tile**

Tile is where a lot of homeowners underestimate the time commitment. Setting tile, letting it cure, grouting, and sealing are separate steps with drying time between them that cannot be compressed.

Large format tiles take more prep work because the substrate has to be flat to a tighter tolerance. Intricate patterns take longer to set. If your tile was backordered or arrives damaged, the project stops until new material comes in.

Sioux Falls water is hard. According to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report, total hardness was about 15 grains per gallon for water treated at the City's plant and about 10 grains per gallon for Lewis and Clark water. Grout selection and sealing matter here because unsealed grout in a hard water home shows scale buildup faster than many homeowners expect.

**Fixtures and Finish Work**

Vanities, toilets, faucets, fans, mirrors, and trim all go in at the end, after tile is cured and surfaces are ready.

This stage can move quickly, but it depends on lead times. Fixtures that are special order or backordered can hold up a nearly finished project for days or weeks. If you want to avoid this, finalize your fixture selections early, before demo starts, and confirm delivery timelines with your contractor.

A note from real homeowner experience: at least one Sioux Falls homeowner found tape left inside a new showerhead after installation. Run every fixture during your final walkthrough and check the ceiling or basement below for drips before you sign off.

**Punch List**

The punch list is the final pass where small items get corrected: a caulk line that needs redoing, a door that doesn't swing cleanly, a fan cover that wasn't secured. A good contractor builds this into the schedule. Do not skip it and do not pay the final installment before it is complete.

## What Makes a Sioux Falls Remodel Take Longer Than Expected

Beyond the stage-by-stage delays, a few things in Sioux Falls specifically tend to extend timelines.

**Contractor availability.** The remodelers with the best reputations in this market are booked well in advance. Waiting months for a spot on a good contractor's schedule is common, and homeowners who try to rush into the first available option sometimes regret it. Build the wait into your planning.

**Permit processing.** City permit timelines vary. Ask your contractor how long permits typically take for a project like yours and whether that is built into their schedule estimate.

**Material lead times.** Supply chain variability affects custom tile, specialty fixtures, and some vanity lines. Confirm your selections and delivery dates before work starts.

**Hidden damage.** Sioux Falls homes with clay soil, freeze-thaw cycles, and older plumbing have more hidden moisture issues than homeowners expect. Budget extra time, not just extra money, for what might be behind the walls.

## How to Live Without Your Only Bathroom

This is the practical question that stops a lot of remodels from ever starting, and it deserves a real answer.

First, talk to your contractor about sequencing. Some work can be done to keep the toilet functional even when the shower is out of service. Ask explicitly whether the toilet can remain usable during demo and rough work.

Second, make a temporary plan before demo starts:

- Ask a neighbor, family member, or close friend if you can use their shower for the duration.
- If you are in a part of Sioux Falls with hotel options nearby, pricing a week or two is worth the peace of mind.
- Some homeowners use a gym membership for showers during a short remodel.
- If you have more than one bathroom, the question is simpler, but sequencing still matters.

Third, ask your contractor for a realistic best-case and worst-case schedule in writing, and ask what specific things would push you toward the worse end. Knowing the triggers helps you plan rather than just hoping for the best.

The homeowners who get through a remodel without major disruption are almost always the ones who planned for the inconvenient version of the timeline, not the ideal one.
