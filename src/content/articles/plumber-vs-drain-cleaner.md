When a drain backs up or water pools where it shouldn't, the first question is always the same: who do I even call? The answer matters more than most people expect, because a drain cleaning company and a licensed plumber are not interchangeable, and picking wrong costs you money in either direction.

For a broad look at how plumbing works in Sioux Falls homes, start with [Plumbing in a Sioux Falls Home, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/plumbing-guide-sioux-falls). This article is narrower: it focuses on the specific decision between a drain company and a plumber, and when each one is the right call.

## When a Drain Cleaning Company Is Exactly What You Need

Drain cleaning companies exist for one purpose: clearing blockages. They are often faster to schedule than a plumber, and for the right job, they are the smarter and usually less costly call.

The right jobs look like these:

- A single slow drain (kitchen, bathroom sink, or tub)
- A toilet that backs up once and has no other symptoms
- A floor drain in the basement that drains sluggishly
- Routine main line cleaning as preventive maintenance
- Tree root intrusion in the sewer line, when the pipe itself is still intact

Sioux Falls homeowners with mature trees near the sewer lateral, common in older neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints, deal with root intrusion more often than newer-build owners do. A drain company with a good auger or hydro-jet can clear that line without any pipe work at all.

The [Drain Cleaning in Sioux Falls](/guides/drain-cleaning-sioux-falls) guide covers what to expect from that service, including what a camera inspection adds and when to ask for one.

## When You Actually Need a Licensed Plumber

A drain company clears things. A licensed plumber repairs, replaces, and installs. Those are different skills, different tools, and in Sioux Falls, different licensing requirements.

Call a licensed plumber when:

- You have a leak anywhere, visible or suspected
- A camera inspection shows a cracked, broken, or collapsed pipe
- Your water heater is failing, leaking, or needs replacement
- You have a frozen or burst pipe (see [Frozen Pipe in Sioux Falls? What to Do Right Now](/guides/frozen-pipe-what-to-do) for immediate steps)
- Any gas line is involved
- The work requires a city permit

That last point matters. According to the City of Sioux Falls, a license is required of all contractors doing plumbing work within city limits, and the City does not accept out-of-state licenses. The City publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors, so you can verify before anyone starts work.

KELO News, quoting the City of Sioux Falls permitting support manager, specifically noted that water heater change-outs are among the projects that need a city permit and that homeowners sometimes skip. Skipping a permit is not a shortcut. It becomes a real problem when you sell.

## The Risk of Calling the Wrong One

This is where homeowners lose money, sometimes quietly and over a long period.

**Calling a drain company when you need a plumber:**

If a pipe is cracked or root-damaged and a drain company only clears the clog, the clog comes back. You pay for cleaning again, and the underlying problem gets worse in the meantime. In Sioux Falls, the soil is heavily clay-based and moves through freeze-thaw cycles every winter. That movement stresses pipes. A line that keeps clogging despite cleaning is often telling you something about the pipe, not just the debris.

**Calling a plumber when a drain company would do:**

You are not in danger, but you may pay significantly more for a service call than the job requires. Homeowners who are unsure sometimes call a plumber first out of habit, only to find out the fix was a straightforward auger job.

**Using an unlicensed contractor for work that needs a permit:**

Repairs done without required permits or by unlicensed contractors can fail inspection at the time of sale. The City of Sioux Falls requires licensed contractors for plumbing work, and there is no workaround for that at closing.

## The One Question to Ask a Drain Company Before They Start

If your drain company arrives and runs a camera, and that camera shows a broken pipe, what happens next?

That is the question to ask before anyone shows up.

Some drain cleaning companies also hold a City of Sioux Falls plumbing contractor license and can transition from clearing to repairing in the same visit. Others do not, and they will call in a referral or leave the job mid-way.

Ask the company directly: "If your camera finds a pipe that needs repair, are you licensed by the City of Sioux Falls to do that work?"

If the answer is no, that is not a reason to avoid them for a simple clog. It is just information you need so you are not surprised.

## Reading the Signs Before Anyone Arrives

You can often make a reasonable guess about which call to make before you pick up the phone. These are the patterns:

**Lean toward a drain company:**
- One slow drain, no others affected
- Recent gurgling that started after a lot of use
- It has been years since the main line was cleaned
- Toilet clogs that respond to a plunger

**Lean toward a licensed plumber:**
- Multiple drains backing up at the same time (often a main line problem or a pipe issue)
- Water stains on ceilings or walls with no obvious source
- A spike in your water bill with no change in usage
- Any visible corrosion, leaking joints, or water pooling under a fixture
- A water heater that is leaking, over 10 to 12 years old, or making unusual sounds

For more on spotting a leak you cannot see, [Hidden Plumbing Leaks and How They Get Found](/guides/hidden-plumbing-leaks) walks through the warning signs and the meter test step by step.

Sioux Falls water, depending on which treatment plant serves your home, can run around 10 to 15 grains per gallon of hardness according to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report. That mineral load builds up inside pipes and water heaters over time, which can look like a clog problem before it reveals itself as a hardware problem. Keep that in mind if a "slow drain" turns out to be a water heater or fixture issue.
