Sioux Falls sits in one of the most active hail corridors in the country. If you have filed a roof claim in the last five years, or watched a neighbor do it, you have probably wondered whether spending more on impact-resistant shingles the next time around actually makes sense. The short answer is: for many Sioux Falls homeowners, yes, but the math depends on your insurance policy, your neighborhood, and how long you plan to stay in the house. For a full picture of the replacement process itself, start with the [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls).

## What Impact-Resistant Ratings Actually Mean

The rating system comes from UL 2218, a standard from Underwriters Laboratories. A steel ball is dropped onto a shingle from increasing heights, and the shingle is graded Class 1 through Class 4 based on how it holds up.

Class 4 is the highest rating. It means the shingle survived a two-inch steel ball dropped from twenty feet without cracking or splitting. When a roofer or insurer mentions class 4 shingles in Sioux Falls, that is what they are referring to.

There is also an FM 4473 standard used by some commercial products, but for residential homes in Sioux Falls you will almost always see the UL 2218 Class 4 label on a spec sheet. Ask to see it in writing before agreeing to any specific product.

A few things the rating does not cover: it does not mean the shingle is dent-proof, it does not guarantee zero granule loss from hail, and it does not eliminate the possibility of a future claim. It means the shingle is significantly more resistant to cracking than a standard architectural shingle under test conditions.

## The Real Cost Difference

Impact-resistant shingles typically cost more per square (a roofing square is 100 square feet) than standard architectural shingles. National estimates for the premium vary widely by manufacturer and market, but a reasonable general expectation is somewhere in the range of 10 to 20 percent more for materials alone. Do not use those numbers to budget; get a written line-item quote from a licensed contractor.

The cost difference also depends on the specific product. Some Class 4 shingles are modified asphalt with a rubberized polymer layer. Others use a different granule coating or core construction. Prices vary even within the Class 4 category.

For a deeper look at everything that moves the total price of a roof replacement in Sioux Falls, including materials, tear-off, decking repairs, and permits, see [Roof Replacement Cost in Sioux Falls, What Drives the Price](/guides/roof-replacement-cost-sioux-falls).

One cost you may not think about: the building permit. According to the City of Sioux Falls, shingle work on a home requires a permit, and your contractor must hold a city Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license backed by a $20,000 compliance bond and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance. That applies whether you are installing standard shingles or Class 4. You can verify a contractor's license by calling Building Services at 605-367-8670.

## Asking Your Insurer About Premium Credits

Some insurance carriers offer a discount on homeowners premiums for Class 4 shingles, because the shingle is statistically less likely to result in a claim. Whether your carrier offers this, and how large it might be, depends entirely on your specific policy and company.

Do not assume a credit exists and do not let a roofer or a door-to-door salesperson promise you one. That promise is not theirs to make.

Here is what to actually do before you replace your roof:

- Call your insurance agent and ask directly: "Does our policy offer a premium reduction for Class 4 or UL 2218-rated shingles?"
- Ask what documentation they need (usually the spec sheet and proof of installation).
- Ask whether the credit applies at renewal or immediately.
- Get the answer in writing or confirmed in a follow-up email.

If a credit is available and your roof needs replacing anyway, the math can shift meaningfully. A discount that persists for ten or fifteen years can offset a meaningful share of the upfront material cost. But run the actual numbers with your agent using your real premium, not a hypothetical.

The South Dakota Division of Insurance is a good resource if you have questions about how your insurer must respond to coverage questions. They can be reached at 605-773-3563.

## Who Benefits Most From Class 4 Shingles in Sioux Falls

Sioux Falls gets meaningful hail most years. The storms that brought 72 mph gusts to the airport and 77 mph winds near Harrisburg on July 25, 2026, according to the National Weather Service Sioux Falls, also caused roof damage across the southern metro. That kind of event is not unusual here, it is a recurring condition.

The homeowners who tend to get the most value from Class 4 shingles are:

- People planning to stay in the house for ten or more years, long enough for the material premium to pay off through reduced claims and potential insurance credits.
- Homeowners who have already filed one or two hail claims and are worried about policy pushback on an older roof.
- People in newer neighborhoods with limited tree cover, where there is nothing to slow hail.
- Anyone replacing a roof after a major storm who wants to reduce the chance of doing this again in five years.

If you are selling in the next two or three years, the calculus is different. A Class 4 roof is a legitimate selling point, but whether the buyer's agent or inspector will capture that value in the offer is not guaranteed.

## The Practical Cons Worth Knowing

Class 4 shingles are not for every situation. Here are the honest drawbacks:

- Not every roofer in Sioux Falls stocks them or regularly installs them. Ask specifically, and ask which manufacturers they are certified to install. An improper installation voids the manufacturer warranty regardless of the shingle's rating.
- Color and style options are more limited than the full line of standard architectural shingles.
- If you have a low-slope or flat section of your roof, Class 4 shingles do not apply there at all. That system uses different materials entirely.
- The Class 4 rating covers impact. It does not automatically mean better wind performance, though some Class 4 products are also rated for high wind. Check both specs.

One thing worth repeating: the warranty on any shingle, impact-resistant or not, matters as much as the product rating. Make sure you understand the difference between the manufacturer's material warranty and your contractor's workmanship warranty, and get both in writing before the job starts.

## What to Ask a Roofer Before You Decide

When you are getting quotes, treat impact resistance as one line item in the conversation, not a vague upgrade. Ask:

- What specific Class 4 product are you quoting, and can I see the UL 2218 spec sheet?
- Are you a certified installer for that manufacturer?
- What is the difference in cost per square between this and your standard architectural shingle option?
- What warranty comes with the product, and how do I register it?
- Has your company installed this product on other homes in Sioux Falls? Can I see references?

A roofer who cannot answer those questions clearly is a roofer to be cautious about, regardless of price.
