Water damage is stressful enough on its own, but mold is the problem that can follow you for months if you miss the window to stop it. In Sioux Falls, our humid summers and long heating seasons create conditions where wet building materials stay damp longer than you might expect. If you're dealing with water in your home right now, start with [Water Damage in Your Sioux Falls Home, What to Do](/guides/water-damage-guide-sioux-falls) for the full picture, then come back here for the mold-specific piece.

This article covers how fast mold develops, what the signs look like, how professionals contain and treat it, and what you need to know about protecting your household's health.

## How Fast Mold Starts After Water Damage

Most people are surprised by this: mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours of a water event, under the right conditions.

It doesn't need much. Warmth, moisture, and an organic surface like drywall, wood framing, or carpet padding are enough to get it started.

In Sioux Falls homes, basements are especially risky. The clay soil common throughout the city holds moisture against foundation walls, and a finished basement keeps humidity trapped with less airflow than above-grade spaces.

A furnace closet leak (a situation Sioux Falls homeowners have described in public reviews of local restoration companies) is another common starting point. The warmth of the mechanical equipment, combined with any moisture from a slow leak, creates nearly ideal growing conditions.

The practical takeaway: you don't have days to wait and see. Drying needs to start fast, and you need to know what to look for in the meantime.

## Signs of Mold After a Leak or Flood

Mold isn't always visible right away, especially when it starts inside a wall or under flooring. Here's what to watch for.

**Visible signs:**

- Black, green, or gray patches on drywall, wood, grout, or caulk
- Fuzzy or powdery texture on surfaces that were wet
- Discoloration that spreads outward from a damp area
- Staining that returns after you clean it

**Smell:**

- A musty, earthy odor that doesn't go away with ventilation
- Smell stronger in certain rooms or near certain walls

**Physical symptoms in the home's occupants:**

- Persistent coughing, sneezing, or eye irritation
- Symptoms that improve when you leave the house
- Reactions that are worse in one part of the home

None of these symptoms alone is a diagnosis. But if you're smelling something musty near an area that got wet, treat it as mold until a professional tells you otherwise.

## What Professional Mold Remediation Actually Looks Like

The word "remediation" can sound vague. Here's what the process generally involves, so you know what to expect when a crew shows up.

**Assessment first.** A qualified crew starts by identifying the extent of the growth, including hidden areas behind walls or under floors. Moisture meters and sometimes air sampling help define the scope.

**Containment.** This is a step Sioux Falls homeowners have specifically described in public reviews: crews set up safe zones, sealing off affected areas with plastic sheeting and negative air pressure. This prevents mold spores from spreading to unaffected parts of the house while work is happening.

**Air filtration.** HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during remediation. This matters because disturbing mold releases spores into the air, and filtration captures them before they settle somewhere new.

**Removal.** Drywall, insulation, and other porous materials that are heavily contaminated often have to come out entirely. Cleaning isn't enough when mold has penetrated the material. Non-porous surfaces can usually be cleaned, treated, and dried.

**Verification.** After remediation, the affected area should be tested or visually cleared before reconstruction begins. Ask your contractor what their clearance process looks like and whether they document it.

The rebuilding that follows is a separate phase. For what that involves, [Rebuilding After Water Damage, What to Expect](/guides/rebuilding-after-water-damage) walks through the steps from dry-out to finished walls.

## Health Precautions While Mold Is Present

This section isn't meant to alarm you, but health cautions matter and you deserve the straight version.

Mold exposure can cause respiratory irritation, allergic reactions, and in some people with underlying conditions, more serious effects. Children, older adults, and anyone with asthma or a compromised immune system are generally more sensitive.

While mold is present and before containment is established, limit time in the affected area. Don't run HVAC systems that could circulate spores through ductwork. If you're doing any initial cleanup yourself before pros arrive, wear an N95 respirator, gloves, and goggles.

Don't attempt to clean large areas of mold yourself. The EPA generally defines anything larger than roughly 10 square feet as a job for a professional, because DIY attempts on larger areas often disturb more spores than they remove. That 10-square-foot figure is a widely cited guideline, not a local code requirement, but it's a reasonable line to use.

After professional remediation, ask specifically when it is safe to return to that part of the house. A reputable contractor will give you a clear answer tied to air quality readings, not just an estimate.

## Mold, Insurance, and What to Document

Whether mold-related costs are covered depends heavily on your specific policy and what caused the water damage in the first place. Don't assume.

The South Dakota Division of Insurance advises homeowners to read their policy carefully, file promptly (policies can set notice deadlines), photograph all damage, and not make permanent repairs before contacting the insurer. Keep a log of every phone call and email. If a settlement is offered, ask for an itemized explanation. The Division of Insurance can be reached at 605-773-3563 if you have questions about your rights as a policyholder.

**Document everything before remediation starts:**

- Photograph visible mold from multiple angles
- Note which rooms and surfaces are affected
- Keep any lab results or moisture readings the contractor provides
- Save all invoices and receipts for temporary protective measures

Mold that developed from a sudden event like a burst pipe is handled differently than mold from a long-term slow leak. Your insurer will ask about the timeline, so being accurate matters.
