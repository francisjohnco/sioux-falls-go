Sioux Falls sits in one of the most hail-active corridors in the country, and a single storm can leave homeowners dealing with damaged siding, gutters, windows, and roofing all at once. Knowing what to look for before you call your insurer, or before anyone knocks on your door, puts you in a much stronger position. This checklist walks you through the whole process, from your first walk around the house to the moment you file a claim. For the full picture on what comes after, see [New Siding in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/siding-guide-sioux-falls).

## Walk the House Before You Do Anything Else

Wait until the storm has passed completely and it is safe to go outside. Then do a slow, systematic walk around the perimeter before touching or cleaning anything.

What you are looking for on this first pass:

- Dents, cracks, or chips in siding panels
- Spatter marks, which look like small splashes of removed finish or color
- Torn, missing, or bent pieces
- Damage to window wells, window frames, and trim
- Dented or bent gutters and downspouts
- Marks on fascia boards and soffit panels

Go slowly and work in sections. The north and west faces of a house often take the worst hit from Sioux Falls storms because of the prevailing storm direction, but check every side.

Do not move damaged pieces or pull anything loose yet. Disturbing material before it is documented can complicate your insurance claim.

## What Hail Damage Looks Like on Each Siding Material

Hail damage looks different depending on what your siding is made of. Knowing the signs for your material helps you describe the damage accurately to an adjuster.

**Vinyl siding** is the most common choice in Sioux Falls, and hail shows up as circular cracks, punctures, or a spiderweb fracture pattern. Sometimes a panel looks fine from a distance but has a hairline crack that lets moisture in behind the wall. Run your hand carefully along panels to feel for breaks you cannot easily see.

**Fiber cement** tends to chip or spall at impact points rather than crack through. The damage can look minor on the surface but may expose the substrate underneath to moisture. Check carefully around the edges of panels where chipping often starts.

**Engineered wood** can dent, dislodge, or split at impact points. If the finish coat is broken, moisture gets into the wood composite core quickly. Any split or exposed core needs attention before the next rain.

**Aluminum siding**, less common now but still present in older Sioux Falls neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints, dents visibly and permanently. Even dents that seem cosmetic can break the paint seal and lead to corrosion over time.

For a side-by-side look at how these materials hold up to hail, wind, and deep cold in general, [Vinyl, Fiber Cement or Engineered Wood Siding?](/guides/vinyl-vs-fiber-cement-siding) covers each in detail.

## Take Photos Before Anything Is Touched or Repaired

Documentation is the single most important thing you can do in the first hours after a storm. Insurance adjusters work from evidence, and the more you give them, the smoother the process goes.

How to document well:

- Use your phone and shoot in good natural light, ideally within a day or two of the storm while fresh marks are still visible
- Photograph each wall as a whole, then move in close for individual impact points
- Get the gutters, downspouts, window frames, and any trim pieces in separate shots
- Note the date and time in your phone's photo metadata, or take a picture of that day's newspaper alongside the damage
- If a neighbor had a vehicle dented or a deck damaged in the same storm, that context can support your claim

Do not upload photos directly to a contractor or sign anything before you have submitted them to your insurer yourself. Keep originals in your own files.

## Check the Rest of the House Too

Hail that damages siding almost always hits every exposed surface at the same time. A thorough check prevents you from filing one claim and then discovering more damage weeks later.

Gutters and downspouts are easy to miss but show hail damage clearly as round dents, especially on aluminum gutters. Check the inside of gutter channels too, where paint or finish may be scraped.

Window frames and exterior trim often show pitting or cracking along the leading edge. Basement window wells, which sit below grade and can collect hail, are worth checking for cracked covers or damaged frames.

Your roof is likely taking the same hits as your siding, and a damaged roof carries bigger consequences for the inside of your home. Before you call your insurer, read [Hail Damage on Your Sioux Falls Roof, First Steps](/guides/hail-damage-roof-sioux-falls-first-steps) so you know what to look for and how to document the roof at the same time.

## Temporary Fixes, What Helps and What to Avoid

If a panel is cracked through or missing and rain is in the forecast, a simple temporary cover can limit further damage. But there are right and wrong ways to do this.

Acceptable temporary steps:

- Tape heavy plastic sheeting over a cracked or missing section using exterior tape, with the top edge tucked under the panel above it
- Do not use roofing tar or caulk to seal siding cracks, those products can interfere with proper repair later
- Do not nail, screw, or staple into undamaged panels while trying to secure a temporary fix

Temporary repairs should be photographed before and after. Your insurer needs to see the original damage, and they also need to see that you took reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

Do not attempt to replace panels yourself before the adjuster has seen the damage. Premature repair can void coverage.

## When to Call Your Insurer and What to Tell Them

Call your insurer as soon as your documentation is complete, usually within a day or two of the storm. Most policies have a reporting window, and waiting too long can create problems.

When you call, have ready:

- The date and approximate time of the storm
- A description of each type of damage you found
- The number of photos you have and how you plan to send them
- Your policy number

Ask specifically whether re-siding your home requires a permit under your policy terms. In Sioux Falls, re-siding does require a building permit from the City of Sioux Falls Building Services, and a licensed contractor must pull it. The City requires a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license for re-siding work, and they do not accept out-of-state licenses. You can verify any contractor at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors.

For the full claim process, including having your contractor present at the adjuster visit and handling matching disputes, [Siding and Your Insurance Claim After a Storm](/guides/siding-insurance-claim) walks through each step.

## Storm Chaser Warning Signs

After significant hail, door-to-door contractors appear in Sioux Falls neighborhoods within hours. Some are legitimate local companies, but many are not.

Red flags to watch for:

- They mention your neighbor's house or point to a truck already on the street to create urgency
- They ask you to sign anything before you have spoken to your insurer
- They offer to waive your deductible, which is insurance fraud in South Dakota
- They cannot or will not give you a physical local address
- They pressure you to decide before the end of the day

A legitimate contractor will give you time to verify their license, check references, and read a written contract. The City of Sioux Falls does not accept licenses from other states, so any contractor doing residential work in city limits must appear on the City's currently licensed list. Take your time, storm damage does not go from bad to catastrophic overnight in most cases.
