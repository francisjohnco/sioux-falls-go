Tree removal, especially of large trees near structures, carries real safety and liability risk — which makes verification more important here than for many other home services.

## What to verify first

- **Liability insurance**, specifically covering property damage. This isn't optional given the real risk of a falling limb damaging a home, vehicle, or neighboring property.
- **ISA certification**, if available — a meaningful signal of proper training, though not the only valid signal of quality.
- **Experience with trees near power lines**, if relevant to your situation — this requires specific technique and sometimes utility coordination.

## Questions worth asking

1. **"Are you insured for property damage, not just general liability?"** Ask to see proof, not just a verbal assurance.
2. **"How do you handle a tree near power lines?"** A company with real experience here should describe a clear process, potentially including utility company coordination.
3. **"Is debris cleanup and hauling included in the quote?"** Get this specified, not assumed.
4. **"What's your plan if weather changes mid-job?"** Sioux Falls weather can shift quickly; a professional company has a real answer here.

## Red flags

- No proof of insurance, or reluctance to provide it
- A bid dramatically lower than others for comparable large-tree work
- No mention of debris cleanup in the quote
- Rushing to start work without a clear written scope

## Why this matters more after an ice storm

Right after a major Sioux Falls ice storm, demand for tree removal spikes, and unfortunately so does the number of underinsured or unqualified operators taking advantage of the urgency. Taking the extra few minutes to verify insurance and ask the right questions matters even more in a post-storm rush than during calmer, planned work.
