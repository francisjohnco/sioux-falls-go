A bathroom remodel is one of the most disruptive projects a homeowner can take on, and also one of the most rewarding when it goes right. Sioux Falls homeowners put off remodels for years, often because they assume the cost will be enormous or they will lose their only bathroom for weeks on end. This guide walks every stage of a bathroom remodel in Sioux Falls from the first idea to the final walkthrough, with the local facts and questions that actually matter.

## Deciding How Much to Remodel

The first decision is scope, and it shapes everything else: cost, timeline, permits, and how complicated your life gets while the work is happening.

A cosmetic refresh (new fixtures, a vanity swap, fresh paint) is a very different project from moving a toilet or converting a tub to a walk-in shower. Both are bathroom remodels, but they involve completely different trades, timelines, and permit requirements.

Ask yourself these questions before you call anyone:

- Is the layout working, or do you need to move plumbing?
- Are you keeping the tub, converting to a shower, or adding both?
- Is this room for the long term, or are you thinking about resale?
- Do you have one bathroom or a backup while this one is out of service?
- Is anything already failing, like soft flooring, loose tile, or a leaking fixture?

Your answers will tell a remodeler what kind of project they are walking into, and they will tell you which sections of this guide matter most for your situation.

## What Drives the Cost of a Bathroom Remodel

Cost is the reason most Sioux Falls homeowners delay a remodel. The range is wide, and the number a friend paid for their remodel may have almost nothing to do with what yours will cost.

The biggest cost drivers are scope, whether plumbing moves, the tile and fixture choices you make, and what turns up behind the walls once demo starts. Prefabricated shower surrounds cost less than custom tile. Swapping a fixture in place costs far less than relocating a drain.

Hidden damage is real. A project that starts as cracked grout can open up into rotted subfloor, damaged framing, or a moisture problem that has been spreading for years. A good contractor will tell you upfront how they handle change orders if that happens. A good homeowner keeps a contingency budget for it.

Always ask for an itemized written bid. That is the only way to compare two contractors fairly and understand exactly what is and is not included. For a deeper look at every cost factor and how to read a bid, see [What Drives the Cost of a Bathroom Remodel in Sioux Falls](/guides/bathroom-remodel-cost-sioux-falls).

## Tub or Shower: Making the Right Call

One of the most common decisions in a Sioux Falls bathroom remodel is what to do with the tub. Many homeowners have a whirlpool or soaking tub that never gets used, and that footprint is exactly the size of a generous walk-in shower.

A prefabricated tub-to-shower conversion can be installed in a single day. That surprises a lot of people. It is not the right choice for every bathroom, but for a secondary bath or a household where nobody uses the tub, it is fast, clean, and far less expensive than custom tile work.

Custom tile takes longer, costs more, and gives you more design flexibility. It also requires careful waterproofing behind the walls, which a one-day prefabricated system handles differently. Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on your budget, your timeline, how long you plan to stay in the home, and whether you need to keep a tub somewhere in the house for resale purposes.

Older homeowners and couples planning to age in place have another layer to consider: threshold height, grab bar blocking in the walls, and whether the entry is safe to step into at any age. That topic gets its own section below.

For a full comparison of one-day systems versus custom tile, including waterproofing and resale considerations, read [Tub to Shower Conversion, One Day Install or Custom Tile?](/guides/tub-to-shower-conversion).

## How Long You Will Lose the Room (and How to Survive It)

Losing a bathroom is the fear that keeps more Sioux Falls homeowners from starting than almost anything else, especially in a one-bathroom house.

The timeline for a bathroom remodel depends on scope, the number of trades involved, how quickly inspections are scheduled, and what turns up during demo. A cosmetic refresh moves faster than a full gut renovation with new plumbing and tile. Neither has a guaranteed day count, and any contractor who gives you a hard finish date without knowing your specific project is guessing.

The stages of a typical full remodel, in order, look like this:

- Demo and removal
- Rough plumbing and electrical (before walls close)
- City inspections (required before walls close)
- Waterproofing and backer board
- Tile and mud work (which needs cure time)
- Fixtures, vanity, and trim
- Punch list and final walkthrough

Each stage has things that can slow it down: material delays, inspection scheduling, hidden damage discovered during demo, or a subcontractor who is not available when needed.

If you have one bathroom, plan your temporary setup before demo starts, not after. Know where you will shower, what your household's morning routine looks like, and how you will communicate with your contractor if something runs long. For a full look at the stages and practical strategies for a one-bathroom household, read [How Long a Bathroom Remodel Takes and Living Without One](/guides/bathroom-remodel-timeline).

## Permits and Licensed Trades in Sioux Falls

Permits are not optional, and in Sioux Falls the rules are specific enough that homeowners need to understand them before signing a contract.

According to the City of Sioux Falls, a Residential Building Contractor's License is required of all contractors working on residential one and two family homes and townhomes within city limits. A plumbing contractor license is also required for anyone doing plumbing work in the city, and the City does not accept licenses from other states.

The City's Building Permit FAQs make clear that a building permit does not cover electrical, mechanical, or plumbing work unless licensed contractors in those trades perform that work. If you are a homeowner doing work yourself in your own single-family home, you can pull your own electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits, but those permits cannot be issued to a friend or family member doing the work for you.

The City published an April 2026 reminder that projects touching a home's building, electrical, mechanical, or plumbing systems require permits to protect life safety and building quality. If your home is outside city limits in Minnehaha County, permits go through the county's Planning and Zoning office instead of the City.

The City publishes a Currently Licensed Contractors list you can check before hiring anyone. Ask every contractor for their license number and verify it. For the full picture of what triggers a permit and why unpermitted work creates problems at resale, read [Bathroom Remodel Permits in Sioux Falls, What Needs One](/guides/bathroom-remodel-permits-sioux-falls).

## Hard Water and What It Does to a New Bathroom

Sioux Falls water is hard, and it matters more than most homeowners realize when they are picking fixtures and finishes for a remodel.

According to the City of Sioux Falls 2020 finished water quality report, total hardness was about 15 grains per gallon for water treated at the City's own plant, and about 10 grains per gallon for Lewis and Clark water. Which source a home receives, and how hard the water actually is, can vary by location.

Water that hard leaves scale on glass shower doors, spots on chrome fixtures, buildup around showerheads, and residue that works into grout over time. It is not a reason to avoid certain finishes, but it should change which finishes you choose and how you plan to maintain them.

Some finishes are easier to clean and show scale less than others. Frameless glass doors look beautiful but require consistent maintenance in Sioux Falls water. Certain grout colors hide buildup better than others. These are conversations to have with your tile installer and contractor before anything is ordered. For a full look at how hardness affects every part of a new bathroom and what to ask a plumber about softening options, see [Hard Water and Your New Bathroom in Sioux Falls](/guides/hard-water-bathroom-sioux-falls).

## What Hides Behind Old Tile

One of the most consistent things Sioux Falls homeowners report after a remodel is surprise at what was behind the walls. What started as a cracked grout line or a soft spot in the floor turned into rotted subfloor, compromised framing, or water damage that had been spreading silently for years.

This is not unusual. Bathroom tile is not inherently waterproof. Grout and caulk fail over time, and once water finds a path behind the tile, it moves into the substrate, the framing, and sometimes into the ceiling or basement below.

Warning signs to look for before demo even starts:

- Soft or spongy spots on the floor near the toilet or tub
- Tile that moves or feels hollow when you press it
- Staining on the ceiling of the room below the bathroom
- A musty smell that does not go away with cleaning
- Grout that keeps cracking in the same spots

If any of these are present, tell your contractor before work begins, not after. It affects how the job is scoped and priced. It also affects the change order conversation, which you should have in writing before work starts.

A new shower or tub surround that is not waterproofed correctly behind the walls will cause the same damage all over again. Ask your contractor specifically how they waterproof before tile goes up. For everything you need to know about hidden damage, waterproofing, and what to do if water has already spread, read [What Remodelers Find Under Old Bathroom Tile](/guides/hidden-water-damage-bathroom).

## Vanities, Storage, and Small Bathroom Realities

Most Sioux Falls homes, especially in older neighborhoods like McKennan Park and All Saints, have bathrooms that are genuinely small. The original vanity might be 24 inches wide. The door swings into the room. There is no linen closet.

Storage and vanity choices in a small bathroom are not just aesthetic. They affect how the room functions every day, whether the door can open fully, and whether two people can use the space at the same time.

A floating vanity can make a small floor look larger. A medicine cabinet recessed into the wall adds storage without taking floor or counter space. Niches tiled into the shower wall eliminate the need for caddies that collect scale and mold.

Lighting at the mirror matters more than most homeowners budget for. A single overhead fixture leaves shadows on a face. Side-mounted or bar lighting at mirror height is more functional and not necessarily more expensive. For sizing guidance, clearance requirements, and material choices that hold up to steam and hard water, read [Choosing a Vanity and Storage for a Small Sioux Falls Bathroom](/guides/small-bathroom-vanity-storage).

## Aging in Place: Planning a Bathroom You Can Use for Decades

A growing number of Sioux Falls homeowners remodeling their bathrooms are thinking past aesthetics. They want a shower they can step into safely now and in twenty years, without the room feeling institutional.

The good news is that most aging-in-place features can be built into a remodel invisibly. Blocking in the walls for future grab bars looks like a standard wall from the outside but means bars can be added later without opening up the tile. A curbless shower entry is also easier to clean. A comfort-height toilet is comfortable at any age.

Features worth asking your contractor about if this is a concern:

- Low or no-threshold shower entry
- Blocking in shower walls for grab bars (now or at rough-in)
- A fold-down or built-in seat in the shower
- A handheld showerhead on a slide bar
- Wider doorway if the opening is narrow
- Slip-resistant floor tile
- Comfort height toilet

Talk to a doctor or occupational therapist about specific needs before the design is finalized. ## Choosing a Remodeler and What to Ask Before You Sign

Meeting a remodeler at a home show, getting a recommendation from a neighbor, or finding someone online are all reasonable starting points. None of them replace checking credentials and getting the right questions answered before you sign anything.

Verify that every contractor has a current City of Sioux Falls Residential Building Contractor's License. Verify that any plumber they bring in has a city plumbing license, since the City does not accept out-of-state licenses. Ask who specifically will be in your home, including any subcontractors, and whether the contractor has worked with those subs before. One common homeowner complaint is a remodeler bringing in a painter or other trade they had never used, with results that did not match the rest of the work.

Ask these questions in writing before you sign:

- What is included in the bid and what is explicitly excluded?
- How are hidden damage and change orders priced and approved?
- What is the payment schedule, and when is the final payment due?
- What is the warranty, and is it in writing?
- Who pulls the permits, and can I see them?
- What does the final walkthrough include?

At the walkthrough, run every fixture. Check the ceiling or floor of the room below for any sign of drips. Check that nothing was left inside the showerhead or supply lines. Confirm the exhaust fan vents to the outside, not into the attic.
