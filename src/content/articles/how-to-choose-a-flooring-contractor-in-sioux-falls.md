Flooring installation quality depends heavily on subfloor prep, which is exactly the part homeowners can't easily judge for themselves — making contractor selection more important than with some other home services.

## What to check before hiring

- **Real local reviews and completed projects**, ideally recent ones in homes similar in age to yours.
- **Whether they address subfloor condition directly**, not just the visible flooring material.
- **Manufacturer certification**, if you're installing a premium product with a warranty tied to professional installation.

## Questions worth asking

1. **"Will you inspect the subfloor before finalizing the quote?"** This matters more than almost any other question — subfloor issues are the most common cause of flooring problems down the line.
2. **"What's included in removal and disposal of my existing flooring?"** Get this in writing, not just discussed verbally.
3. **"Do you recommend engineered or solid hardwood for this room, and why?"** A contractor who considers humidity and location (basement vs. main floor) in the recommendation is thinking about your home specifically, not just selling a product.
4. **"What's your warranty on installation labor, separate from the manufacturer's material warranty?"**

## Red flags

- A quote with no mention of subfloor condition
- Pressure toward the most expensive material without discussing your home's specific conditions
- No physical showroom or verifiable local presence
- Reluctance to provide references from recent local jobs

## Material recommendations by space

For below-grade basements, most local flooring pros recommend vinyl plank or tile over solid hardwood, given Sioux Falls's seasonal moisture patterns. For main living areas, engineered hardwood offers a good balance of look and stability against our humidity swings. A contractor who walks you through these tradeoffs — rather than just quoting whatever you initially asked for — is worth extra consideration.
