Sioux Falls winters do not ease into things. One week you are raking leaves, and the next a Canadian air mass is pushing temperatures below zero across the prairie. According to SiouxFalls.Business, the February 2021 cold snap was the longest stretch of extreme cold the city had seen since about 1996, and local technicians reported a surge in spring and opener failures right alongside it. Cold changes how metal contracts, how lubricants thicken, and how rubber seals behave, so the fall is exactly the right time to run through a solid garage door maintenance checklist before those conditions arrive.

For a full picture of how garage door systems work and what repairs cost when something does break, start with [Garage Door Repair in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/garage-door-repair-sioux-falls). This article is narrower: a step-by-step checklist you can work through on a dry Saturday afternoon in September or October.

## Lubricate the Right Parts with the Right Product

This is the single most effective thing you can do to winterize your garage door, and it is also the step people most often do wrong.

Use a lithium-based spray lubricant or a product specifically labeled for garage doors. Do not use WD-40 on the mechanical parts. WD-40 is a solvent and water displacer, not a long-term lubricant, and it can wash away the grease that is already doing its job on springs and hinges.

Apply lubricant to these parts:

- Torsion or extension springs (a light coat along the full length)
- All hinges, including the ones at the top of each panel
- Rollers, but only on the stem and bearing, not the nylon wheel itself if yours are nylon
- The two curved sections of track where the door bends
- The lock and lock bar if your door has a manual lock

Do not lubricate the tracks themselves. The rollers are supposed to roll inside a dry track. Adding grease to the tracks causes buildup that attracts grit and eventually makes the door run rough or loud. ## Test the Balance and Auto-Reverse Safety

A balanced door is easier on your opener motor, your springs, and your cables. Sioux Falls winters punish any weakness, so catching a balance problem in October is far better than discovering it at 6 a.m. in January.

Here is how to test balance:

1. Disconnect the opener by pulling the red emergency release cord.
2. Manually lift the door to about waist height and let go.
3. It should stay in place or drift only slightly. If it falls or shoots up, the springs are out of balance.

Do not adjust spring tension yourself. Springs are under serious stored energy and are responsible for a significant share of serious garage door injuries. If the door fails the balance test, call a technician.

The auto-reverse test is equally important and easier to do safely:

1. Reconnect the opener.
2. Place a 2x4 flat on the ground in the center of the door opening.
3. Close the door using the wall button.
4. When the door contacts the board, it must reverse direction within two seconds.

If it does not reverse, the sensitivity settings need adjustment or the system needs service. This is a real safety feature, not a convenience. Learn more about how the sensor side of that system works in [Garage Door Sensors Not Working? Simple Checks First](/guides/garage-door-sensors-not-working).

## Inspect Rollers, Cables, and Hardware

Cold amplifies small problems. A roller that is just starting to crack will get worse faster when temperatures drop into the single digits.

Check rollers by looking at each one as you slowly raise and lower the door manually. You are looking for:

- Cracked or chipped nylon wheels
- Rollers that wobble rather than roll smoothly
- Stems that are bent or visibly worn at the bracket

Cables run along each side of the door and bear the weight when the door opens. Look at the full length of each cable for:

- Fraying or any broken strands
- Kinks or bends
- Rust or corrosion near the drum or bottom bracket

If you see fraying or broken strands, stop using the door and call for service. A snapped cable can allow the door to drop suddenly. For more detail on why cables fail and what repair involves, see [Broken Garage Door Cable, Causes and Repair](/guides/broken-garage-door-cable).

Also check every bolt and nut on the track brackets and door hinges. Vibration loosens hardware over time. Tighten anything that moves with a wrench, but do not overtighten to the point of stripping.

## Check the Bottom Seal, Threshold, and Weatherstripping

Sioux Falls gets plenty of snow every winter, and blowing prairie wind pushes that snow into every gap it can find. A good bottom seal and tight weatherstripping keep cold air, drifted snow, and water out of the garage.

Run your hand along the bottom seal when the door is closed. The rubber or vinyl should contact the floor evenly across the full width. If you can see daylight anywhere, or if the seal is cracked and brittle, replace it before the freeze-thaw cycles start in earnest. A cracked seal also lets in water, which refreezes at the threshold and can freeze your door to the floor overnight.

Clear the threshold of any dirt, leaves, or debris before the season starts. Some homeowners add a low rubber threshold seal to the floor itself to close gaps caused by an uneven concrete slab.

Also check the weatherstripping along the top and both sides of the door frame. Press it with your fingers. If it has gone hard or is tearing away from the frame, it needs replacement. This work is straightforward for most homeowners and requires no special tools.

## Test the Opener's Backup Battery

Modern openers with battery backup allow you to open the garage door during a power outage. Sioux Falls ice storms can knock out power for hours, and the last thing you want is to discover the backup battery is dead while your car is stuck inside.

Here is how to test it:

1. Unplug the opener from its outlet.
2. Try to open and close the door using the wall button or remote.
3. The door should operate, though possibly more slowly than usual.
4. Plug the opener back in.

If the door does not move on battery power, the battery likely needs replacement. Most opener batteries are standard sizes and available at hardware stores. The opener manual lists the correct type. If your opener is older and does not have a battery backup feature at all, this is a reasonable time to evaluate whether an upgrade makes sense. [Garage Door Opener Repair or Replace? How to Decide](/guides/garage-door-opener-repair-or-replace) covers what to look for when making that call.

## A Note on Contractors and Permits in Sioux Falls

If your inspection turns up something beyond routine maintenance, you will want to hire a qualified company. According to the City of Sioux Falls, homeowners can check whether a contractor holds a current city license at siouxfalls.gov/licensed-contractors or by calling Building Services at 605-367-8672. Since February 2024, the city also offers a Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license for certain smaller jobs including door replacements on homes with no structural changes. Because garage doors are not specifically named in the city's announcement, ask any company you call which city license it holds before work begins.

For structural changes, such as widening the opening or modifying the header, a building permit is required. For a same-size door replacement, ask the installer directly or contact Planning and Development Services at 605-367-8670. Getting that answer in writing protects you if any question comes up later.

Several Sioux Falls garage door companies offer evening and weekend service, and some provide emergency response. Ask about after-hours fees before you book so the final bill matches what you expected.
