After a significant hail event, the knocking starts fast. Sometimes it begins the same afternoon, before you have even walked your yard to look at the damage yourself. These contractors, often called storm chasers, travel from city to city following severe weather, and Sioux Falls gets its share of them. The [Roof Repair and Replacement in Sioux Falls, the Complete Guide](/guides/roof-repair-replacement-sioux-falls) covers the full picture of finding a legitimate roofer, but this article focuses on one specific threat: the high-pressure sales visit at your door after a storm.

After the July 25, 2026 storms that brought 72 mph gusts at the Sioux Falls airport and 77 mph winds near Harrisburg, according to the National Weather Service Sioux Falls, neighborhoods across the southern metro had real damage. Legitimate roofers got busy fast. So did storm chasers.

## The Door-Knock Pattern After a Sioux Falls Hailstorm

Storm chasers have a playbook, and it rarely changes.

They arrive in pickup trucks, sometimes with magnetic logos on the door that can be swapped out or removed. They target neighborhoods where hail was confirmed, often working street by street.

The opening line is usually something like: "I was just doing work down the street and noticed your roof took a hit." They want to get on your roof immediately, and they want you to sign something before they leave.

The pitch moves fast on purpose. They know that if you have time to think, compare, and check references, they lose the sale. The pressure is the product.

Common opening moves to recognize:

- Offering a "free inspection" and then climbing your roof before you agree to anything
- Claiming they can "get you a new roof for just your deductible"
- Saying the offer or the crew availability expires today
- Asking you to sign an "insurance assignment" or a contract before an adjuster has even looked at your roof
- Requesting a large cash deposit on the spot

## Red Flags That Signal a Roofing Scam in South Dakota

The South Dakota Attorney General's consumer protection office specifically warns homeowners after hailstorms to watch for pressure to sign quickly, cash-only payment demands, and contractors who cannot show proof of insurance or a bond. Those are not edge cases. They are the standard storm-chaser toolkit.

Here is the full list of warning signs to know:

- **Out-of-state license plates.** A crew from Missouri or Texas is not necessarily dishonest, but it is a reason to verify everything more carefully. Local accountability matters when something goes wrong six months later.
- **No city license.** In Sioux Falls, a contractor doing shingle work on a home must hold a city Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license (or the broader Residential Building Contractor license), according to the City of Sioux Falls. That license requires a $20,000 compliance bond and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance. A storm chaser from out of town almost certainly does not have it.
- **Cash only or a large upfront deposit.** The Attorney General advises against paying in full before the job is done. A large deposit before materials are even ordered is a red flag.
- **No South Dakota sales or contractor's excise tax license.** The Attorney General specifically recommends asking to see one. A legitimate contractor working in South Dakota has it.
- **Pressure to sign an insurance assignment agreement at the door.** This document can transfer your rights over the insurance claim to the contractor, limiting your control over the process and the payout.
- **No written contract with a detailed scope and price.** Vague agreements protect the contractor, not you.
- **Reluctance to provide local references.** A crew that moves city to city has no local track record to point you to.

## Your Three-Day Right to Cancel

This is one of the most important protections you have, and many homeowners do not know it exists.

Under South Dakota law, door-to-door sales contracts typically carry a three-day right to cancel. If you sign a contract at your home with a salesperson who came to your door, you generally have three business days to cancel it in writing with no penalty.

If you cancel, you are entitled to a refund within 10 days, according to the South Dakota Attorney General.

What this means in practice: even if you felt pressured and signed something, you likely have time to undo it. Do not wait. Cancel in writing, keep a copy, and send it in a way you can document, such as certified mail or email with a read receipt.

This right exists specifically because door-to-door sales create pressure that in-store or online purchases do not. The law accounts for that.

## How to Check a Contractor's License Before You Agree to Anything

Checking a city license takes a few minutes and costs you nothing.

For the City of Sioux Falls, call Building Services licensing at **605-367-8670**. Ask whether the company name or the individual holds a current Residential Roofing and Repair Contractor license or Residential Building Contractor license. You can also ask whether a permit has been pulled for your address (legitimate roofers pull permits; storm chasers usually do not).

For state licensing, ask the contractor to show you their South Dakota contractor's excise tax license before you sign anything. This is a reasonable, standard request. A hesitation or a runaround on this question tells you something.

This Sioux Falls homeowner roofing resource on [storm chaser contractors and how to identify them](https://siouxfallsroofs.com/storm-chaser-contractors-sioux-falls/) goes into additional detail on the verification steps specific to this area.

Steps to take before signing anything:

1. Get the contractor's full legal business name and license number.
2. Call Building Services at 605-367-8670 to verify the city license.
3. Ask for proof of general liability insurance and the bond.
4. Ask to see the South Dakota contractor's excise tax license.
5. Look up the company name and check for complaints with the Attorney General.
6. Ask for at least two local references from jobs completed in the past year.

## Who to Call If You Think You Were Scammed

If you have already signed something and believe you are dealing with a fraudulent contractor, or if a contractor has taken your money and disappeared, you have real options.

**South Dakota Attorney General, Consumer Protection:** 1-800-300-1986. The Attorney General's office handles roofing fraud complaints and can advise you on your rights, including the three-day cancel right and next steps if money has already changed hands.

**City of Sioux Falls, Building Services:** 605-367-8670. If work was done without a permit or by an unlicensed contractor, report it here. Unpermitted work can create serious problems when you sell the home or file a future insurance claim.

**South Dakota Division of Insurance:** 605-773-3563. If the dispute involves your insurance claim, the assignment agreement the contractor had you sign, or how your insurer is handling things, the Division can explain your rights and accept complaints about insurer conduct.

Do not assume a problem will resolve itself. Storm chasers move on to the next city. Getting a complaint filed quickly matters.
