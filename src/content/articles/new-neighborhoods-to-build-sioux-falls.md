If you are ready to build a custom home in Sioux Falls, one of the first real decisions is where you are going to put it. The lot shapes everything else: your builder options, your commute, your school district, your drainage situation, and ultimately a big chunk of your budget. The good news is that 2026 has brought several notable new developments to the market, ranging from golf course-adjacent lots in the mid-$100,000s to premium northeast Sioux Falls parcels pushing past $300,000. Before you go too far into plans and builder conversations, read through [How to Build a Custom Home in Sioux Falls (2026 Guide)](/guides/build-custom-home-sioux-falls) so you understand how lot selection fits into the full sequence of decisions. Then come back here and dig into the specific neighborhoods that are actively selling lots right now.

One important caveat before you read any price or availability detail below: lots sell. What was available in spring 2026 may already be spoken for by the time you read this. Every figure in this article comes from reporting by SiouxFalls.Business in April 2026, and you should confirm current lot availability, pricing, and any neighborhood covenants directly with the developer before you make any decisions.

## The Sanctuary: Northeast Sioux Falls's Most Active Premium Development

If you have been watching the Sioux Falls lot market at all, you have probably heard The Sanctuary come up. According to SiouxFalls.Business reporting from April 2026, this Deffenbaugh Homes development sits on roughly 200 acres in northeast Sioux Falls and includes 160 lots. The average lot list price was around $333,000 in spring 2026, and the development accounted for somewhere between 70 and 75 percent of all Sioux Falls lot sales above $200,000 during that period. Those numbers tell you something important: if you are shopping in the upper tier of the Sioux Falls lot market, there is a real chance your search starts and ends here.

What drives the price? Northeast Sioux Falls has seen consistent demand, and a larger, master-planned development with controlled architectural standards tends to hold value differently than a scattered infill lot. That said, a $333,000 lot is a significant line item before you have poured a single footing. Make sure you understand what is included in that price, what the covenants require, and whether the development restricts you to a specific builder or list of builders. Those details matter enormously for your total project budget. If you want to understand how lot cost fits into the full picture, [What a Custom Home Costs to Build in Sioux Falls](/guides/custom-home-cost-sioux-falls) breaks down lot, site work, construction, and soft costs in one place.

Also worth noting: northeast Sioux Falls sits on the same expansive clay soil and freeze-thaw pattern as the rest of the metro. The 42-inch frost line required by Sioux Falls building code applies here, and drainage and grading on your specific lot should be evaluated before you sign anything, not after.

## Mapleton Highlands and the Van Buskirk Portfolio

Van Buskirk Companies has been one of the more active lot developers in the Sioux Falls market heading into 2026, and their flagship offering right now is Mapleton Highlands, located near Mapleton Golf Club. According to SiouxFalls.Business (April 2026), the development opened with 66 lots and has 60 more planned. Pricing in spring 2026 ran from roughly $150,000 to $275,000 for standard lots, with golf course-adjacent lots listed above $550,000.

That spread is wide enough to serve very different buyers. A $160,000 lot in Mapleton Highlands and a $560,000 golf course lot are essentially different products that happen to share a neighborhood boundary. If you are drawn to the development because of the golf course views, be honest with yourself about what those premium lots will add to your all-in cost and what your construction budget looks like on top of that.

Van Buskirk also has several other active developments worth knowing about. Westwater at Cherry Lake is planned for the far west side of Sioux Falls and would include around 150 lots next to a private lake, according to the same SiouxFalls.Business reporting. Willow Ridge is in northeast Sioux Falls near Fred Assam Elementary. River Park is located in Brandon, and Heritage Pond is in Crooks. Each of these sits in a different jurisdiction, which matters for permits, utilities, and the approval process. Homes built in Brandon go through Brandon's city offices, Crooks goes through Crooks or Lincoln County depending on the parcel, and neither uses Sioux Falls Building Services. That is covered in more detail in [Building Outside Sioux Falls City Limits, What Changes](/guides/building-outside-sioux-falls-city-limits) if Brandon or Crooks appeals to you.

For families with school-age kids, Willow Ridge near Fred Assam Elementary has an obvious draw. Confirm current school boundary assignments with the Sioux Falls School District before assuming a specific lot feeds a specific school, since boundaries do get adjusted as neighborhoods grow.

## Empire Companies: Mixed-Use Options in SouthLoop and 57th Street

Empire Companies announced two distinct projects that are worth understanding if your lifestyle fits something other than a traditional single-family suburban lot. According to SiouxFalls.Business (April 2026), Empire is developing a 75-acre project off 57th Street between Highway 11 and Six Mile Road. The plan includes single-family homes, villas, townhomes, and twin homes, so there is a range of product types within a single development rather than one uniform lot style.

Empire is also behind SouthLoop, located off 69th Street and Sycamore Avenue, which is planned to include additional townhomes and twin homes. If you are interested in a villa or twin home as a lower-maintenance option, these projects are relevant to your search. The SiouxFalls.Business reporting from April 2026 noted that many buyers in the premium lot segment were relocating from Minnesota and Iowa, often in the 53-to-65 age range, and that a significant number were specifically requesting slab-on-grade homes with no basement. If that describes your situation, the villa and twin home inventory in Empire's projects may line up with what you are looking for. The article [Basement or Slab on Grade in Sioux Falls?](/guides/basement-or-slab-sioux-falls) is worth reading before you finalize that decision, since the tradeoffs in Sioux Falls's climate are real.

As with any development, check whether Empire's projects require you to use a specific builder or select from an approved list. Some developers tie lot purchases to their own construction arm or a short roster of preferred builders, and that affects how much flexibility you have on design and price.

## The Bigger Picture: What the 2026 Market Is Telling You

The Sioux Falls lot market in spring 2026 is active but showing signs of cooling from the pace of a year earlier. SiouxFalls.Business reported in April 2026 that the city had 604 single-family homes and townhomes under construction as of April 1, down from 821 at the same point in 2025. New residential construction value in the first quarter of 2026 came in at $23.8 million, compared to $32.3 million in the first quarter of 2025. Those are meaningful shifts, and they suggest that buyers have more negotiating room than they did at the peak, though premium lots in established developments like The Sanctuary were still moving quickly.

One data point that stands out from the same SiouxFalls.Business reporting: more than 75 percent of buyers in the top lot segment paid cash. If you are financing your lot and construction through a loan, understand that you may be competing with cash buyers in the premium tier, and that lenders often have specific requirements around lot purchases and construction sequencing. Getting your financing structure sorted early gives you more credibility with developers and builders alike.

If you want to visit new homes before committing to a neighborhood, the Sioux Empire Parade of Homes runs twice a year, in spring and fall, with homes open from 1 to 5 PM on parade weekends. Details are at siouxempireparadeofhomes.com, and the Home Builders Association of the Sioux Empire is involved in organizing it. Walking homes in the neighborhoods you are considering is one of the best ways to get a feel for quality, layout, and what builders are actually delivering in a given development.

## What to Do Before You Buy Any Lot

Regardless of which development appeals to you, do not skip the due diligence steps that experienced buyers take for granted. Visit the lot in person at different times of day. Walk the grade and look at how water would sheet off the lot toward (or away from) the foundation. Ask the developer for the covenants and architectural review requirements in writing, not a verbal summary. Find out whether the lot has access to city water and sewer already or whether you are waiting on extension of utilities, and ask who pays for that connection.

If the lot is inside Sioux Falls city limits, your permits will go through City of Sioux Falls Building Services, located on the first floor of City Center at 231 N. Dakota Avenue. You can reach them at 605-367-8670 for general questions or 605-367-8672 to verify a contractor's license. Permit fees start at $40 and increase with the value of construction, with separate fees for electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits. Work must begin and an inspection be requested within 180 days of permit issuance, so timing your permit to match your actual construction start matters.

For lots in Brandon, Crooks, or unincorporated areas of Minnehaha or Lincoln County, contact that jurisdiction directly. The rules, fees, and timelines are different, and your builder needs to know which authority is issuing permits before they begin any site work. And before any digging begins on any lot, call 811 (South Dakota One Call) to have underground utilities marked. That step is not optional.
