Sioux Falls summers arrive fast and push older air conditioners hard. A unit that limped through last July may not survive this one, and by the time it fails on a 95-degree afternoon, every HVAC company in town has a full schedule. Knowing the signs of a system near the end of its life, before the heat hits, is one of the most practical things a homeowner can do. For a full picture of how heating and cooling decisions fit together, start with [Heating and Cooling in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/hvac-guide-sioux-falls).

## How Old Is Too Old for a Central AC in Sioux Falls

Most central air conditioners are designed for roughly 15 to 20 years of service. That range assumes regular maintenance, clean filters, and a system that was properly sized from the start.

Sioux Falls puts real stress on that timeline. The climate swings from brutal winters to humid summers, and those freeze-thaw cycles affect the outdoor condenser unit sitting on your foundation pad year after year. A unit that hits 15 years here has worked hard.

Age alone is not a reason to replace. But age combined with any of the signs below is worth taking seriously. A good rule of thumb: if the unit is over 12 years old and needs a repair that costs more than half what a new system would, replacement is usually the smarter financial move.

Ask yourself these questions:

- Has it needed repairs two or more times in the past few years?
- Is it struggling to cool the house on hot days even when it runs constantly?
- Are your summer electricity bills noticeably higher than a few years ago?
- Does it make noises it did not used to make?

If the answer to two or more of these is yes, start getting quotes before summer starts.

## The Refrigerant Question and Why R-22 Changes Your Decision

If your AC was installed before 2010, there is a good chance it uses R-22 refrigerant, which was phased out under federal environmental rules. As of January 2020, R-22 can no longer be manufactured or imported in the United States.

Existing stockpiles are still legal to use for repairs, but supply is limited and prices have risen significantly. If a technician tells you that your R-22 system has a refrigerant leak, the repair cost can be substantial because of that supply situation.

This is one of the clearest cases where AC replacement timing becomes straightforward. Recharging a leaking R-22 system does not fix the leak, and you will likely face the same repair again. A new system uses R-410A or the newer R-454B, which are widely available.

Ask your technician directly: what refrigerant type does my current system use, and what does that mean for future repairs? Get the answer in writing as part of any repair quote.

## Matching Your AC Replacement to Your Furnace

This one surprises a lot of homeowners: in a forced-air system, the air conditioner and furnace share the indoor coil and blower. If one is significantly older than the other, replacing just the AC can create a mismatch.

Manufacturers design their systems to work together. Installing a new high-efficiency AC on an aging furnace may prevent the new unit from reaching its rated efficiency, and it can void the manufacturer's warranty on the new equipment. Ask any contractor quoting a replacement to explain in writing how they plan to handle the indoor coil and whether the furnace age affects the warranty.

If your furnace is also over 15 years old, replacing both at the same time often makes financial sense. A contractor can usually offer a better total price for a combined install than two separate jobs, and you get one unified warranty start date. ## Why Scheduling Before Summer Demand Matters in Sioux Falls

The busiest weeks for every HVAC company in Sioux Falls are June through early August, and they fill up fast. Homeowners who discover a failed AC on a 90-degree day often wait days for an appointment or pay emergency rates.

If your unit is showing signs of age or has needed repairs recently, the smart move is to get one or two replacement quotes in April or May. That is not to pressure yourself into buying something you do not need. It is so you have information before you are in a hot house with no leverage.

Scheduling installation in the spring also gives contractors more flexibility to work around your schedule. Some manufacturers offer promotional pricing on equipment in the shoulder season, though prices vary and you should ask specifically what is available rather than assume any discount applies.

One practical step: check that any contractor you call is properly licensed. According to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs, the city licenses mechanical contractors separately from general contractors, and a mechanical permit is required for AC replacement. You can verify a contractor's license by calling 605-367-8672 or checking the city's Currently Licensed Contractors list online. This matters because a permit creates an inspection record that protects you at resale.

## What to Ask Before You Say Yes to a Replacement

Getting a quote is not a commitment. It is information. Here is what to ask every contractor who comes out:

- What is the age and condition of my current system, in writing?
- What SEER2 rating are you quoting, and why?
- Does my furnace age affect the warranty on a new AC?
- What permits will you pull, and who schedules the inspection?
- What does the quote include, specifically, for indoor coil work?
- What is the warranty on parts and on labor, separately?

Sioux Falls homeowners have reported in public reviews getting very different prices for the same work. If a quote surprises you in either direction, a second opinion is reasonable and costs nothing. See [When to Get a Second Opinion on an HVAC Repair](/guides/hvac-second-opinion) for how to compare quotes honestly.

Do not accept a verbal quote for a job this size. Ask for a line-by-line written quote before agreeing to anything.
