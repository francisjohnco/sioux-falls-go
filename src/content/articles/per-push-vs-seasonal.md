Choosing between per push snow removal and a seasonal snow contract is one of the first real decisions you make when hiring a snow service in Sioux Falls. Neither option is automatically better. The right one depends on your driveway, your schedule, your risk tolerance, and honestly, what kind of winter you think you're going to get. For the full picture on hiring a snow service, start with [Snow Removal in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/snow-removal-guide-sioux-falls).

## How Per Push Snow Removal Works

Per push means you pay each time the service visits your property. One storm equals one charge. Two visits in the same storm because snow kept falling equals two charges.

This model is straightforward in a light winter. If only a handful of storms hit your neighborhood hard enough to trigger a visit, you pay only for those visits.

The catch is variability. In a heavy winter, the charges stack up fast. Some homeowners are surprised by how quickly that adds up after a string of back-to-back storms in January and February.

Per push pricing typically depends on factors like:

- Driveway size and shape
- Whether sidewalks are included or priced separately
- Trigger depth (how many inches must fall before the crew comes)
- Whether return trips after a continuing storm cost the same or more

Always ask what the trigger is in writing. Some services set it at two inches, others at four. If the trigger is too high, you could be stuck with an icy driveway on a morning when you need to leave early.

## How a Seasonal Snow Contract Works

A seasonal contract charges you one flat amount for the entire winter season, regardless of how many times the crew shows up.

That predictability is the main appeal. You know what you're paying in October and nothing changes if December turns out to be relentless.

The tradeoff is that the contractor is pricing in their own risk. In a light winter, you may feel like you overpaid. In a heavy winter, the flat rate looks like a great deal. You are essentially agreeing to split that gamble with the contractor.

Seasonal contracts often come with clearer service expectations baked in, since the contractor knows exactly what they're being paid. That said, the details still vary widely. Before you sign anything, read [What a Good Snow Removal Contract Says](/guides/snow-removal-contract-checklist) so you know exactly what to look for.

## What "Included" Actually Means in Each Model

This is where homeowners get burned most often. Neither pricing model automatically includes everything you need.

Sidewalk clearing is frequently a separate line item or add-on, not a default part of the service. That matters in Sioux Falls, where City Ordinance 96.100 requires homeowners to clear sidewalks edge to edge and down to the concrete within 48 hours after snow stops falling. Corner lots also have to clear curb ramps. According to Dakota News Now as reported by KSOO in 2025, an uncleared sidewalk can bring a $100 penalty, and the City can hire a removal company at the owner's cost on top of that.

Whether you choose per push or seasonal, ask these questions before signing:

- Does the quote include the sidewalk, or just the driveway?
- Is salting or ice melt included, or priced separately?
- What happens after a second wave of snow in the same storm?
- Is there a limit on how many visits are included (for seasonal)?
- What is the trigger depth that starts the clock?

If the contractor is vague on any of these, get the answers in writing before anything is agreed to. A contractor who is confident in their service will not hesitate to spell it out.

## Light Winters Versus Heavy Winters

Sioux Falls winters vary enough that the pricing model you choose can feel like a smart bet one year and a frustrating one the next.

Per push tends to cost less in genuinely light winters. If you have a short driveway and storms stay manageable, you may come out ahead.

Seasonal contracts offer peace of mind when winters run long or storms arrive close together. For older homeowners or anyone who cannot shovel at all, that predictability and guaranteed service can be worth more than the math.

A few things to think about when deciding:

- How often in recent years have you felt like your driveway was a problem all winter, or just occasionally?
- Do you have flexibility in your morning schedule, or do you absolutely need the drive clear by a specific time?
- Is your property a simple rectangle or does it have curves, tight spots, or a long run to the street?
- Are you okay with an unpredictable bill, or does a known fixed cost help you budget?

There is no wrong answer, but you should be honest with yourself about which kind of risk bothers you more: paying more in a heavy winter, or feeling like you overpaid in a light one.

## Protecting Your Property Either Way

One concern that comes up often, regardless of pricing model, is damage. Plows can clip lawn edges, tear up landscaping at the end of a driveway, or leave snow piles that block sightlines at the street.

Before the first storm, mark your driveway edges clearly. Driveway markers help crews see where the pavement ends, especially after a heavy overnight fall. If anything does get damaged, document it with photos right away. The article [When a Plow Damages Your Lawn or Driveway](/guides/snow-removal-damage) walks through exactly how to handle that conversation with a contractor.

Also think about where snow will go. The City prohibits depositing snow into the street (City ordinance 96.103). If your piles already push to the edge of your property, talk to the contractor before the season starts about where they plan to stack it.
