A kitchen remodel is worth it. The weeks without a working kitchen are the part nobody fully prepares for. Sioux Falls homeowners who have been through it consistently say the same thing: the disruption lasted longer than they expected, and the families who coped best were the ones who planned ahead before demolition started. [Remodeling a Kitchen in Sioux Falls, the Homeowner's Guide](/guides/kitchen-remodel-guide-sioux-falls) covers the full project from planning to final walkthrough. This article focuses on the stretch in between, when your kitchen is gone and you are figuring out how to eat, stay sane, and keep the project moving.

## Set Up a Temporary Kitchen Before Demo Day

The single best thing you can do is build your temporary kitchen before the crew shows up for demolition. Once the cabinets come out, you will not have time or space to think clearly about where to put things.

Pick a room that has an outlet and room for a folding table. A garage, basement, dining room, or spare bedroom all work. You are not cooking elaborate meals, so your temporary kitchen does not need to be elaborate.

Gear worth gathering before demo:

- Electric skillet or single-burner induction cooktop
- Instant Pot or slow cooker (one-pot meals save dishes)
- Countertop toaster oven
- Mini fridge if your main fridge is moving to another room
- Paper plates, bowls, and compostable cutlery to cut down on washing
- A plastic bin with dish soap, a sponge, and a drying rack (you will wash dishes in a bathroom sink)

A five-gallon water jug with a spigot is surprisingly useful for rinsing things without walking to the bathroom every time.

## Understand the Remodel Timeline in Stages

Your [kitchen remodel timeline](/guides/kitchen-remodel-quotes) depends entirely on what your contractor is doing, but the work tends to follow a rough order. Knowing what happens when helps you predict which days are the worst and plan around them.

**Stage 1: Demolition.** Usually the first one to three days. Cabinets come out, old flooring may come up, walls open for plumbing or electrical. Very loud, very dusty. Plan to be out of the house as much as possible.

**Stage 2: Rough-in work.** Plumbing, electrical, and mechanical changes happen before walls close. This is when inspections are scheduled. Progress can look invisible from the outside. The City of Sioux Falls requires licensed contractors for electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work, and those trades need their own permits separate from any building permit, according to the City of Sioux Falls Building Permit FAQs.

**Stage 3: Drywall and paint.** Walls close, mud dries, primer and paint go on. This stage produces fine dust that settles everywhere. See the dust section below.

**Stage 4: Cabinets and countertops.** Cabinets install first. Countertops are templated after cabinets are in and then fabricated, which adds time. According to the City of Sioux Falls April 2025 home improvement reminders, cabinets and countertops do not require a permit, but the trades behind the walls do.

**Stage 5: Appliances, backsplash, and trim.** The kitchen starts to look like a kitchen again. Appliances hook up, backsplash tile goes in, finish work closes everything out.

Ask your contractor at the start of the project: which stage is likely to take the longest, and what could cause it to run over? Homeowners who get a detailed timeline that stays on target consistently say the contractor communicated what the homeowner needed to do at each stage, including things like clearing the space, being available for decisions, and signing off on changes quickly.

## Managing Dust and Protecting the Rest of Your Home

Dust from a kitchen remodel does not stay in the kitchen. Drywall dust in particular is extremely fine and travels through HVAC returns, under doors, and into rooms you thought were sealed off.

Before demo starts:

- Ask your contractor whether they will hang plastic sheeting at the kitchen entry points. Many do; not all do it thoroughly without being asked.
- Cover HVAC vents in nearby rooms with painter's tape and a piece of cardboard or a plastic bag.
- Move anything you do not want coated in dust out of adjacent rooms.
- Keep bedroom doors closed and put a rolled towel at the base during heavy dusty work.

Change your furnace filter more often than usual during the project. A clogged filter during a remodel is a real problem, not a minor inconvenience.

If you have young children or anyone with respiratory concerns at home, talk to your contractor about which days will generate the most airborne dust, and plan to be somewhere else.

## Keeping the Schedule Moving (Your Role Matters)

Delays in a kitchen remodel are not always the contractor's fault. Homeowners are often surprised to learn how much the schedule depends on their own decisions.

Things that commonly stall a project from the homeowner's side:

- Changing cabinet selections after orders are placed (see [Ordering Cabinets and Avoiding Delays](/guides/cabinet-lead-times) for why this matters)
- Slow answers to texts or calls when a contractor needs a decision to keep moving
- Not being available for inspections or final walkthroughs
- Late countertop material selections after cabinets are in

The fastest way to extend your time without a kitchen is to slow down decisions. Set a habit of responding to your contractor within a few hours during the active build weeks.

Also ask early: who is my point of contact if I have a question? A named person, not "just call the office," makes communication far less frustrating.

## Keeping Daily Life Manageable

Living without a kitchen is a logistics problem, not just a cooking problem. The families that handle it best treat it like a camping trip with a fixed end date.

A few things that actually help:

- Batch cook at the start of the week when the contractor is not working, using your temporary kitchen setup
- Identify two or three nearby spots for a quick weeknight meal so you are not making that decision when you are already tired (Sioux Falls has solid options across neighborhoods like McKennan Park and downtown if you live on the east or central side)
- Build a small meal plan for each week of the project so grocery shopping stays simple
- Keep a cooler stocked with easy snacks and breakfast items so mornings do not require cooking
- Give yourself and your household permission to spend a little more on convenience during this stretch. It is temporary.

If you have kids, keep their routines as normal as possible. The mess and noise of a remodel is disorienting for children. A temporary kitchen that is well-organized and predictable helps.

Before the project is over, do one final check with your contractor on what still needs to happen and in what order. Ask about [choosing a kitchen remodeler](/guides/choosing-kitchen-remodeler) questions you may not have thought to ask at the start, especially around the final walkthrough and any warranty on the work.
