Water damage can go from a small problem to a major repair in a matter of hours. Whether it starts with a burst pipe inside a wall, a flooded basement Sioux Falls homeowners know all too well after a heavy spring thaw, or a slow appliance leak that nobody noticed for weeks, the steps you take in the first hour matter more than almost anything else. This guide walks you through the whole process, from shutting off the water to finishing the rebuild, with links to deeper articles at each stage.

## Stop the Water First

The single most important move is cutting off the source.

If a pipe has burst, find your main water shutoff and turn it off immediately. In many Sioux Falls homes, that valve is in the basement near the front foundation wall, but locations vary. If you don't know where yours is, find it today, before anything goes wrong.

If a water heater is leaking, the appliance has its own cold-water supply valve above or beside the unit. Turn that valve first so you don't have to shut off the whole house. For full steps on protecting your floors and deciding repair versus replace, see [Water Heater Leaking? What to Do Right Now](/guides/water-heater-leak-what-to-do).

If a sump pump has failed and water is rising in your basement, the pump is the problem to solve, not the pipe. See [Sump Pump Failure, Prevention and Backup Options](/guides/sump-pump-failure-sioux-falls) for what to check and what backup systems exist.

Whatever the source, do not wait to see if it slows down on its own.

## Make It Safe Before You Walk In

Standing water and electricity are a deadly combination.

Do not enter a flooded room until you have confirmed the electricity to that area is off. Go to your breaker panel and cut power to any circuit that could feed outlets, lights, or appliances in the affected space. If the panel itself is in the flooded area or you're not certain which breakers to cut, call your utility or an electrician before entering.

If you smell gas, leave the house immediately and call your gas utility from outside.

Once electricity and gas are confirmed safe:

- Take photos and video of everything before moving a single item.
- Move valuables, documents, and electronics to a dry area.
- Pull up area rugs so they don't hold moisture against your flooring.
- Open windows if outdoor humidity is low and the temperature allows.

For a detailed safety checklist covering a basement flood specifically, see [Flooded Basement? What to Do in the First Hours](/guides/flooded-basement-first-steps).

## Call Your Insurance Company, But Know What to Say

Most homeowners know to call their insurer, but they don't know what to say or when to say it.

According to the South Dakota Division of Insurance, you should file promptly because your policy may set notice deadlines. Read your policy before you call so you know what coverage you actually have. Standard homeowner's policies typically cover sudden and accidental water damage from burst pipes but do not cover flooding from outside the home without a separate flood policy. What your specific policy covers depends entirely on its language, so ask your insurer directly.

The SD Division of Insurance recommends you:

- Make temporary repairs to stop additional damage and keep every receipt.
- Do not make permanent repairs before the insurer has documented the damage.
- Photograph everything and keep a written log of every call, email, and visit.
- Ask for an itemized explanation of any settlement offer.

Insurers are required to respond to claim communications within 30 days. If you have questions about your rights, the Division of Insurance can be reached at 605-773-3563.

For a full walkthrough of the claims process, including the difference between water backup coverage and flood coverage and what documentation to gather, see [Water Damage Insurance Claims, What Homeowners Should Know](/guides/water-damage-insurance-claim).

## What Professional Drying Actually Involves

A lot of homeowners assume that once the visible water is gone, the problem is over. It isn't.

Moisture hides inside wall cavities, under subfloors, and in insulation. In Sioux Falls, the combination of humid summers and tightly built homes means trapped moisture has nowhere to go on its own. Mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours of a water event, so the goal is to get materials dry before that window closes.

Professional drying is not just fans from a hardware store. Restoration crews use:

- Industrial extractors to pull standing water from carpet and hard surfaces.
- High-velocity air movers aimed at baseboards and wall cavities.
- Commercial dehumidifiers that remove moisture from the air continuously.
- Moisture meters to read the actual moisture content inside walls and floors, not just the surface.

Drying a typical water loss can take several days. Readings are logged at each visit so there is a documented record showing when materials reached acceptable levels. That documentation also matters for your insurance claim.

For a detailed explanation of the equipment and process, see [How Professional Drying Works After Water Damage](/guides/drying-process-explained).

## Watch for Mold, It Can Follow Quickly

After any water event, mold is the follow-on risk that catches homeowners off guard.

The furnace closet leak and the wall cavity after a burst pipe are exactly the situations where mold grows without being visible for weeks. By the time you smell something or see discoloration, the colony is already established.

Signs that mold may be present after water damage:

- A musty or earthy smell that wasn't there before the water event.
- Discoloration on drywall, wood framing, or ceiling tiles.
- Visible dark spotting along baseboards or behind cabinets.
- Allergy-like symptoms in family members that clear up when they leave the house.

Professional mold remediation in Sioux Falls typically involves containment, negative air pressure to keep spores from spreading through the house, and removal of affected materials. Restoration crews that homeowners have described in public reviews often set up visible safe zones around the work area, which is standard containment practice.

Do not attempt to clean significant mold growth yourself with bleach. Bleach kills surface mold on non-porous materials but does not reach mold inside drywall or wood, and disturbing a colony without containment spreads spores. For a full look at signs, containment, and what remediation looks like, see [Mold After Water Damage, Signs and What to Do](/guides/mold-after-water-damage).

## Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company in Sioux Falls

Water damage restoration Sioux Falls homeowners need is not the same as a general contractor or a cleaning service. The right company matters.

A restoration company should be able to respond quickly because drying timelines are short, document the drying process for your insurer, and communicate clearly about what is happening and when. Homeowners who have shared their experiences publicly describe both companies that showed up within hours and kept them informed at every stage, and situations where communication broke down and they had to start over with a different company. The second scenario costs time, money, and stress that is very hard to recover.

When you are evaluating a company, ask:

- Do you work directly with insurance adjusters, or do I have to manage that communication myself?
- How do you document drying progress and when will I see those readings?
- Who is my contact person and how often will you update me?
- Are your technicians certified through the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC)?
- What is your typical response time for a water loss in Sioux Falls?

Red flags include pressure to skip the insurance process, vague timelines, and reluctance to put the scope of work in writing.

For a full guide to evaluating restoration companies, including questions to ask and warning signs to avoid, see [How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company](/guides/choose-water-damage-restoration-company).

## Common Sources of Water Damage Sioux Falls Homeowners See

Not every water loss starts with a dramatic flood. Some of the most expensive damage in Sioux Falls homes starts quietly.

Sioux Falls sits above the Big Sioux Aquifer and the city's water is notably hard, running between roughly 12 to 15 grains per gallon. That mineral load builds up inside supply lines, water heaters, and appliance hoses over time, accelerating wear. Combined with the freeze-thaw cycles that come with a humid continental climate and around 38 inches of snow per year, pipes in exterior walls and unheated crawl spaces are under real seasonal stress.

Common sources worth knowing:

- Burst pipes in walls during cold snaps, especially in exterior walls or near garage doors.
- Sump pump failure during spring snowmelt, which can fill a basement faster than most homeowners expect.
- Appliance supply lines, particularly washing machine hoses and refrigerator ice maker lines, that fail quietly over years.
- Water heaters that corrode from the inside out without obvious external signs until the tank fails.
- Frozen pipes that thaw and crack once temperatures rise.

For the specific situation of a pipe bursting inside a wall, including how to find the main shutoff and what opening the wall involves, see [Burst Pipe in the Wall, What Happens Next](/guides/burst-pipe-water-damage).

For slow leaks behind appliances and in utility closets, see [Hidden Leaks From Appliances and Fixtures](/guides/appliance-leaks-water-damage).

## Rebuilding After the Water Is Gone

Once the structure is fully dry and any mold has been remediated, the rebuild can begin.

Depending on what had to be removed, the rebuild might include new drywall, insulation, flooring, trim, and paint. Some homeowners use the same restoration company for the full job. Others use a separate general contractor for reconstruction. Both approaches work. What matters is that work does not start before drying is confirmed complete, that you have a written scope of work, and that all permits required by the City of Sioux Falls Building Services are pulled before work begins.

Keep every receipt from temporary repairs, drying, remediation, and reconstruction. Your insurer will need documentation, and receipts are your protection if there is any dispute about what was done and when.
